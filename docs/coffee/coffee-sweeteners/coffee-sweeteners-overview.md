## Overview

Sweetening coffee is one of the most common modifications made to the beverage worldwide. Whether the purpose is to balance bitterness in a commercial-grade cup, soften acidity in a well-extracted filter, or build complexity in a café drink, sweetener choice affects both flavour and texture. Understanding the chemical composition, sweetness intensity, solubility characteristics, and flavour interactions of different sweeteners allows for more intentional application.

## Sweetener Categories

### Refined Sugar

Pure sucrose from sugarcane or sugar beet, refined to remove all molasses and colour. The most widespread global coffee sweetener. Highly neutral in flavour; excellent hot solubility; poor cold solubility.

→ See [Coffee and Sugar](coffee-and-sugar.md)

### Unrefined and Less-Refined Sugars

Sugars that retain some molasses, minerals, or natural compounds from their source. Include raw sugar, demerara, turbinado, muscovado, and coconut sugar. Add light caramel or brown sugar character to the cup.

→ See [Coconut Sugar](coconut-sugar.md) · [Raw Sugar and Demerara](raw-sugar-and-demerara.md)

### Liquid Natural Sweeteners

Syrups derived from plants, offering varying degrees of flavour complexity and superior cold solubility compared to granular sweeteners.

→ See [Simple Syrup](simple-syrup.md) · [Agave Syrup](../cafe-drinks/agave-syrup.md) · [Honey](honey.md) · [Maple Syrup](maple-syrup.md)

### Artificial and Reduced-Calorie Sweeteners

Synthetic or highly purified sweeteners including sucralose, aspartame, saccharin, steviol glycosides (stevia), and monk fruit extract. Not covered in depth in this vault's current scope.

## Key Properties for Coffee Use

| Property | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Sweetness intensity** | Determines dose; highly sweet options require less volume, reducing dilution |
| **Cold solubility** | Critical for iced coffee, cold brew, and shaken drinks |
| **Flavour neutrality** | High neutrality preserves coffee character; lower neutrality adds its own flavour |
| **Viscosity / body** | Liquid sweeteners with body (honey, syrups) can add mouthfeel |
| **Glycaemic index** | Relevant to dietary and health-conscious choices |
| **Vegan status** | Honey is not vegan; all plant-derived syrups and sugars are |

## Sweetness Intensity Reference

Sweetness is perceived relative to sucrose (white sugar = 1.0×):

| Sweetener | Relative Sweetness (approx.) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Fructose | 1.2–1.8× |
| Agave syrup (light) | ~1.3× |
| Honey | ~1.2–1.5× |
| White sugar (sucrose) | 1.0× (baseline) |
| Simple syrup 1:1 | ~0.5× (diluted; equivalent sweetness at double volume) |
| Simple syrup 2:1 (rich) | ~0.65× (diluted; equivalent sweetness at 1.5× volume) |
| Maple syrup | ~0.9–1.0× |
| Coconut sugar | ~1.0× |

Sweetness is temperature-dependent for fructose-dominant sweeteners (honey, agave) — perceived sweeter when cold.

## Flavour Neutrality Spectrum

For applications where the coffee's own flavour should dominate (single-origin filter, specialty espresso), sweeteners toward the neutral end are preferred. For drinks where added character is desirable (café lattes, cold brew, flavoured beverages), sweeteners toward the flavourful end offer more opportunity.

```
MORE NEUTRAL ←————————————————————————→ MORE FLAVOURFUL
White sugar | Simple syrup | Agave (light) | Coconut sugar | Maple syrup | Honey | Buckwheat honey
```

## Sweetener by Application

| Application | Recommended Sweeteners |
| :--- | :--- |
| Specialty filter coffee | Simple syrup, white sugar, light agave — avoid overpowering the coffee's character |
| Espresso (straight) | White sugar, simple syrup, small amount agave |
| Milk-based (latte, flat white) | Simple syrup, agave, honey syrup, maple syrup (amber) |
| Cold brew | Agave syrup, simple syrup, coconut sugar syrup, dark maple syrup |
| Iced coffee / iced latte | Any liquid sweetener; avoid granular unless pre-dissolved |
| Flavoured café drinks | Flavoured syrups (vanilla, cinnamon, etc.) based on simple syrup |

## Key Facts

- Sucrose (white sugar) has the highest flavour neutrality of common coffee sweeteners; it is the baseline against which other sweeteners are measured
- Honey and agave syrup are perceived as sweeter when cold (fructose temperature dependence) — relevant for iced applications
- Granular sweeteners (white sugar, coconut sugar) have poor cold solubility; liquid sweeteners are strongly preferred for cold brew and iced coffee
- Honey is the only common coffee sweetener that is not vegan
- Agave syrup has a low glycaemic index (15–30) despite high sweetness intensity, making it relevant for health-conscious café menus
