# Raw Sugar and Demerara

**Raw sugar** is a broad category of minimally refined cane sugars that retain a thin coating of natural molasses around each sucrose crystal. The most widely known variety used in coffee is **demerara**, though turbinado, muscovado, and several regional equivalents fall within the same family. These sugars occupy a position between fully refined white sugar and truly unrefined whole-cane sugars, adding a mild caramel or toffee character to the cup without the assertive flavour of honey or maple syrup.

---

## The Refining Spectrum

All commercial cane sugars originate from the same source — the concentrated juice of *Saccharum officinarum* (sugarcane). Their differences arise entirely from how far the refining process proceeds:

```
LEAST REFINED ←———————————————————————————→ MOST REFINED
Muscovado | Jaggery/Panela | Demerara/Turbinado | Raw sugar | Brown sugar* | White sugar
```

*Brown sugar is refined white sugar with molasses re-added; it is not the same as unrefined raw sugar.

| Category | Molasses Content | Sucrose Content | Processing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Muscovado** | 8–14% | ~86–92% | Unrefined; molasses retained naturally; not centrifuged |
| **Demerara / Turbinado** | ~1–2% | 97–99% | Single crystallisation; centrifuged to remove most molasses |
| **Raw sugar (generic)** | < 2% | 97–99% | Similar to demerara; varies by origin |
| **Brown sugar** | 3–10% | ~90–97% | Refined white sugar + re-added molasses |
| **White sugar** | 0% | ~99.9% | Fully refined; all molasses removed |

## Varieties

### Demerara
Named after the Demerara region of present-day Guyana, where it was historically produced under British colonial rule. Now produced globally, particularly in Mauritius.

- **Crystal size:** Large, coarse, golden-amber
- **Molasses content:** ~1–2%
- **Flavour:** Mild toffee-caramel, warm, slightly honey-adjacent
- **Texture:** Crunchy, free-flowing, not sticky
- Demerara is the standard raw sugar variety in UK and Australian cafés and is commonly offered as an at-table option in specialty coffee settings

### Turbinado
Named after the turbine centrifuge used to spin off excess molasses during processing. Functionally very similar to demerara.

- **Crystal size:** Medium-coarse, slightly smaller than demerara
- **Colour:** Light golden to amber
- **Molasses content:** ~1–2%
- **Flavour:** Comparable to demerara — mild caramel with minimal molasses character
- **Origin:** Common in the United States; "Sugar in the Raw" is the best-known brand
- Practically interchangeable with demerara in coffee applications

### Muscovado
A genuinely unrefined sugar in which the molasses is never separated from the crystals during processing.

- **Crystal size:** Fine to medium, moist and clumping
- **Colour:** Dark brown (dark muscovado) or mid-brown (light muscovado)
- **Molasses content:** 8–14%
- **Flavour:** Rich, deep, complex — strong molasses, dark caramel, a hint of smoke or treacle
- Significantly more assertive than demerara; can overpower coffee in typical doses
- Best suited to dark roasts, robust espresso, or coffee drinks where strong caramel character is explicitly desired

### Panela / Jaggery / Piloncillo
Traditional whole-cane sugars produced primarily in Latin America, South Asia, and Southeast Asia. The cane juice is boiled and poured into moulds without centrifugation — all molasses is retained.

- **Flavour:** Very complex — fruity, earthy, deep molasses, often with regional character
- **Coffee use:** Grated or dissolved into hot coffee; traditional sweetener in many producing regions
- Less commonly encountered in Western café contexts but culturally significant in origin countries

## Composition

All demerara and turbinado-style raw sugars are primarily sucrose (~97–99%), with the remaining 1–3% comprising:

- **Molasses solids** — the source of colour and flavour; contains residual glucose, fructose, organic acids, and trace minerals
- **Potassium** — the most notable mineral; raw sugars contain more than white sugar but less than coconut sugar
- **Calcium, iron, zinc** — trace amounts

The nutritional difference between raw sugar and white sugar is negligible at normal coffee doses. The GI is approximately equivalent (~62 vs ~65 for white sugar), since both are primarily sucrose.

---

## Flavour Profile in Coffee

The flavour contribution of demerara and turbinado is subtle but perceptible:

- **Primary note:** Mild caramel or toffee warmth, slightly richer than white sugar
- **Secondary notes:** Faint honey-like character in high-quality demerara; very mild molasses in turbinado
- **Effect on coffee:** Adds sweetness with a slight golden, warm flavour that can complement medium roasts, milk-based drinks, and coffees with natural caramel-toffee notes (e.g. Brazilian, Colombian, Guatemalan)
- At typical doses (one teaspoon), the flavour difference from white sugar is noticeable but not dominant

Muscovado, by contrast, adds a very strong molasses character that significantly changes the flavour of the cup — more appropriate as a flavour-forward ingredient than a neutral sweetener.

## Demerara Syrup

Demerara sugar is commonly made into a syrup for use in coffee and cocktails. As a liquid, it dissolves instantly in cold drinks and provides consistent, easy-to-measure doses.

**Preparation:**
- Combine 2 parts demerara sugar with 1 part hot water by weight (2:1 ratio)
- Stir over low heat until fully dissolved; do not boil
- Cool and bottle; refrigerate
- Shelf life: 3–4 weeks refrigerated

The resulting syrup is amber-coloured with a pronounced caramel flavour — richer than standard simple syrup but less intense than molasses-based syrups. It is a popular house syrup in specialty cafés.

---

## Solubility

| Form | Hot Coffee | Cold Coffee |
|---|---|---|
| Demerara (granular) | Good — dissolves with stirring | Poor — sinks; does not dissolve |
| Turbinado (granular) | Good | Poor |
| Muscovado (granular) | Good — moist texture dissolves readily | Moderate — partial dissolving |
| Demerara syrup | Excellent | Excellent |

For cold brew and iced coffee applications, demerara syrup is strongly preferred over the granular sugar.

## Use in Specialty Coffee

Demerara and turbinado have a specific cultural foothold in specialty cafés as at-table options — offered alongside or instead of white sugar sachets. Their use reflects several specialty coffee values:

- **Less-processed** — retains some natural character from the cane
- **Flavour compatibility** — the mild caramel note pairs well without dominating the cup
- **Visual identity** — the golden crystals signal intentionality and craft

Demerara syrup appears on menus at many specialty espresso bars and is often the default sweetener option for cold drinks where a flavoured or house-made syrup adds perceived value over standard simple syrup.
