# Maple Syrup

**Maple syrup** is a natural liquid sweetener produced by concentrating the sap of sugar maple trees (*Acer saccharum*) and related species. It is native to northeastern North America — primarily Canada (which produces approximately 70–80% of the world's supply) and the northeastern United States. Maple syrup is characterised by its distinctive caramel-wood-vanilla flavour profile and is one of the few natural liquid sweeteners with genuine flavour complexity that can complement rather than mask coffee's inherent character.

---

## Production

Maple syrup is produced through a seasonal tapping process in early spring, when cold nights and warm days cause sap to flow from the roots to the crown of the tree.

1. **Tapping** — small holes are drilled into mature sugar maple trunks and spouts inserted to collect flowing sap
2. **Collection** — sap is clear and watery with a sugar content of approximately 2–3% sucrose
3. **Evaporation** — sap is boiled in open evaporators or processed through reverse osmosis to remove water and concentrate the sugars
4. **Concentration** — approximately **40 litres of sap** are required to produce **1 litre of maple syrup**
5. **Grading and packaging** — syrup is graded by colour and flavour, filtered, and bottled

The temperature and microbial activity of the sap during the tapping season determine the resulting grade.

---

## Composition

| Component | Approximate Content |
|---|---|
| **Sucrose** | ~66% |
| **Water** | ~32% |
| **Glucose + Fructose** | ~2–4% |
| **Minerals** (manganese, zinc, potassium, calcium, iron) | Trace |
| **Phenolic compounds / antioxidants** | Small but meaningful amounts |

Maple syrup is predominantly sucrose — similar in fundamental composition to cane sugar — but retains naturally occurring minerals and over 65 identified phenolic antioxidant compounds, including quebecol (unique to maple syrup). Its distinctive flavour arises from Maillard reactions and caramelisation occurring during the evaporation process.

## Grades

The current international grading system (introduced in 2016) classifies all maple syrup as **Grade A** with four colour/flavour subgrades:

| Grade | Colour | Flavour Profile | Best Use in Coffee |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Golden – Delicate** | Pale gold | Very subtle, lightly sweet, mild maple | Light roasts, filter coffee — least intrusive |
| **Amber – Rich** | Amber | Classic balanced maple, caramel-forward | Most versatile; pairs with most coffee styles |
| **Dark – Robust** | Dark amber | Strong, bold maple, pronounced caramel/wood | Espresso, dark roasts, cold brew |
| **Very Dark – Strong** | Deep brown | Intense, molasses-adjacent | Primarily cooking; can overpower coffee |

**Amber Rich** is the most widely available and the standard café choice. **Golden Delicate** is preferred when minimal flavour interference is desired.

## Sweetness Profile

- Maple syrup is approximately **0.9–1.0× the sweetness of cane sugar** by volume — slightly less sweet than refined sugar, meaning recipes may require a slightly higher dose
- Its sucrose-dominant composition means sweetness perception is relatively stable across temperatures (unlike honey or agave, which are more fructose-heavy and perceived sweeter when cold)
- Glycemic Index: approximately 54 — lower than white sugar (~65) but not as low as agave (~15–30)

## Performance in Coffee

### Solubility
Maple syrup dissolves readily in hot coffee with simple stirring. In cold beverages, it is more soluble than honey but may require brief mixing or pre-dilution in iced drinks. Its thinner consistency compared to honey makes it easier to incorporate.

### Flavour Interaction
Maple syrup is the most flavourful of the common coffee sweeteners. Unlike honey, whose florality can clash with certain origins, maple's caramel-wood character tends to blend harmoniously with:
- **Medium to dark roasts** — the caramel/toffee notes align with roast-derived browning compounds
- **Cold brew** — maple syrup's robust grades are a natural pairing with cold brew's smooth, low-acid profile
- **Milk-based drinks** — integrates smoothly in lattes and flat whites, where its warmth complements steamed milk

It can overpower very delicate light roasts or high-clarity single-origin filter coffees, where simple syrup or light-grade agave may be more appropriate.

### Recommended Starting Doses

| Application | Maple Syrup (Amber Rich) |
|---|---|
| Espresso (double) | 5–10 ml |
| Filter coffee (250 ml) | 10–15 ml |
| Cold brew (200 ml serve) | 10–15 ml |
| Latte / milk-based (350 ml) | 10–20 ml |

## Nutritional Profile

| Measure | Per tbsp (~20 ml) |
|---|---|
| Calories | ~52 kcal |
| Carbohydrates | ~13 g |
| Sugars | ~12 g (primarily sucrose) |
| Glycemic Index | ~54 |
| Manganese | ~33% of RDI |
| Zinc | ~5% of RDI |

Maple syrup is one of the more nutritionally notable natural sweeteners, providing meaningful amounts of manganese and small contributions of zinc, potassium, and calcium. It also contains polyphenols with antioxidant activity. However, at typical coffee doses (10–15 ml), these contributions are modest.

Maple syrup is **vegan** and suitable for most dietary frameworks. It is not suitable for individuals with nut allergies to tree products in rare cases, though true maple allergy is uncommon.

## Storage

- Store in a sealed container in a cool, dark place; refrigerate after opening
- Unopened: 2–4 years
- Opened (refrigerated): 12 months
- May develop surface mould if left at room temperature after opening; this can be skimmed off and the syrup re-boiled if no off-flavour has developed
