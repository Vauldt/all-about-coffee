# Burundi Coffee

## Overview

Burundi produces some of East Africa's finest coffees, nearly identical in profile to neighboring ../Rwanda Coffee. Despite ongoing political and economic challenges, Burundi's coffee industry maintains exceptional quality standards, with smallholder farmers producing clean, sweet, fruit-forward coffees that rival any in the region.

## Historical Background

- Coffee introduced in 1930s by Belgian colonists
- Grown under colonial mandate system
- Post-independence (1962), coffee remained state-controlled
- Privatization began in 1990s
- Civil conflict (1993-2005) damaged infrastructure
- Recent years: Focus on specialty coffee and quality improvement

## Growing Regions

Burundi's hilly terrain creates ideal coffee-growing conditions:

### Northern Regions
- **Kayanza**: Most famous region, highest quality (1,700-2,000m)
- **Ngozi**: Clean, bright, complex fruit (1,600-2,000m)
- **Muyinga**: Emerging quality (1,400-1,800m)

### Central Regions
- **Gitega**: Balanced, sweet (1,400-1,800m)
- **Muramvya**: Good acidity, fruit notes (1,500-1,900m)

### Western Regions (Kivu Lake)
- **Cibitoke**: Lake effect moderation (1,250-1,600m)
- **Bubanza**: Lower elevation, fuller body (1,200-1,500m)

### Southern Regions
- **Bururi**: Complex, varied microclimates (1,400-2,000m)
- **Makamba**: Stone fruit, chocolate notes (1,300-1,700m)

## Varieties

**Red Bourbon** (dominant, ~95% of production):
- Excellent cup quality
- Sweet, clean, complex
- Adapted to Burundian terroir
- Same genetics as Rwanda

**Jackson** (Bourbon mutation):
- Similar quality to Bourbon
- Less common

**Mibirizi** (introduced):
- Bourbon-Typica cross
- Limited presence

See: [Bourbon Variety](../../../coffee-varieties/bourbon-variety.md), Coffee Varieties MOC

## Processing Methods

### Washed Process (Dominant)

**Standard Burundi process**:
1. Selective picking of ripe cherries
2. Floatation to remove defects
3. Pulping (mechanical depulping)
4. Fermentation in tanks (12-36 hours)
5. Washing in channels with grading
6. Soaking (6-24 hours)
7. Drying on African raised beds (10-15 days)

**Coffee Washing Stations (Stations de Lavage)**:
- Central processing facilities
- Serve hundreds of smallholders
- Quality control and consistency
- Many are cooperative-owned or state-run (privatizing)

### Natural Process (Limited)

Small amount of natural process coffee produced:
- Growing interest in specialty market
- Experimental lots at some stations
- Intense fruit-forward profiles

See: ../Washed Process, ../Natural Process

## Flavor Profile

Burundi coffee closely resembles Rwanda:

- **Acidity**: Bright, clean, juicy, citric
- **Body**: Medium, silky, tea-like
- **Sweetness**: Brown sugar, caramel, honey
- **Fruit**: Red berries, stone fruit, cherry, plum
- **Floral**: Jasmine, orange blossom
- **Other**: Sometimes chocolate, black tea
- **Finish**: Long, clean, sweet

**Compared to neighbors**:
- Nearly identical to ../Rwanda Coffee (same terroir and varieties)
- More balanced than ../Kenya Coffee (less intense)
- Cleaner than ../Tanzania Coffee
- Similar elevation and processing to Ethiopian Coffee/Ethiopia Coffee Articles/Ethiopia and Coffee highlands

### Regional Characteristics

- **Kayanza/Ngozi** (North): Brightest acidity, most complex
- **Lake Kivu regions**: Balanced, consistent
- **Southern regions**: Fuller body, more chocolate notes

## Quality & Grading

### Burundian Grading System

Screen size based:
- **AA** (Screen 17/18): Largest, premium
- **A** (Screen 15/16): Standard specialty
- **B** (Screen 13/14): Smaller, lower price
- **C and below**: Local consumption

Also graded by defect count and cup quality.

### Cup of Excellence

Burundi participates in Cup of Excellence auctions:
- Showcases exceptional lots
- International buyers access
- Premium prices for farmers
- Quality recognition

## Production Structure

### Smallholder System

- Approximately 600,000 smallholder families
- Average farm size: 0.25-0.75 hectares
- Coffee intercropped with bananas, beans, other crops
- Family labor predominates
- Most farmers organized into cooperatives

### Cooperative Organization

**Cooperatives (Sociétés Coopératives)**:
- Democratic farmer organizations
- Access to washing stations
- Technical support
- Collective bargaining
- Quality premiums distributed to members

**Washing Station Ownership**:
- Some cooperative-owned
- Some privately owned
- Some state-owned (being privatized)
- All critical to quality

### Challenges in Organization

- Political instability affects coordination
- Infrastructure limitations
- Limited access to credit
- Market access challenges

## Growing Conditions

### Climate & Geography

- **Elevation**: 1,250-2,000 meters optimal for Arabica
- **Temperature**: 18-24°C (64-75°F)
- **Rainfall**: Two rainy seasons, 1,200-1,600mm annually
- **Terrain**: "Land of a thousand hills" (similar to Rwanda)
- **Soil**: Volcanic, nutrient-rich, well-draining

### Harvest Seasons

**Main Harvest**: March-June (majority of production)
**Secondary Harvest**: October-January (smaller, variable quality)

### Ideal Conditions

- High elevation with cool nights
- Volcanic soil
- Adequate rainfall with dry processing window
- Similar to Rwanda's perfect coffee climate

See: Terroir in Coffee

## Economic & Political Context

### Coffee's Economic Importance

- Second largest export after tea
- Employs majority of rural population
- Critical for foreign exchange
- 90% of export revenues from coffee and tea

### Challenges

**Political instability**:
- Periodic conflict and unrest
- Affects infrastructure and investment
- Market access disruptions
- International concern over human rights

**Economic pressures**:
- Poverty among smallholders
- Price volatility
- Limited processing capacity
- Infrastructure needs

**Agricultural challenges**:
- Aging coffee trees (need replanting)
- Disease pressure (CBD, CLR)
- Climate variability
- Small farm sizes

### International Support

Various development programs working to:
- Improve quality and processing
- Support cooperatives
- Build infrastructure
- Enhance market access
- Promote social development

## Sourcing & Buying

### What to Look For

- **Specific washing station name**: Traceability
- **Region specified**: Kayanza, Ngozi preferred
- **Bourbon variety confirmation**
- **Recent harvest**: Within 6-12 months
- **Cup score**: 85+ for quality specialty
- **Lot information**: Day lot or specific producer group

### Price Expectations

- **Specialty grade**: $16-26 per pound (roasted)
- **Premium micro-lots**: $24-40 per pound
- **Cup of Excellence lots**: $40-100+ per pound
- **Cooperative/direct trade**: Often at premium

### Ethical Considerations

When buying Burundi coffee, consider:
- Political and human rights situation
- Fair payment to farmers
- Cooperative support
- Direct trade relationships
- Transparency in supply chain

Many specialty roasters work directly with cooperatives to ensure farmer benefit.

## Brewing Burundi Coffee

### Optimal Methods

**Pour Over** (V60, Kalita Wave, Chemex):
- Highlights clean, bright acidity
- Shows fruit complexity
- Temperature: 195-205°F (91-96°C)
- Ratio: 1:15-1:17

**Aeropress**:
- Versatile, forgiving
- Can emphasize different characteristics
- Quick brewing

**French Press**:
- Fuller body presentation
- Rich, sweet profile
- Good for daily drinking

### Roast Recommendations

- **Light roast**: Preserves brightness and fruit (ideal for specialty)
- **Light-medium**: Balanced acidity and sweetness
- **Medium**: Develops caramel, reduces acidity
- **Medium-dark**: Not recommended (masks origin character)

### Brewing Tips

- Use clean, filtered water (essential for clarity)
- Grind fresh, medium to medium-fine
- Consistent water temperature
- Bloom 30-45 seconds
- Total brew time 2:30-3:30 for pour over

## Cultural Context

- Coffee grown for export, not traditional in daily life
- Tea more common for domestic consumption
- Growing café culture in Bujumbura (capital)
- Coffee seen as economic necessity and opportunity
- Pride in quality emerging in specialty sector

## Comparing Burundi Coffee

### vs. ../Rwanda Coffee
- **Similarity**: Nearly identical in terroir, varieties, processing
- **Quality**: Comparable potential and cup profiles
- **Difference**: Rwanda has more developed specialty infrastructure
- **Note**: Often indistinguishable in blind cupping

### vs. ../Kenya Coffee
- **Acidity**: Burundi softer, more approachable
- **Intensity**: Kenya bolder, more wine-like
- **Approachability**: Burundi easier to brew and enjoy

### vs. Ethiopian Coffee/Ethiopia Coffee Articles/Ethiopia and Coffee
- **Processing**: Burundi exclusively washed vs. Ethiopia's natural tradition
- **Clarity**: Burundi cleaner vs. Ethiopia's wild complexity
- **Variety**: Burundi's Bourbon vs. Ethiopia's heirloom diversity
