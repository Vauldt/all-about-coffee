## Overview

Uganda is East Africa's largest coffee producer and the continent's largest Robusta exporter, with Coffea canephora (Robusta) native to the country's forest ecosystems around Lake Victoria and accounting for approximately 80% of national production. The remaining 20% is washed Arabica grown in the highland regions of Mount Elgon in the east, the Rwenzori Mountains in the west, and to a lesser extent the slopes of Mount Sabinyo in the southwest. Uganda's Robusta is significant not only commercially but botanically: the forests around Lake Victoria are considered one of the primary centres of wild Robusta diversity, and the Uganda National Coffee Research Institute (NaCORI) maintains gene bank collections of indigenous Robusta material with relevance for the global coffee breeding programme. Uganda's specialty Arabica from Bugisu (Mount Elgon) is internationally recognised for its clean, wine-like acidity and structural similarity to Kenyan coffee.

## Geography and Growing Regions

| Region | Coffee Type | Altitude | Character |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Bugisu (Mount Elgon, eastern Uganda) | Arabica | 1,500–2,200 m | Uganda's premier Arabica region; clean, bright, wine-like; often marketed as "Bugisu" |
| Rwenzori Mountains (western Uganda) | Arabica | 1,500–2,100 m | Complex, full-bodied; floral and fruit notes |
| Lake Victoria basin (central, south) | Robusta | 1,000–1,300 m | Commercial Robusta; earthy, full body; used in blending and instant coffee |
| West Nile region | Robusta | 1,000–1,200 m | Robusta; lower altitude; commercial grade |

**Bugisu** — the name for the Arabica-growing region on the slopes of Mount Elgon on the Uganda-Kenya border — is Uganda's specialty identity. At altitudes up to 2,200 metres, the volcanic soils and high altitude produce Arabica that frequently draws comparison to Kenyan coffee in its structural brightness and wine-like acidity. Bugisu Arabica is commonly sold under the Bugisu certification mark administered by the Uganda Coffee Development Authority (UCDA).

## Robusta's Significance

Uganda's indigenous Robusta is genuinely wild in origin — *Coffea canephora* is believed to be native to the Lake Victoria basin and surrounding forests, and Uganda's Robusta populations represent some of the greatest genetic diversity found in the species globally. This diversity has made Uganda an important source of Robusta breeding material for researchers seeking disease resistance, drought tolerance, and quality-adjacent traits in the species.

Commercially, Uganda's washed Robusta — particularly from central Uganda — is among the better-regarded Robustas available internationally, used in espresso blends for body, crema, and cost efficiency, and in instant coffee production.

## Processing

Arabica from Bugisu and Rwenzori is predominantly washed, with centralised wet mills operating similarly to the East African model. Cherry is delivered to mills, pulped, fermented, washed, and dried on raised beds or patios. Natural-processed Arabica is produced in small quantities. Robusta is often dried as whole cherries (natural process) or as stripped parchment at the farm level, with significant quality variation.

## Flavour Profile

Uganda Arabica (Bugisu):

- **Aroma:** Blackcurrant, citrus peel, dark chocolate, light florals
- **Acidity:** Bright and clean; structured wine-like acidity reminiscent of Kenya; phosphoric in top lots
- **Body:** Medium to full
- **Flavour:** Blackcurrant, plum, dark chocolate, dried citrus, brown sugar
- **Aftertaste:** Long, clean, tannic

Uganda Robusta:

- **Aroma:** Earthy, woody, nutty, mild chocolate
- **Acidity:** Very low
- **Body:** Full, heavy
- **Flavour:** Dark chocolate, earthiness, mild bitterness; clean commercial grade at its best

## Key Facts

- East Africa's largest coffee producer; continent's largest Robusta exporter
- Approximately 80% Robusta, 20% Arabica by production volume
- Robusta native to Lake Victoria basin; Uganda holds exceptional indigenous Robusta genetic diversity
- Premier Arabica region: Bugisu (Mount Elgon), 1,500–2,200 m
- Bugisu Arabica profile: bright, wine-like acidity; compared to Kenyan coffee in structure
- Uganda Coffee Development Authority (UCDA) administers Bugisu certification mark
- NaCORI (National Coffee Research Institute) maintains Robusta gene bank collections
