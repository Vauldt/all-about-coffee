# Ethiopia Coffee Grading Standards

## Overview

Ethiopian green coffee is classified using a defect-based grading system that assigns grades from 1 (highest quality, fewest defects) to 5 (lowest). A separate classification — UGQ (Unwashed Good Quality) — applies to natural-process coffees, recognising that whole-cherry drying produces visible surface characteristics counted as "defects" in visual sorting, even when cup quality is high. The grading system is administered in the context of the Ethiopia Commodity Exchange (ECX), though direct trade arrangements have expanded since 2017 reforms.

## Grade Definitions

Ethiopian washed coffee grading is based on defect count per 300 g sample:

| Grade | Defects per 300 g | Market category |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Grade 1 | 0–3 | Specialty; typically 85+ SCA points |
| Grade 2 | 4–12 | High-quality commercial to specialty |
| Grade 3 | 13–25 | Commercial specialty threshold |
| Grade 4 | 26–45 | Commercial; domestic and low-end export |
| Grade 5 | 46–100 | Commodity; domestic and instant coffee |

### UGQ (Unwashed Good Quality)

UGQ classifies natural-process (dry-process) coffees independently of the washed grading system. During natural drying, the dried cherry skin and fruit adhering to the bean creates visual characteristics that score as defects in standard physical sorting, even when the cup quality is high. An UGQ lot can score 80–88+ SCA points and command prices equivalent to Grade 1 or Grade 2 washed coffees.

## The Ethiopia Commodity Exchange (ECX)

The ECX was established in 2008 as a centralised trading platform to improve market transparency and pricing efficiency. Under the original ECX system, coffee was graded and traded by region and grade only — no washing station or farm-level information was preserved. This structure limited traceability for specialty buyers.

In 2017, the ECX system was reformed to permit cooperatives achieving 80+ SCA cup scores and licensed private washing stations to sell directly outside the ECX. The 2017 reforms enabled a surge in traceable, washing-station-specific lots reaching the specialty market — the named washing stations (Koke, Aricha, Worka, Shakiso) now appearing on specialty roaster bags are a direct result of these reforms. Commercial-grade coffee continues to trade through the ECX.

## Regional Overview

Ethiopia's principal specialty regions each have characteristic grading contexts:

| Region | Elevation | Typical grades | Primary processing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Yirgacheffe | 1,700–2,200 m | Grade 1–2 | Washed and natural |
| Sidama | 1,500–2,200 m | Grade 1–3 | Washed and natural |
| Guji | 1,700–2,300 m | Grade 1–3, UGQ | Natural predominant |
| Harrar | 1,400–2,100 m | UGQ | Exclusively natural |
| Limu | 1,400–1,900 m | Grade 2–3 | Washed |
| Jimma | 1,400–2,000 m | Grade 2–4 | Mixed |

## Washing Stations and Cooperatives

Washing stations are the quality control point for washed lots. A single washing station receives cherry from dozens or hundreds of smallholder farmers, depulps, ferments, washes, and dries it on raised beds. The station's management practices determine the lot's defect count and cup quality. Notable washing stations with established specialty reputations include Koke, Aricha, Worka, and Biloya (Yirgacheffe), Shakiso and Hambela (Guji), and Duromina (Sidama).

Cooperatives aggregate smallholder members, provide shared processing infrastructure, and sell collectively — often directly to specialty buyers under the post-2017 ECX rules. The Yirgacheffe Coffee Farmers Cooperative Union (YCFCU) and the Sidama Coffee Farmers Cooperative Union are the most widely recognised.

## Heirloom Varieties and Grade

Because the vast majority of Ethiopian coffee is grown from indigenous heirloom landraces rather than named commercial varieties, the variety designation on Ethiopian lots is typically "Heirloom" or "Ethiopian Heirloom" — a collective term reflecting genetic diversity rather than a single cultivar. Grade and washing station origin are more informative indicators of cup quality than variety designation in the Ethiopian context. See [Ethiopian Landraces Deep Dive](ethiopian-landraces-deep-dive.md).

## Key Facts

- Ethiopian grading: Grade 1 (0–3 defects/300 g) through Grade 5 (46–100 defects); Grade 1 typically scores 85+ SCA points
- UGQ (Unwashed Good Quality) classifies natural-process coffees separately; can score 80–88+ despite visual defects from drying
- ECX (established 2008) originally masked washing station traceability; 2017 reforms enabled direct trade for cooperatives and licensed private exporters
- Named washing stations (Koke, Aricha, Shakiso) are indicators of traceability and specialty quality focus
- "Heirloom" variety designation reflects multi-genotype lot composition from indigenous landraces, not a single cultivar
