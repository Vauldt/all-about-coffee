# Chipinge and Its Coffee Culture

[Chipinge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipinge) is a district in [Manicaland Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manicaland_Province) in the south-eastern corner of Zimbabwe, bordering [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique) to the east and sitting within the broader zone known as the [Eastern Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Highlands,_Zimbabwe). It is Zimbabwe's most important coffee-growing region — accounting for roughly **80% of the country's total coffee production** — and is widely regarded as one of the finest origins on the African continent.

---

## Geography and Climate

Chipinge sits at an average elevation of around **1,200 metres (4,000 ft)** above sea level, with some farm plots and surrounding peaks reaching close to 1,946 metres. The town itself was originally named *Chipinga* after a local chief, receiving town status in **1946**. The surrounding district is characterised by:

- **Deep, rich, and fertile red soils** derived from ancient granite and basalt formations
- **High annual rainfall** — January (the peak wet season) can receive over 400mm in a single month
- **Moderate temperatures** — annual average around 21°C, with cool dry winters (July lows around 8°C) tempering the growing cycle
- A **humid subtropical, dry winter climate** (Köppen classification *Cwa*), ideal for [Arabica coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffea_arabica) cultivation

The district's microclimate mimics conditions found at **around 1,600 metres** in equatorial East Africa, which explains why Chipinge coffee cups much like a fine [Kenyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Kenya) or [Ethiopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Ethiopia) origin despite Zimbabwe's more southerly latitude.

---

## History of Coffee in Chipinge

Coffee cultivation in the Chipinge district has colonial roots, introduced by [British settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesia) in the mid-to-late 19th century. The region's early agricultural identity was built around both tea and coffee — two crops that thrived in its highland environment. A devastating plant disease in the **1920s** wiped out much of the country's early stock, but the industry was relaunched commercially during the **1960s**, when Rhodesia established strict quality grading standards that made its beans competitive on world markets.

By the **late 1980s**, Zimbabwe's coffee production had peaked at approximately **16,000 metric tonnes annually** — a figure driven largely by the productivity of Chipinge's large commercial estates. Coffee had become Zimbabwe's fifth-largest export earner, with Chipinge beans fetching premium prices in London and New York.

The collapse came swiftly. [Robert Mugabe's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mugabe) land reform programme, accelerated from **2000** onward, forced thousands of commercial farmers off their land. Production fell by more than 90% within a few years. Many of the Chipinge estates were either abandoned, subdivided, or converted to subsistence farming. By the mid-2010s, only a handful of working farms remained, and fewer than **400 smallholder farmers** were still growing coffee in the entire country.

## Varietals

The dominant [coffee varietal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_coffee_varieties) grown in Chipinge is **[Catimor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catimor)**, a hybrid of [Caturra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caturra_coffee) and [Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor_coffee), developed in Portugal and introduced to Zimbabwe in the 1950s. Catimor is grown on more than **90% of farms** in the Chipinge district, chosen for its:

- **Disease resistance**, particularly against [coffee leaf rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_leaf_rust) (*Hemileia vastatrix*)
- **Short maturation period**, enabling faster turnaround between planting and first harvest
- **Compact plant size**, supporting higher yields per hectare

Other varietals present in the district — typically on older, more established estates — include **[SL28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SL28)**, **[Bourbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_coffee)**, and **[Caturra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caturra_coffee)**. Some estates, such as [Crema Coffee Garage's](https://cremacoffeegarage.com.au/blog/crema-trekkers-exploring-zimbabwe-new-years-gift-aaa) source from New Year's Gift, work with a Catimor/Caturra blend to achieve a broader flavour range.

## Processing Methods

Chipinge coffee is predominantly [wet processed (washed)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_processing#Wet_processing). The typical processing workflow involves:

1. **Sorting** — cherries floated in water to remove underripes and foreign matter
2. **Depulping** — skin and pulp removed mechanically
3. **Fermentation** — beans soaked in fermentation tanks for **18–36 hours** to dissolve mucilage
4. **Washing** — rinsed in clean water after fermentation
5. **Drying** — sun-dried for **1–3 weeks**, followed by mechanical drying
6. **Hulling** — parchment, hull, and silver skin removed before milling

The washed process produces the clean, articulate flavour profiles Chipinge is known for — allowing the terroir-driven acidity and fruit notes to express clearly in the cup.

## Flavour Profile and Grading

Chipinge coffee is graded using a system inherited from Zimbabwe's colonial-era export standards, with **AA** and **AA+** being the highest designations — indicating large bean size and strict quality control at both the farm and milling level.

Typical cup characteristics include:

- **Vibrant, winey acidity** — often compared to [Kenyan AA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Kenya) in its brightness and structure
- **Medium to full body** with a rich, lingering finish
- **Tasting notes** of red currant, dark chocolate, blackberry, and dried fruit
- **High aromatic complexity**, particularly in AAA-graded lots

Within Africa, Chipinge coffee is considered to be exceeded in quality only by [Kenyan coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Kenya), making it one of the continent's most coveted [specialty coffee](https://sca.coffee) origins.

## Notable Estates and Producers

Several estates within or near the Chipinge district have built reputations in the international specialty coffee market:

### La Lucie Estate
Founded in **1970** and operated by the renowned **Michel Fayd'herbe**, [La Lucie Estate](https://coffeeworks.com/zimbabwe-aa-la-lucie-estate/) is one of the most celebrated in the district. It produces small-lot, estate-specific coffee graded to AA and AA+ standards. La Lucie has attracted buyers from the United States and Australia, and is frequently cited as a benchmark for what Chipinge coffee can achieve at its finest.

### New Year's Gift Estate
Located approximately **25km before Chipinge town**, [New Year's Gift Estate](https://tangandatea.com/nyg-estate/) was first purchased in **1924** and is operated today under [Tanganda Tea](https://tangandatea.com). The estate has historically grown both tea and coffee, and continues to produce a Catimor/Caturra blend that appears in specialty roaster offerings globally, including in Australia through roasters like Crema Coffee Garage.

### Zimunda Estate
[Zimunda](https://www.zimunda.com/coffee) is a forward-looking producer in the Chipinge area, investing in regenerative agricultural practices including aquaponics-based irrigation and [agroforestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agroforestry) systems. It represents a new model for Zimbabwean coffee — commercially viable, environmentally conscious, and export-oriented.

## The Revival: Nespresso and TechnoServe

The most significant development in Chipinge's recent coffee history has been the **Nespresso [Reviving Origins](https://www.nespresso.com/au/en/reviving-origins) programme**, launched formally in **May 2019** in partnership with [TechnoServe](https://www.technoserve.org). The programme:

- Provided **training and technical assistance** to around 400 smallholder coffee farmers across Manicaland
- Offered farmers a **producer price of approximately US$8/kg** — nearly double prevailing local prices
- Resulted in the commercial release of **Tamuka Mu Zimbabwe** (*"Awakening of Zimbabwe"* in [Shona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shona_language)), a single-origin capsule now sold in 18 countries
- In **2022**, Zimbabwe joined Uganda in piloting Nespresso's **US$4.1 million Sustainability Innovation Fund**, focused on regenerative farming practices across the Honde Valley, Vumba, and Chipinge growing belt

The programme is primarily concentrated in [Honde Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honde_Valley) for smallholders, but its success has catalysed renewed investment and confidence across the broader Chipinge district.

## Chipinge Within Zimbabwe's Coffee Map

Zimbabwe's coffee-growing regions are concentrated in [Manicaland Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manicaland_Province), with three principal zones:

| Region | Character | Relative Importance |
|---|---|---|
| **[Chipinge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipinge)** | Deep soils, high rainfall, premier estates | ~80% of national production |
| **[Vumba Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vumba_Mountains)** | Mist-laden, aromatic, nuanced lots | Significant; smaller scale |
| **[Honde Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honde_Valley)** | Smallholder-driven, Nespresso focus | Growing rapidly post-2019 |

Chipinge's supremacy in the national coffee map reflects both its climatic advantages and the legacy of its large, well-resourced commercial estates — infrastructure that survived the land reforms better than in other regions.
