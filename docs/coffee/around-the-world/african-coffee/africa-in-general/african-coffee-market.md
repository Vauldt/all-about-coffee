## Overview

African coffee production is characterised by smallholder-dominated supply chains, cooperative and auction-based trade structures, and a growing specialty sector that has made East Africa one of the world's most important fine-coffee sources. Ethiopia is the continent's largest producer by volume; Kenya commands some of the highest specialty premiums globally through its formalised auction system.

## Auction Systems

**Kenya Nairobi Auction** — The weekly auction at the Nairobi Coffee Exchange sets benchmark prices for Kenyan grades. Factory (cooperative washing station) names attached to each lot create quality reputations over time, with consistently high-scoring factories commanding sustained premiums. See Kenyan Auction System.

**Ethiopian ECX** — The Ethiopia Commodity Exchange centralises much of Ethiopia's coffee trade, limiting traceability to regional grade rather than specific farm or washing station. Direct-trade relationships with private washing stations bypass the ECX and provide full origin transparency. See ECX vs Direct Trade Ethiopia.

## Key Facts

- Ethiopia is Africa's largest coffee producer and one of the top five globally; exports generate a significant share of the country's foreign exchange
- Kenya's Nairobi auction system creates price transparency and quality accountability through named factory lots
- The Ethiopia Commodity Exchange (ECX) was introduced in 2008 and controversially centralised much of Ethiopia's export trade, reducing traceability
- Direct-trade relationships (bypassing ECX) allow roasters to source traceable single-origin Ethiopian coffees from specific washing stations
- Rwanda and Burundi have developed export-oriented cooperative models with international support that have raised quality and producer incomes since the early 2000s
