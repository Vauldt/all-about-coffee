## Overview

Butter in the context of coffee refers to the practice of adding dairy butter — or specifically, grass-fed unsalted butter — to brewed coffee as a flavour enhancer, mouthfeel modifier, or nutritional supplement. The practice is most widely associated with "bulletproof coffee," a proprietary preparation developed and popularised by American entrepreneur Dave Asprey from approximately 2011 onward, which blends coffee with grass-fed butter and MCT (medium-chain triglyceride) oil. Butter has also been used as a coffee additive in traditional contexts, notably in Tibetan butter tea (*Po Cha*) and in some Ethiopian coffee ceremonies.

## Bulletproof Coffee

The "bulletproof" preparation method gained significant mainstream attention from approximately 2013–2018 as part of ketogenic diet and biohacking culture:

**Standard recipe:**
- 240–480 ml brewed coffee (Asprey recommends low-mycotoxin-tested beans)
- 1–2 tablespoons (14–28 g) grass-fed unsalted butter
- 1–2 tablespoons MCT oil or coconut oil
- Blended until frothy (high-speed blender required; emulsifies the fat)

**Claimed benefits (as marketed):**
- Sustained energy without blood sugar spike
- Cognitive enhancement from MCTs
- Appetite suppression (used as a breakfast meal replacement in ketogenic diets)

**Scientific status:** The health claims associated with bulletproof coffee are not well-supported by peer-reviewed evidence. The caloric content is high (approximately 250–450 kcal per serving, primarily from saturated fat). The beverage may suit low-carbohydrate dietary patterns but is not recommended as a general health practice.

## Flavour and Mouthfeel Effect

When properly emulsified (blended), butter in coffee:
- **Adds richness and body** — fat emulsification creates a thick, creamy texture similar to a latte
- **Reduces perceived bitterness** — fat coats the palate and buffers bitter compounds
- **Contributes dairy flavour** — grass-fed butter has a pronounced dairy and slight grassy note
- **Reduces acidity** — fat-coating can mute bright, acidic notes

Un-emulsified butter (simply melted on top of coffee) produces an unpleasant oily surface rather than the intended creamy emulsion.

## Traditional Uses

### Tibetan Butter Tea (*Po Cha*)

The most historically significant example of butter combined with a hot caffeinated beverage. Tibetan butter tea is made from:
- Strongly brewed tea (Pu-erh or black tea)
- Yak butter (dri butter; from female yak)
- Salt
- Churned together in a wooden churn (*chandong*)

*Po Cha* is a staple food and social beverage in Tibet and neighbouring Himalayan regions, providing calories and warmth at altitude. The preparation is culturally distinct from "bulletproof" coffee despite the superficial similarity.

### Ethiopian Coffee Ceremony

In some Ethiopian regional traditions, a small amount of butter or salt may be added to coffee as part of the ceremony, though the dominant preparation is plain black coffee.

## Use in Specialty Coffee

In the specialty coffee context, adding butter to coffee is uncommon and generally not aligned with the specialty ethos of highlighting origin character. Fat suppresses delicate aromatic compounds and mutes the acidity and fruity notes that specialty roasters and baristas seek to preserve. Butter is more appropriate as an additive in:
- Ketogenic dietary contexts where macronutrient composition is prioritised over flavour transparency
- Traditional cultural preparations (Tibetan, Ethiopian)

## Key Facts

- "Bulletproof coffee" — brewed coffee blended with grass-fed butter and MCT oil — popularised butter as a coffee additive from approximately 2013
- Blending (emulsification) is essential for a creamy texture; simply adding butter produces an oily surface
- Fat additions reduce perceived bitterness and acidity; suppress delicate aromatic notes — not compatible with specialty coffee flavour goals
- Tibetan butter tea (*Po Cha*) is the most historically significant traditional butter-in-beverage preparation; culturally distinct from modern bulletproof coffee
- Health claims associated with bulletproof coffee are not well-supported by evidence; the beverage is high in saturated fat and calories
