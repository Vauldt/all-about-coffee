# How do cafés communicate their sourcing and environmental values to customers?

← Part of [Common Questions About Coffee Culture 1](../coffee-culture/common-questions-about-coffee-culture-1.md)

Sourcing communication in specialty cafés has evolved from vague claims ("ethically sourced," "direct trade") toward specific, verifiable information: farm name, region, varietal, processing method, and increasingly the price paid at origin. This shift reflects both growing consumer sophistication and competitive differentiation — specificity signals credibility in a way that category claims do not.

Physical communication channels include menu boards and coffee descriptions (origin, tasting notes, processing), bag labels on retail coffee, and staff dialogue. The barista-as-educator model — where staff are expected to explain the provenance and character of the coffee they serve — is a defining feature of third-wave specialty culture and functions as a trust-building mechanism as well as a knowledge transfer.

Digital communication has become equally important: roaster websites, Instagram, and increasingly QR codes linking to extended origin stories function as sourcing documentation. Some roasters publish detailed transparency reports including prices paid, farm relationships, and impact metrics — a standard borrowed from the fair trade certification model but applied with greater specificity.

Environmental communication covers packaging (compostable cups, recyclable materials, reusable cup incentives), energy sources, waste management, and community initiatives. The challenge for cafés is credibility: environmental claims are easy to make and difficult to verify, and consumer scepticism of greenwashing is rising. Specific, auditable claims ("we offset 100% of our roasting energy," "our cups are certified compostable in commercial facilities") are more durable than category statements.
