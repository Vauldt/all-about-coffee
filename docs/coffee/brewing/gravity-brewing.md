## Overview

Gravity brewing (also called percolation brewing) is a category of coffee extraction in which hot water is driven through a bed of coffee grounds by gravity alone — without applied pressure or immersion. Water contacts the grounds progressively, extracts as it passes through, and drains into a vessel below. It is contrasted with [Pressure Brewing](../coffee-brewing/pressure-brewing.md) (espresso, AeroPress) and [Immersion Brewing](immersion-brewing.md) (French press, cold brew, cupping), and encompasses both manual pour-over methods and automatic drip machines.

## How Gravity Brewing Works

Water is poured or dripped onto a bed of coffee grounds held in a filter. Gravity draws the water downward through the bed, where extraction occurs continuously as fresh water contacts the grounds. The brewed coffee passes through the filter and collects below.

This single-pass extraction model means:

- Each water molecule contacts the grounds only once
- Extraction rate is controlled primarily by [flow rate](../coffee-brewing/flow-rate.md) and contact time
- Concentration cannot exceed what the single pass extracts
- The bloom phase is critical — CO₂ outgassing from fresh coffee resists water penetration and must be allowed to escape before the main pour (see Bloom and Degassing)

## Key Variables

| Variable | Effect on extraction |
| :--- | :--- |
| ../Maps of Content/Grind Size MOC | Finer = slower flow, longer contact time, higher extraction |
| [Water Temperature](../water/water-temperature.md) | Higher = faster dissolution of solubles |
| [Brew Ratio](../brewing-fundamentals/brew-ratio.md) | Determines strength; does not directly change extraction yield |
| Bed depth | Deeper bed = more resistance, slower flow, higher extraction potential |
| Filter type | Paper absorbs oils and fines; metal passes them through |
| Pour rate / agitation | Influences turbulence and evenness of extraction |

Flow rate is the governing variable in gravity brewing. Unlike immersion, where contact time is controlled by a timer, gravity brewers self-regulate based on resistance in the coffee bed. Grind size is the primary lever for adjusting this resistance.

## Flavour Profile

Gravity brewing typically produces:

- **High clarity** — clean, articulate cup with well-defined flavour notes
- **Bright acidity** — percolation preserves volatile aromatics that give perceived brightness
- **Light to medium body** — paper filters in particular strip oils and colloidal particles
- **Origin transparency** — the method is well-suited to [single origins](../coffee-green-beans/single-origin-vs-blends.md) and light roasts

Compared to [Immersion Brewing](immersion-brewing.md), gravity methods sacrifice body and texture in favour of definition and clarity.

## Gravity Brewing Methods

### Pour-Over Drippers

Manual methods where the brewer controls the pour. Each dripper's geometry, vent design, and filter type produces a distinct flow profile.

- **[V60](../coffee-equipment/v60.md)** — Conical, single large drain hole, ribbed walls allowing fast, unrestricted flow. High skill ceiling; very technique-sensitive. Produces bright, complex cups
- **[Chemex](../coffee-equipment/chemex.md)** — Conical glass brewer with proprietary thick bonded filters. Very slow flow, very high clarity, removes most oils. Best for delicate, floral or citrus-forward coffees
- **[Kalita Wave](../coffee-equipment/kalita-wave.md)** — Flat-bed dripper with three small holes. More forgiving than the V60; produces a rounder, more balanced cup
- **[Melitta-style filter coffee](../coffee-brewing/melitta-style-filter-coffee.md)** — Simple flat-bed or cone with a single restricted hole. Household staple; reliable

### Automatic Drip Machines

Machine-controlled gravity brewers. Water is heated and distributed via a showerhead onto the grounds bed. Quality varies considerably — a well-engineered machine maintaining 92–96 °C with proper bloom and even saturation is capable of excellent results. See [Automatic Drip Machine](../coffee-equipment/coffee-equipment/automatic-drip-machine.md).

### Hybrid Methods

- **[Clever Dripper](../coffee-brewing/clever-dripper.md)** — Uses gravity percolation but holds water in the brew chamber via a valve until release, adding an immersion phase; a bridge between categories
- **[Blue Bottle Dripper](../coffee-equipment/coffee-equipment/blue-bottle-dripper.md)** — Similar valve-based design

## The Bloom

In gravity brewing, the bloom (pre-infusion) is essential. Fresh coffee off-gasses CO₂, and continuous pouring without a bloom pause creates channels and uneven extraction.

Standard bloom parameters:

- Water volume: approximately 2–3× the coffee mass (e.g. 40–60 g water for 20 g coffee)
- Duration: 30–45 seconds
- Temperature: same as brew water

During the bloom the bed should swell, bubble, and settle before the main pour begins.

## Extraction Dynamics

Because water passes through the coffee bed only once, the extraction gradient across the bed matters. Water entering at the top is unsaturated and highly active; by the time it exits at the bottom it has already extracted a significant quantity of solubles and is less aggressive.

Agitation during pouring — spiral pours, centre pours, and pulse pours — produces different extraction distributions across the bed. Channelling (preferential flow paths through the bed) is a major failure mode; it bypasses sections of the bed entirely, leading to simultaneous over- and under-extraction. See [Channeling and Evenness](../brewing-fundamentals/channeling-and-evenness.md) and [Extraction Variables](../brewing-fundamentals/extraction-variables.md).

## Filter Media Comparison

| Filter type | Oils passed | Fines passed | Body | Clarity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Bonded paper (Chemex) | None | Minimal | Low | Very high |
| Standard paper (V60, Kalita) | Very little | Minimal | Low–medium | High |
| Metal mesh | Most | Some | Medium–high | Lower |
| Cloth / flannel | Some | Minimal | Medium | High |

Filter choice is a significant flavour decision, not merely a practical one.

## Gravity Brewing vs Other Methods

| | Gravity | Immersion | Pressure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Water contact | Single pass | Sustained | Single pass + force |
| Body | Low–medium | Medium–full | Full |
| Clarity | High | Lower | Low (emulsified) |
| Technique sensitivity | High | Low | Very high |
| Equipment cost | Low–medium | Low | High |
| Best for | Light roasts, single origins | Forgiving recipes, body | Concentrated espresso drinks |

## Key Facts

- Gravity brewing drives water through coffee by gravity alone — no applied pressure, no immersion; each water molecule contacts the grounds only once
- Flow rate is the governing extraction variable; grind size is the primary control for adjusting flow resistance
- Paper filters produce high-clarity, low-body cups; metal filters pass more oils and produce heavier body
- The bloom phase is essential in gravity brewing to degas CO₂ and ensure even extraction
- Methods range from manual pour-over drippers (V60, Chemex, Kalita Wave) to automatic drip machines
