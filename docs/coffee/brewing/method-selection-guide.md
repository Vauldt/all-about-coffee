# Method Selection Guide

A practical reference for choosing a brewing method based on time, desired strength, equipment budget, ease of use, and cleanup. Most brewing methods produce excellent coffee when executed well — the best choice is the one that fits your context.

## By Time Available

### Under 2 minutes
- **[Espresso](../coffee-brewing-espresso/espresso.md)** — 25–35 second extraction once the machine is up to temperature (but machine warm-up takes 20–30 minutes)
- **AeroPress (inverted fast method)** — 1–1.5 minutes total; ideal for single servings

### 2–5 minutes
- **[AeroPress](../brewing-fundamentals/aeropress.md) (standard)** — 2–3 minutes total brew time; very versatile
- **../V60 / [Kalita Wave](../coffee-equipment/kalita-wave.md)** — 3–4 minutes; requires practice for consistency
- **[Moka Pot](../coffee-equipment/moka-pot.md)** — 4–5 minutes on the hob

### 5–10 minutes
- **[French Press](french-press.md)** — 4-minute steep plus pour; easy and forgiving
- **[Clever Dripper](../coffee-brewing/clever-dripper.md)** — 4–5 minutes including immersion phase
- **[Siphon (Vacuum Pot)](../coffee-brewing/siphon-vacuum-pot.md)** — 5–8 minutes; includes setup and cleanup
- **[Chemex](../coffee-equipment/chemex.md)** — 4–6 minutes; larger volumes take longer

### 10+ minutes (active) or extended unattended time
- **Cold brew** — 12–24 hours steeping (almost entirely hands-off)
- **Cold drip** — 3–12 hours slow drip (set up and wait)

## By Desired Strength

### Concentrated (high TDS)
- **[Espresso](../coffee-brewing-espresso/espresso.md)** — 8–12% TDS; ~1:2 ratio
- **[Moka Pot](../coffee-equipment/moka-pot.md)** — ~5–8% TDS; strong, espresso-adjacent
- **Cold brew concentrate** — made at 1:5–1:8; diluted at serving
- **Ristretto** — shorter pull of espresso; very concentrated and sweet

### Medium strength (standard specialty range)
- **../V60 / [Kalita Wave](../coffee-equipment/kalita-wave.md) / [Chemex](../coffee-equipment/chemex.md)** — ~1.2–1.5% TDS at 1:15–1:17 ratio
- **Drip Coffee Makers** — ~1.2–1.5% TDS; consistent when well calibrated
- **[AeroPress](../brewing-fundamentals/aeropress.md)** — Very flexible; 1.2–10% TDS depending on recipe and dilution
- **[French Press](french-press.md)** — ~1.3–1.5% TDS; slightly higher perceived strength due to oils

### Lighter strength
- **Lungo** — extended espresso pull; lower TDS, higher volume
- **Americano / Long Black** — espresso diluted with hot water; 0.5–1% TDS
- **Diluted cold brew** — concentrate cut with water or milk to preference
- **Batch brew at longer ratio** — 1:17–1:18 for a lighter brew

## By Equipment Cost

### Under £50
- **[French Press](french-press.md)** — £10–30; durable, requires only a grinder
- **../V60 / [Kalita Wave](../coffee-equipment/kalita-wave.md)** — £15–40 for the dripper; add filters and a kettle
- **[AeroPress](../brewing-fundamentals/aeropress.md)** — £30–40; includes all accessories; travel-friendly
- **[Moka Pot](../coffee-equipment/moka-pot.md)** — £20–40; no filters needed; stovetop only
- **Manual grinder** — £20–80; essential for all methods

### £50–200
- **Quality pour over setup** — gooseneck kettle + dripper + scale + grinder (~£150 total)
- **Electric grinder (entry)** — £50–150; significantly better than manual for daily use
- **[Clever Dripper](../coffee-brewing/clever-dripper.md)** — £25–40; simpler than pour over; forgiving
- **Better French Press** — £30–80; borosilicate glass or insulated stainless

### £200–1,000
- **Entry espresso setup** — basic machine + grinder; minimum viable for quality espresso
- **Premium grinder** — £200–600; flat burr or quality conical; transformative improvement
- **[Siphon (Vacuum Pot)](../coffee-brewing/siphon-vacuum-pot.md)** — £80–200; niche but rewarding
- **Temperature-controlled kettle** — £50–150; consistency improvement for pour over

### £1,000+
- **Quality espresso machine and grinder** — the benchmark for home espresso
- **Professional batch brewer** — SCA-certified; commercial quality batch brewing
- **Complete pour over bar** — premium grinder + kettle + multiple drippers + scales

## By Ease of Use

### Easiest (minimal skill required)
- **[French Press](french-press.md)** — grind coarse, add water, wait, press; very forgiving
- **Cold brew** — grind coarse, add water, refrigerate overnight; almost impossible to get wrong
- **Drip Coffee Makers** — add coffee and water, press a button; consistency depends on machine
- **[AeroPress](../brewing-fundamentals/aeropress.md)** — short brew time limits impact of mistakes; very forgiving; great for beginners

### Moderate (requires practice to dial in)
- **../V60** / **[Kalita Wave](../coffee-equipment/kalita-wave.md)** — pouring technique and grind size both matter; rewarding once dialled
- **[Clever Dripper](../coffee-brewing/clever-dripper.md)** — more forgiving than pour over; hybrid approach reduces complexity
- **[Moka Pot](../coffee-equipment/moka-pot.md)** — heat management on the stovetop; grind size critical; easy to burn

### Advanced (significant skill ceiling)
- **Espresso** — most variables; most sensitive to errors; highest skill ceiling; most rewarding
- **[Siphon (Vacuum Pot)](../coffee-brewing/siphon-vacuum-pot.md)** — precise heat management; technique-dependent; dramatic to use
- **Competition pour over** — standard pour over taken to its technical limits

## By Cleanup

### Minimal cleanup
- **Drip Coffee Makers** (with paper liner) — remove and bin liner; rinse jug
- **../V60 / pour over** (paper filter) — remove filter with grounds; rinse dripper
- **[AeroPress](../brewing-fundamentals/aeropress.md)** — eject puck, rinse; 30 seconds total
- **Coffee bags** — discard bag; rinse cup

### Moderate cleanup
- **Espresso** — knock out puck; rinse portafilter and basket; wipe steam wand; backflush at end of day
- **[French Press](french-press.md)** — discard grounds; wash mesh filter and body; fines stick to mesh
- **[Clever Dripper](../coffee-brewing/clever-dripper.md)** — remove paper filter with grounds; rinse body

### More involved
- **[Siphon (Vacuum Pot)](../coffee-brewing/siphon-vacuum-pot.md)** — disassembly and cleaning of both chambers and filter; handle carefully
- **[Moka Pot](../coffee-equipment/moka-pot.md)** — disassemble; clean basket, filter plate, and rubber gasket; do not use soap on some models
- **Turkish coffee** — clean cezve of fine sediment stuck to sides

## Decision Tree

**Do you want a milk-based drink?** → Espresso is the only method that produces a base suitable for cappuccinos, flat whites, and lattes. (AeroPress can approximate with adaptation.)

**Do you want the cleanest, brightest cup?** → Washed pour over (V60, Chemex) with paper filter.

**Do you want the most body and texture?** → French press (unfiltered) or espresso.

**Do you have 30 seconds of hands-on time?** → AeroPress.

**Do you want to serve a group?** → Batch brew or French press (multiple presses).

**Do you want cold coffee?** → Cold brew (smooth, low-acid) or Japanese iced coffee (bright, aromatic).

**Do you want the most control over every variable?** → Espresso or pour over (both have high technique ceilings).

**Do you want something culturally rooted and theatrical?** → Siphon, Ethiopian coffee ceremony, or Turkish coffee.
