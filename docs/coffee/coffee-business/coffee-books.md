## Overview

Coffee books — a publishing category that expanded significantly with the third wave — range from popular consumer guides such as James Hoffmann's *The World Atlas of Coffee* (2014) and Anette Moldvaer's *Coffee Obsession* (2014), to professional references including Scott Rao's *The Coffee Roaster's Companion* (2014) and *The Professional Barista's Handbook* (2008), to historical works such as Mark Pendergrast's *Uncommon Grounds* (1999, revised 2010). The proliferation of quality coffee publishing from the 2000s onward reflects and reinforces the specialty movement's emphasis on knowledge, transparency, and craft, giving both professionals and consumers accessible frameworks for understanding coffee quality, origin, and preparation. Key titles have become de facto standard references in barista training programmes worldwide.
