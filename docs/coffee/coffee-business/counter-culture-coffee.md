# Counter Culture Coffee

## Overview

Counter Culture Coffee is a specialty coffee roaster founded in 1995 in Durham, North Carolina, by Brett Smith and Fred Houk. It is recognised as one of the three American roasters — alongside [Intelligentsia Coffee](intelligentsia-coffee.md) and [Stumptown Coffee](stumptown-coffee.md) — that collectively defined the principles and practices of the third-wave specialty coffee movement. Counter Culture distinguished itself through an early and rigorous commitment to sustainable sourcing, supply-chain transparency, and structured coffee education, including the publication of annual Transparency Reports disclosing the prices paid to each origin partner.

## History and Founding

Brett Smith and Fred Houk founded Counter Culture Coffee in Durham, North Carolina, in 1995. Durham, situated in North Carolina's Research Triangle, was not an obvious home for a pioneering specialty roaster, but Counter Culture's location outside the major coastal cities gave it a distinct identity — serious and technically rigorous without the brand posturing that sometimes characterised the coasts.

From its early years, Counter Culture signalled a different set of priorities from most roasters of the period. Where many specialty businesses focused primarily on retail café quality, Counter Culture built its model around wholesale supply to restaurants and cafés, pairing that supply with an unusually high level of technical training and ongoing support for wholesale accounts.

## Transparency Report

One of Counter Culture Coffee's most significant and lasting contributions to the specialty coffee industry is the annual Transparency Report. First published in the mid-2000s, the report discloses the prices Counter Culture paid to each of its origin partners in the preceding year, expressed in US dollars per pound, alongside information about the producing countries, farms, and cooperatives involved.

The Transparency Report is one of the most rigorous exercises in supply-chain transparency in the global coffee industry. By publishing actual prices — not merely stating adherence to Fair Trade or [Direct Trade](../specialty-coffee/direct-trade.md) principles — Counter Culture created an auditable record that allowed industry peers, wholesale customers, and the public to assess whether its sourcing claims were substantiated. The practice set a benchmark that few other roasters have matched.

## Sourcing Philosophy

Counter Culture's sourcing model centres on long-term relationships with producing partners in Ethiopia, Colombia, Bolivia, Kenya, and other origins. The company invests in agronomic and post-harvest support for partner farms, seeking to improve cup quality iteratively over successive harvest cycles rather than simply purchasing whatever quality is available in a given year.

This approach reflects a broader philosophy within third-wave specialty: that the roaster's role does not begin at the point of green coffee purchase but extends back through the supply chain to influence how coffee is grown, picked, and processed. Counter Culture was an early and consistent advocate for this model.

## Training Centres

Counter Culture established a network of Counter Culture Training Centres in major US cities, including New York, Atlanta, Chicago, Washington DC, Boston, Los Angeles, Portland, and Denver. These facilities offer barista training, sensory calibration, and Q Grader preparation to wholesale customers and the general public.

The Training Centre network was unusual in its geographic scope and its openness: Counter Culture offered training not only to its own accounts but to anyone in the specialty community. The centres published free brewing guides and educational materials, contributing to an open-source approach to coffee education that the company has maintained throughout its history.

## Competition Involvement

Counter Culture supported World Barista Championship (WBC) and World Brewers Cup (WBrC) competitors, providing coffee, preparation support, and technical consultation. Employees competed in national coffee championships, and the company's competition involvement reinforced its reputation for technical rigour.

## Ownership History

Counter Culture remained independently owned for more than 25 years — a notable achievement in a period characterised by rapid consolidation within the specialty coffee industry. Its independent status was often cited as evidence that the values of transparency and quality-driven sourcing were most durably expressed outside large corporate ownership structures.

In 2024 Counter Culture was acquired by Joh. A. Benckiser (JAB), the same investment group that owns Peet's Coffee, Intelligentsia, Stumptown, and several other coffee brands. The acquisition ended Counter Culture's run as one of the last independently owned first-generation third-wave roasters.

## Key Facts

- Founded 1995 in Durham, North Carolina
- Co-founders: Brett Smith and Fred Houk
- Annual Transparency Report discloses actual prices paid per origin partner
- Training Centres established in 10+ US cities
- Sourcing focused on Ethiopia, Colombia, Bolivia, Kenya, and other origins
- Wholesale-primary business model with strong technical support for accounts
- Independently owned for over 25 years before 2024 acquisition by JAB
