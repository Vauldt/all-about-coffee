# CFC (French Coffee Council)

## Overview

The French Coffee Council (CFC) is a conceptual label used in this encyclopaedia to refer collectively to the principal French organisations that perform national-level representation, coordination, and promotion functions for the coffee sector in France. France does not have a single statutory institution formally named the French Coffee Council; instead, these functions are distributed across two primary bodies: the Syndicat Français du Café, which represents the mainstream industrial and commercial segment, and the Collectif Café, which represents specialty and craft roasting interests.

## Key French Coffee Sector Bodies

### Syndicat Français du Café

The Syndicat Français du Café is the established trade body representing commercial coffee importers, roasters, and distributors in France. It covers mainstream and industrial market participants, engages in policy dialogue with French government bodies, and represents French industry interests in European coffee sector discussions.

### Collectif Café

The Collectif Café represents France's specialty and artisan roasting sector. It promotes quality standards, barista training, and specialty coffee culture, and engages with the French specialty community across the supply chain.

### International Engagement

French industry actors engage with the International Coffee Organization (ICO) on statistics, sustainability commitments, and trade policy. France also participates in international value-chain development projects through bodies such as the Common Fund for Commodities.

## Key Facts

- France lacks a single statutory body named the French Coffee Council; representation is divided between the Syndicat Français du Café (commercial sector) and the Collectif Café (specialty sector)
- The CFC label as used in this encyclopaedia is a conceptual shorthand for this collective representation ecosystem
- French coffee actors engage internationally through the ICO and development finance institutions
