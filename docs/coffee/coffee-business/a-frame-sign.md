# A-Frame Sign

## Overview

An A-frame sign — also called a sandwich board or pavement sign — is a freestanding, self-supporting display board used by cafés and coffee businesses to communicate daily specials, promotions, menus, or brand messaging to pedestrians outside the premises. The name derives from the sign's triangular profile when viewed from the side, resembling the letter A. A-frame signs are one of the most common and cost-effective forms of street-level marketing for independent cafés.

## Types of A-Frame Signs

| Type | Surface | Best Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Chalkboard A-frame | Chalkboard panel (both sides) | Daily specials; hand-written menus; artisan café aesthetic |
| Whiteboard A-frame | Whiteboard panel | Erasable marker use; indoor or sheltered outdoor |
| Snap-frame A-frame | Interchangeable printed insert | Seasonal promotions; high-volume hospitality; quick updates |
| LED A-frame | Illuminated panel | Night trading; high-traffic locations; visibility in low light |
| Acrylic / poster A-frame | Poster insert behind acrylic | Branded menus; professional printed artwork |

## Common Uses in Coffee Businesses

- **Daily specials** — single-origin filter offerings, seasonal espresso blends, or limited pastries
- **Promotions** — loyalty program incentives, opening week discounts, new product launches
- **Directional signage** — directing foot traffic in laneways, markets, or multi-tenancy premises
- **Brand messaging** — café values, sourcing statements, or taglines targeted at passing trade
- **Menu display** — abbreviated menus for quick reading by pedestrians deciding whether to enter

## Operational Considerations

- **Weather resistance** — outdoor A-frames must withstand wind and rain; chalkboard chalk may wash off in rain unless sealed with chalk markers or weatherproof chalk
- **Weight and stability** — lightweight aluminium or plastic frames may require sandbag ballast in windy locations; timber frames are heavier and more stable
- **Foot traffic placement** — signs should be placed within one to two metres of the entrance; local council regulations in many jurisdictions restrict footpath obstruction and require a minimum clearance for pedestrian passage (typically 1.5–2 metres)
- **Council permits** — many local authorities require a permit to place A-frame signs on public footpaths; fees and restrictions vary by council area
- **Legibility** — text should be readable from three to five metres; use large, high-contrast lettering; avoid over-crowding the panel

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Footpath A-frame signs are subject to local council regulations in most Australian jurisdictions. Operators must check footpath trading and signage permit requirements with their local council before placing signs on public footpaths.

> [!TIP]
> Chalkboard A-frames with hand-lettered daily specials perform strongly in specialty coffee contexts — they communicate a handcrafted, artisan identity that resonates with the specialty coffee customer. Consistent, well-executed chalk lettering is a valued barista skill in independent cafés.

## Key Facts

- A-frame signs are freestanding pavement displays used by cafés for specials, promotions, and directional signage
- Chalkboard A-frames are the most common format in independent specialty coffee businesses
- Placement on public footpaths typically requires a local council permit; clearance requirements apply
- Effective A-frame messaging is brief, large-format, and readable from three to five metres
- LED A-frames provide visibility in low-light conditions and are suited to evening-trading venues
