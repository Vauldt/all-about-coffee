## Overview

The coffee business encompasses the commercial structures, financial disciplines, and operational practices involved in running a coffee-focused hospitality venue — principally the specialty café. Coffee shops operate on a high-volume, low-margin model: revenue derives primarily from beverage sales (typically 60–80%), supplemented by food (15–35%) and retail or merchandise (5–15%). Thin net margins (5–15% in well-run operations) demand precise cost management across cost of goods sold (COGS), labour, rent, and equipment. Business literacy in the coffee sector requires understanding of pricing strategy, menu economics, supplier relationships, sustainability obligations, and staff development — each of which directly affects both the quality proposition and commercial viability of the business.

## The Café Business Model

A coffee shop's financial performance is governed by the interaction of revenue and four primary cost categories:

| Cost category | Typical % of revenue |
| :--- | :--- |
| COGS (coffee, milk, food, packaging) | 25–35% |
| Labour | 30–40% |
| Rent and rates | 10–20% (location-dependent) |
| Equipment, utilities, maintenance | 5–10% |
| Net margin (after all costs) | 5–15% |

**COGS** (cost of goods sold) represents the direct cost of products sold. For a coffee drink, COGS typically represents 25–35% of the sale price. A target contribution margin (revenue minus COGS) of 65–75% is required to cover the remaining fixed costs and achieve profitability.

**Labour** is the largest single cost category and is partly fixed (minimum staffing levels) and partly variable (additional staff at peak periods). Turnover in café labour is high; training a new barista to competent level takes three to six months, and to advanced level one to two years. Staff retention is therefore a significant cost-control lever as well as a quality investment.

## Pricing Strategy

**Cost-plus pricing** calculates the COGS per menu item and applies a target margin to arrive at a selling price. In practice, market-rate pricing — understanding the prevailing local competitive price for comparable quality and positioning relative to it — tends to dominate.

**Premium pricing** in specialty coffee requires customer understanding of the quality differential. Staff able to articulate the provenance, processing method, and roast character of the coffee being served are integral to maintaining price premium. Average transaction value (ATV) — total spend per customer visit, including food and any retail — is a more meaningful metric than margin per individual item.

## Menu Engineering

Menu engineering analyses items by two variables — contribution margin per sale and popularity (volume sold) — to guide menu design decisions:

| | High margin | Low margin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High popularity** | Stars: promote and protect | Ploughhorses: high-volume, slim profit |
| **Low popularity** | Puzzles: margin exists; needs promotion | Dogs: consider removing |

Stars should receive prominent placement and positive staff recommendation. Ploughhorses (such as a basic black filter coffee) may require pricing review or active cross-selling. Dogs occupy menu space without proportionate commercial return.

## Supplier Relationships

The relationship between a café and its coffee roaster is central to the quality proposition. Most specialty roasters provide staff training, technical support, and ongoing consultation as part of supply agreements — benefits that extend well beyond the coffee itself. Key commercial considerations include volume commitments, pricing structures, exclusivity arrangements, and contract length.

Direct trade sourcing — purchasing through an importer with direct producer relationships — enables story-led marketing and traceability, which supports premium pricing. Some operators diversify across multiple roasters for variety; others commit exclusively to a single roaster to simplify operations and deepen the relationship.

## Sustainability and Ethics

**Supply chain equity:** The commodity coffee price has historically been below the cost of sustainable production for smallholders. Specialty coffee's premium pricing, direct trade, and certification systems (Fairtrade, Rainforest Alliance, organic) address this with varying degrees of effectiveness. See [Certifications](certifications.md) for the major schemes.

**Environmental footprint:** Coffee production is water-intensive, and café operations generate significant single-use packaging waste. Sustainability commitments — reusable cups, ground composting, energy efficiency — carry both environmental and marketing value.

**Staff compensation:** The specialty coffee industry increasingly recognises living wages and transparent career pathways as both an ethical obligation and a retention investment. B Corp certification has been adopted by some specialty operators as a framework for measuring and communicating social and environmental performance.

## Key Performance Metrics

| Metric | What it measures |
| :--- | :--- |
| Revenue per labour hour | Labour investment efficiency |
| Average transaction value (ATV) | Upselling and menu design effectiveness |
| COGS % | Food and beverage cost control |
| Staff retention rate | Culture, compensation, and development quality |
| Returning customer rate | Customer satisfaction and loyalty |
| Net Promoter Score (NPS) | Customer advocacy |

## Key Facts

- Specialty café revenue is 60–80% beverages; 15–35% food; 5–15% retail/merchandise
- Target contribution margin (revenue minus COGS): 65–75%; net margin 5–15% after all costs
- Labour is the largest cost category (30–40% of revenue) and the highest-leverage area for both cost control and quality investment
- Premium pricing in specialty coffee depends on staff ability to communicate quality, provenance, and craft
- Menu engineering assesses items by margin and popularity; stars (high/high) should be protected; dogs (low/low) considered for removal
- Sustainability frameworks (Fairtrade, Rainforest Alliance, B Corp) serve both ethical obligations and marketing differentiation
