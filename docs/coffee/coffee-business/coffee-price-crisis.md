## Overview

The coffee price crisis refers specifically to the collapse of the C-Market price for Arabica in 2001–2004, when the price fell below $0.50/lb — well below the cost of production for most farmers — causing significant economic hardship, farm abandonment, and migration in major producing countries including Colombia, Mexico, and Central America. This crisis was a catalyst for the growth of fair trade certification, direct trade models, and broader industry attention to producer sustainability and supply chain equity.
