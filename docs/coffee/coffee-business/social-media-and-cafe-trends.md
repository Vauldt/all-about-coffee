# How do social media and "Instagrammable" drinks shape café trends?

← Part of [Common Questions About Coffee Culture 1](../coffee-culture/common-questions-about-coffee-culture-1.md)

Social media has restructured the café product development cycle. A drink that photographs well — vivid colour, visual drama, unusual format — travels through image-sharing platforms at a speed and reach that no advertising budget can match. The dalgona coffee (whipped instant coffee on cold milk) became a global phenomenon in 2020 driven entirely by user-generated content; the same dynamic has operated for rainbow lattes, charcoal drinks, flower lattes, and the various aesthetic formats that define each season's café trend cycle.

This creates a tension between visual appeal and product quality that specialty coffee culture has been navigating for a decade. A drink designed primarily for photography may not be a drink designed primarily for taste; the skills are different and the incentives can diverge. The "Instagrammable café" aesthetic — which prioritises visual differentiation, branded moments, and shareable interiors — can produce venues that succeed on social media while offering mediocre coffee and a transactional rather than hospitable service experience.

The counter-movement within specialty culture is deliberate resistance to visual gimmickry: the commitment to products that taste good rather than photograph well, interiors that feel comfortable rather than curated, and a rejection of the "gram-worthy" as a design criterion. This positioning is itself a cultural signal — seriousness about coffee as a sensory product rather than as a content opportunity — that appeals to a particular consumer segment. The tension between the visual and the gustatory dimensions of café culture is unlikely to resolve; they appeal to different consumer motivations.
