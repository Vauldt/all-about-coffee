# How do trends like subscription coffee or delivery apps change café culture?

← Part of [Common Questions About Coffee Culture 1](../coffee-culture/common-questions-about-coffee-culture-1.md)

Coffee subscriptions — roasters delivering freshly roasted beans on a recurring schedule — have accelerated the development of home brewing competence among a segment of the coffee-interested population. This has mixed implications for café culture. On one hand, it creates a more knowledgeable customer base that appreciates quality and is willing to pay for it. On the other hand, it reduces certain visit occasions: the person whose excellent home espresso setup is supplied by a regular subscription has less functional need to visit a café.

Delivery apps introduce a different disruption. Coffee is among the most challenging categories for delivery: espresso-based drinks degrade within minutes of preparation, and the economics of delivering a $5 item with a $3–5 delivery fee are difficult for all parties. Despite this, delivery platforms have created café revenue streams that did not previously exist — particularly for breakfast and working-from-home scenarios — at the cost of margin (platform commissions typically run 15–30%) and the complete elimination of the in-venue experience.

The deeper cultural shift is the decoupling of the coffee experience from the physical café. When the coffee comes to you — via subscription, delivery, or increasingly via pop-up and workplace hospitality services — the café loses its monopoly on quality coffee consumption. The café's response has been to differentiate on experience rather than product: the space, the service, the community function. The product alone is no longer a sufficient reason to visit.
