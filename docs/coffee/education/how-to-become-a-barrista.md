# How to Become a Barista

**Definition:**
A barista is a trained coffee professional who prepares and serves espresso-based drinks and other coffee beverages, typically in a café or hospitality setting. Becoming a barista involves a combination of foundational knowledge, hands-on technical skill, and customer service ability.

---

## 1. Understand the Role

Before entering the field, it helps to understand what the job involves:

- Preparing espresso, milk-based drinks, and filter coffee to a consistent standard
- Operating and maintaining commercial espresso machines and grinders
- Dialling in grind size, dose, and yield for changing conditions
- Serving customers and communicating menu knowledge
- Maintaining cleanliness, hygiene, and equipment care

See also: [Barista](../barista/barista.md) · Head Barista · Café Jobs – MoC

---

## 2. Build Foundational Coffee Knowledge

A working barista needs to understand:

- **Espresso basics** — extraction, pressure, grind, dose, yield, and time
- **Milk texturing** — stretching and steaming to microfoam for latte art
- **Brew variables** — grind size, water temperature, brew ratio, contact time
- **Coffee origins** — how origin, processing, and roast affect flavour
- **Menu drinks** — flat white, cappuccino, latte, long black, filter, etc.

## 3. Get Hands-On Training

### Formal Pathways (Australia)
- **SIT30622 Certificate III in Hospitality** — includes barista units, available at TAFE NSW and registered training organisations (RTOs)
- **SITHFAB025 Prepare and serve espresso coffee** — the core industry unit, often taken as a standalone short course
- **Specialty coffee workshops** — offered by roasters such as Single O, ONA Coffee, or ST. ALi (common in Sydney and Melbourne)

### Informal Pathways
- Practice at home on a semi-commercial or prosumer machine
- Volunteer at community events or pop-ups to gain reps
- Shadow an experienced barist
