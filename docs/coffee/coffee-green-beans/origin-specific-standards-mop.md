# Origin-Specific Standards MoP

Coffee-producing countries have developed unique grading systems, quality standards, and cultural practices that define their coffee. This Map of Place organises origin-specific information, helping you navigate the diverse world of coffee standards, from Ethiopian defect-based grading to Colombian size classifications, from Kenyan auction systems to Indonesian wet-hulling traditions.

## Why Origin Standards Matter

**The Reality:**
- No universal grading system exists
- "Grade 1" in Ethiopia ≠ "Grade 1" in Indonesia
- Colonial legacy influences current systems
- Geography and culture shape standards
- Understanding origins enables informed sourcing

**What You'll Learn:**
- Country-specific grading systems
- Regional quality distinctions
- Processing method variations
- Flavour profile characteristics
- Cultural and market context

## African Origins

### East Africa

**Ethiopia - Coffee Grading and Standards**
- Grade 1-5 defect-based system
- Heirloom varieties and genetic diversity
- Washed vs natural (UGQ) classifications
- ECX commodity exchange vs direct trade
- Regional distinctions (Yirgacheffe, Sidamo, Guji, Harrar)
- Washing station reputations

**Kenya - Coffee Grading and Standards**
- Screen size grading (AA, AB, PB, C, E)
- Nairobi Coffee Auction system
- SL28/SL34 legendary varieties
- Double fermentation process
- Factory (washing station) classifications
- Mbuni (natural process) grades

**Rwanda - Coffee Grading and Standards**
- A1, A2, BX grading system
- Post-genocide quality renaissance
- Cooperative washing stations
- Bourbon variety dominance
- Cup of Excellence success
- Community coffee model

**Burundi - Coffee Grading and Standards**
- Similar system to Rwanda
- Political challenges and quality potential
- Bourbon varieties
- Growing specialty presence

**Tanzania - Coffee Grading and Standards**
- Similar to Kenya (AA, AB, PB)
- Northern highlands (Kilimanjaro, Arusha)

- Peaberry valued
- Moshi auction system

## Latin American Origins

- FNC (Federación Nacional de Cafeteros) quality control
- Regional denominations (Huila, Nariño, Tolima, Antioquia)
- European Preparation (EP) standards
- Juan Valdez branding and marketing
- Smallholder cooperative strength

**Costa Rica - Coffee Grading and Standards**
- Altitude-based grading (SHB, GHB, MHB, HB)
- Micro-mill revolution
- Honey process innovation (yellow, red, black)
- Regional distinctions (Tarrazú, West Valley, Central Valley)
- Processing method legality changes (2018)
- Quality pioneer status

**Guatemala - Coffee Grading and Standards**
- Altitude grading (SHB/SHG, HB, Semi-HB)
- Eight distinct ANACAFE regions
- Antigua (protected designation)
- Huehuetenango, Atitlán, Cobán, Fraijanes
- Bourbon variety heritage
- Volcanic and non-volcanic terroir

**El Salvador - Coffee Grading and Standards**

- Quality over quantity strategy

**Honduras - Coffee Grading and Standards**

- Processing innovation
- Cup of Excellence success

**Mexico - Coffee Grading and Standards**

## Understanding Grading Systems

### Common System Types

**../../Screen Size Grading**
- Kenya, Tanzania, Colombia
- Based on bean size (64ths of inch)
- Larger often (not always) indicates quality
- Examples: AA (18), AB (15-17), Supremo (17+)

**Defect-Based Grading**
- Ethiopia, Brazil (partially)
- Count defects per sample (typically 300-350g)
- Category 1 vs Category 2 defects
- Lower defects = higher grade

**Altitude-Based Grading**
- Costa Rica, Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras
- SHB/SHG (Strictly Hard/High Grown)
- Higher altitude = denser, harder beans
- Altitude ranges vary by country

**Density Grading**
- Integrated into many systems
- Gravity tables, flotation, pneumatic
- Indicates altitude and quality
- Hard Bean designations

**Regional Denomination Systems**
- Guatemala's eight regions
- Brazil's protected origins
- Colombia's departmental focus
- Adds terroir specificity

### Cross-Referencing Standards

**Comparing Grading Systems**
- No direct equivalents between countries
- Grade 1 Ethiopia ≠ Grade 1 Indonesia
- Cup quality (80+ points) is universal language
- Understanding context essential

**Specialty Grade Across Origins**
- 80+ cup score universal threshold
- Physical requirements vary by origin
- Traceability increasingly expected
- Direct trade bypasses some systems

## Processing Variations by Origin

### Washed Process Dominance

**Central American Washed Standards**
- Costa Rica, Guatemala, El Salvador
- Clean, bright, acidic profiles
- Infrastructure requirements
- Quality consistency

**East African Washed Standards**
- Kenya double fermentation
- Rwanda/Burundi cooperative methods
- Ethiopia Grade 1-3 washed
- Complex, floral, fruity

### Natural Process Traditions

**Ethiopian Natural Standards**
- UGQ classification
- Drying bed traditions
- Fruit-forward profiles
- Grade 3-4 typical

**Brazilian Natural Standards**
- Climate-enabled tradition
- Mechanised processing
- Chocolate, nutty profiles
- Large-scale production

### Unique Regional Methods

**Indonesian Giling Basah**
- Wet-hulling process
- Climate adaptation
- Earthy, herbal character
- Grade implications

**Indian Monsooning**
- Monsoon wind exposure
- Unique aging process
- Low acidity, spicy notes
- Speciality product

**Costa Rican Honey Process**
- Yellow, red, black classifications
- Mucilage percentage variations
- Recent innovation (post-2018)
- Sweetness and complexity
