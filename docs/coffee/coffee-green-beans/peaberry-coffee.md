## Overview

A peaberry (also called "caracol" in Spanish-speaking origins) is a naturally occurring mutation in the coffee cherry where only one seed develops instead of the usual two. Rather than two flat-sided beans facing each other, the single seed grows into a small, rounded, oval shape — denser and more compact than a standard flat bean.

Peaberries occur in approximately 5–10% of all coffee cherries across every origin and variety. Because they look and behave differently from regular beans, they are typically hand-sorted and sold separately, often at a significant premium. Tanzania is the most internationally recognised origin for intentional peaberry production and marketing, but peaberries are found wherever coffee grows.

## Market & Pricing

### Why Peaberry Commands a Premium

1. **Sorting labour:** Hand-sorting is time-intensive and expensive
2. **Lower yield:** Peaberry lots are a fraction of total production (5–10%)
3. **Roasting consistency:** Practical advantage valued by specialty roasters
4. **Market perception:** Strong consumer recognition, especially for Tanzania
5. **Traceability signal:** A peaberry lot implies careful sorting at origin — a proxy for overall processing quality

### Typical Price Premiums

| Origin | Premium over equivalent flat-bean grade |
|---|---|
| Tanzania PB | 20–50% |
| Kenya PB | 15–40% |
| Kona Peaberry | 30–60%+ |
| Colombia Caracol | 15–30% (variable) |
| Other specialty origins | 10–30% (where marketed) |

**Note:** Premium reflects sorting labour and market perception more than scientifically confirmed cup superiority. Informed buyers assess cup score and lot notes rather than relying solely on peaberry designation.

### Peaberry in the Specialty Market

- Well-established premium designation in specialty trade
- Most third-wave roasters familiar with Tanzania PB as a benchmark East African lot
- Growing interest in peaberry lots from non-traditional origins (Ethiopia, Colombia, Central America)
- Some buyers purchase peaberry exclusively for espresso blends due to roasting consistency benefits
- Peaberry designation increasingly used by cooperatives and exporters as a quality signal and marketing tool

## Quick Reference

**What it is:** A naturally occurring single-seed mutation in the coffee cherry, producing a round, dense bean

**Incidence:** 5–10% of cherries across all origins and varieties

**Most famous for:** Tanzania (Kilimanjaro Peaberry), Kenya PB, Kona Peaberry

**The key advantage:** Roasting consistency (round shape tumbles evenly)

**The honest verdict on flavour:** Concentrated origin character; not inherently superior, but the roasting consistency benefit is real

**Brewing adjustment:** Grind slightly coarser than flat-bean baseline

**Premium range:** 20–50% over equivalent flat-bean grade
