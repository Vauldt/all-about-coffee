## Overview

The term "coffee beans" refers to the processed, dried seeds of the coffee cherry, the fruit of plants in the genus *Coffea*. Despite the name, coffee beans are botanical seeds — not legumes — encased in layers of fruit that must be removed through processing before the seed (the green bean) is revealed. Green coffee beans are traded globally as an agricultural commodity and roasted to produce the aromatic, soluble compounds that define brewed coffee. See [Coffee Bean Structure](coffee-bean-structure.md) for the detailed anatomy and chemical composition of the green bean, and [Coffee Beans Overview](coffee-beans-overview.md) for species and origin factors.

## Key Facts

- Coffee beans are seeds (not true beans) of the *Coffea* plant; misnomer from early European contact
- After harvest, coffee cherries are processed to remove fruit layers, yielding the green (raw) bean
- Two principal commercial species: *Coffea arabica* (Arabica) and *Coffea canephora* (Robusta)
- Green beans are roasted to develop the flavour compounds extracted in brewing
