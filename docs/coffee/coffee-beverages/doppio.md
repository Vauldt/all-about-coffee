# Doppio

**Tags:** #coffee/beverages #coffee/brewing/espresso
**Aliases:** Double espresso, Double shot

## Overview

A doppio is a double shot of espresso — two espresso shots extracted together through the same portafilter basket — yielding approximately 50–60 ml of beverage. It is the standard espresso serving size in most specialty cafés globally, having largely displaced the single shot as the default espresso base for both standalone service and milk-based drinks. The term derives from the Italian *doppio*, meaning "double".

## Preparation

A doppio is prepared by dosing a double basket — typically 16–20 g of ground coffee — and extracting through a double-spout or bottomless portafilter. Pressurised hot water is forced through the compacted coffee bed at approximately 9 bar over 25–30 seconds, producing a concentrated beverage with a layer of crema on top.

Standard doppio parameters in specialty coffee:

| Parameter | Typical range |
| :--- | :--- |
| Dose | 16–20 g (commonly 18 g) |
| Yield | 32–40 g (typically 1:2 ratio) |
| Extraction time | 25–30 seconds |
| Volume | Approximately 50–60 ml |
| Pressure | ~9 bar |

## Flavour Profile

A doppio delivers the full espresso flavour profile: concentrated, aromatic, and balanced between sweetness, acidity, and bitterness. The doppio is the preferred espresso base for milk drinks — a single shot's flavour would be overwhelmed by the volume of milk in a standard latte or cappuccino, whereas the doppio's greater coffee mass maintains the espresso character through dilution.

## Key Facts

- A doppio is a double espresso shot; standard yield is approximately 50–60 ml
- The doppio is the default espresso base in most specialty café contexts
- Standard recipe: 18 g in, 36 g out, extracted in 25–30 seconds at ~9 bar
- The term derives from the Italian *doppio*, meaning "double"
- The doppio displaced the single shot as the café standard in the specialty coffee era
