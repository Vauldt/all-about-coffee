## Overview

Coffee extraction science is the body of physical and chemical knowledge that explains how soluble compounds transfer from roasted coffee grounds into water during brewing. It encompasses dissolution thermodynamics, diffusion kinetics, compound solubility hierarchies, and the role of brewing variables (grind size, temperature, contact time, water chemistry, agitation) in controlling extraction rate and completeness. Extraction science underpins all modern brewing optimisation and professional quality control in specialty coffee.

## Key Facts

- Extraction science draws on thermodynamics (solubility and temperature relationships), mass transport (diffusion), and food chemistry (compound classes and their extraction behaviour)
- Grind size is the primary extraction variable because it controls particle surface area and diffusion path length
- The SCA extraction yield target range (18–22%) was established empirically by E.E. Lockhart at MIT in the 1950s and validated by subsequent sensory research
- Extraction yield and brew strength (TDS) are independent variables; the Brewing Control Chart maps their relationship
