# Jinotega Coffee Region

**Tags:** #coffee/geography #coffee/geography/central-america #coffee/geography/nicaragua

## Overview

Jinotega is Nicaragua's most celebrated coffee department and the source of the country's highest-scoring Cup of Excellence lots. Located in the northern highlands at 1,000–1,700 metres, the department benefits from the protective microclimate of the **Datanlí-El Diablo Biological Reserve**, a cloud forest reserve that moderates temperature, maintains humidity, and provides forest canopy that shades the highest-quality growing zones. Bourbon and Caturra are the primary specialty varieties; the best Jinotega washed Bourbon lots produce a complex, fruit-forward, floral profile that represents the peak of Nicaraguan specialty expression.

---

## Geography and Terrain

Jinotega department occupies Nicaragua's northern central highlands, bordered by Matagalpa to the south and Honduras to the north. The departmental capital, Jinotega city, sits at approximately 1,000 metres; the highest coffee farms on the slopes of the Datanlí massif reach 1,700 metres.

The **Datanlí-El Diablo Biological Reserve** is the most significant geographic feature of the specialty growing zone. This protected cloud forest reserve covers the upper slopes of the Jinotega highlands and creates the cool, misty, shaded conditions that slow cherry maturation and build the aromatic complexity associated with the department's best lots. The Tuma River, which drains the Jinotega highlands, provides water for wet mills in the lower valley zones.

The soils are Andean-derived volcanic Inceptisols and Andosols, well-drained and mineral-rich, providing excellent growing conditions for Arabica at altitude.

---

## Farming Systems

A mixture of smallholder cooperatives and medium-to-large estates characterises Jinotega. **CECOCAFEN** (Central de Cooperativas Cafetaleras del Norte) is headquartered in Jinotega and is the most internationally prominent cooperative in Nicaragua, with thousands of smallholder members, organic and fair-trade certification, a cupping laboratory, and direct-trade relationships with specialty roasters across Europe, North America, and Japan. Several internationally recognised private estates — Finca La Hammonia, Finca Idealista, and others — have become well known through Cup of Excellence placements and direct-trade channel development.

---

## Processing

**Washed processing** is the regional standard and the basis for Jinotega's most celebrated lots. Cooperative wet mills and estate beneficios manage fermentation of 24–48 hours, followed by thorough washing in clean mountain water and raised-bed or patio drying. The combination of altitude, clean water, and controlled fermentation produces the clarity and precision that defines top Jinotega lots.

**Natural processing** is practiced by a small number of farms, producing fuller, more fruit-forward lots that show good complexity at competition but represent a minor share of total production.

---

## Varieties

Bourbon — particularly red Bourbon — is the variety most associated with Jinotega's specialty reputation, delivering winey red fruit, floral, and caramel complexity at altitude. Caturra is the dominant planted variety by volume and produces reliable quality across the altitudinal range. Catuai is present in commercial-scale plantings. Pacamara is grown by a small number of specialty producers with strong competition results. Marsellesa and Catimor are increasingly planted in areas affected by the 2012–2013 *Roya* epidemic.

---

## Cup Profile

Jinotega washed Bourbon (1,400–1,700 m): winey red fruit (plum, cherry, red currant), caramel, milk chocolate, florals (jasmine, rose), bright clean citric acidity, medium-full body, long finish. The most complex and internationally recognised Nicaraguan profile; comparable to mid-tier Colombian specialty or high-altitude Honduran Montecillos lots. SCA 85–90 for quality lots; CoE finalists score 88–93.

Jinotega washed Caturra (1,200–1,600 m): peach, stone fruit, caramel, chocolate, soft citric acidity; clean and sweet; less intensity than Bourbon but reliably high quality. SCA 83–87.

---

## Key Facts

- Northern Nicaragua highlands; 1,000–1,700 m altitude
- Datanlí-El Diablo Biological Reserve: protected cloud forest microclimate for highest-altitude farms
- Nicaragua's most celebrated specialty region; most consistent Cup of Excellence department
- CECOCAFEN: flagship cooperative; organic, fair-trade, and specialty tracks
- Dominant varieties: Caturra (volume), Bourbon (specialty flagship), Pacamara, Catimor
- Profile: winey red fruit, floral, caramel; highest complexity in Nicaraguan coffee
