## Overview

Research into coffee as a health-promoting beverage has grown significantly since the 2000s, with peer-reviewed studies associating moderate coffee consumption with reduced risk of type 2 diabetes, Parkinson's disease, certain liver conditions (cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma), and all-cause mortality. The mechanisms are believed to involve coffee's antioxidant compounds (chlorogenic acids) as well as caffeine's neurological effects. While associations are well-established in epidemiological studies, causality is difficult to isolate given the complexity of coffee composition and the confounding variables in population health research.
