# Biennial Bearing

## Overview

Biennial bearing (also called alternate bearing) is the tendency of *Coffea arabica* plants to alternate between heavy-yielding "on" years and light-yielding "off" years in a repeating two-year cycle. A typical on-year may produce 100–150% of average yield; an off-year may produce only 30–60% of average. The pattern significantly impacts farm economics, harvest labour planning, and income stability, and creates supply fluctuations that ripple through the wider coffee market.

## Physiological Mechanisms

**Resource depletion:** Heavy fruit production exhausts the plant's carbohydrate reserves and nutrient stores. After a large crop, the plant lacks the energy to support another heavy flowering and fruit set. Coffee cherries are nutrient sinks requiring substantial nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and micronutrients; excessive fruiting depletes these, leaving insufficient resources for the next cycle.

**Hormonal regulation:** Developing cherries produce hormones that suppress new vegetative growth and subsequent flower bud formation. Heavy crops prevent adequate vegetative renewal, reducing flowering sites for the following year.

**Node exhaustion:** Coffee flowers form on new wood (current season's growth). Heavy cropping on existing nodes limits new vegetative shoot development, reducing future bearing sites.

## Contributing Factors

**Variety differences:** Some varieties exhibit stronger biennial tendencies than others:
- Strong tendency: Typica, Bourbon, some Ethiopian heirloom varieties
- Moderate tendency: Caturra, Catuai (less severe than Typica/Bourbon)
- Reduced tendency: Some F1 hybrids and improved selections

**Environmental stress:** Drought, nutrient deficiency, or disease stress during heavy crop years intensifies biennial bearing by further depleting plant resources.

**Management practices:** Inadequate fertilisation fails to replenish nutrients extracted by heavy crops. Insufficient pruning prevents vegetative renewal, limiting flowering sites for subsequent years.

## Economic Impact

**Income volatility:** Farmers experience dramatic income swings between on and off years, complicating financial planning, loan repayment, and household stability.

**Labour management:** Harvest labour demand fluctuates drastically — on-years require maximum labour (potentially exceeding local availability) while off-years leave seasonal workers underemployed.

**Processing capacity:** Mills and processing equipment sit underutilised during off-years while potentially being overwhelmed during on-years.

**Market effects:** Regional synchronisation of biennial bearing creates global supply fluctuations, affecting coffee prices and availability in consuming markets.

## Management Strategies

**Balanced fertilisation:** Applying adequate nutrition — especially nitrogen, potassium, and phosphorus — post-harvest allows plants to rebuild reserves before the next flowering period.

**Strategic pruning:**
- *Stumping (recepa):* Cut plants to 30–40 cm, forcing new stem growth
- *Selective pruning:* Remove exhausted branches, promoting new shoots
- *Phased renovation:* Prune portions of the farm annually, avoiding whole-farm on/off year cycles
- A staggered approach — pruning approximately 20% of the farm annually on a 5-year rotation — maintains more stable overall production while renewing individual plants

**Crop load management:** Selectively thinning flowers or young cherries during heavy flowering years prevents excessive fruiting that triggers severe off-years. This sacrifices some on-year production for more stable long-term yields.

**Variety selection:** Planting varieties with reduced biennial bearing tendency — modern F1 hybrids, certain improved selections — minimises the pattern from the start. See [Biennial Bearing Reduction](biennial-bearing-reduction.md).

**Farm diversification:** Maintaining multiple coffee blocks at different stages in the biennial cycle through staggered pruning creates more stable whole-farm production.

## Regional Variations

Biennial bearing severity varies by region:
- **Strong expression:** East Africa (Kenya, Rwanda, Burundi), Central American highlands
- **Moderate expression:** Colombia (ameliorated by multiple harvests per year due to varied geography)
- **Variable expression:** Brazil (depends on frost events, variety, and management)

## Key Facts

- Biennial bearing produces on-year yields of 100–150% of average and off-year yields of only 30–60% of average
- Caused by carbohydrate and nutrient depletion during heavy crop years, limiting vegetative recovery and the following year's bearing potential
- Typica and Bourbon varieties show strong biennial bearing; F1 hybrids tend toward more stable annual production
- Management strategies: balanced post-harvest fertilisation, staggered pruning programmes, and crop load management
- Regional synchronisation of biennial bearing contributes to global supply volatility
