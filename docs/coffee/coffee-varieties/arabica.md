## Overview

*Coffea arabica* — commonly called Arabica — is the most widely cultivated coffee species, accounting for approximately 60–70% of global coffee production and the overwhelming majority of specialty coffee. Native to the highland forests of Ethiopia and South Sudan, Arabica is valued for its broad aromatic complexity, balanced acidity, and lower caffeine content relative to *Coffea canephora* (Robusta). It is self-fertile, diploid (2n = 44 chromosomes), and comparatively sensitive to disease and temperature extremes.

## Origin and Botany

Arabica originated in the montane forests of southwestern Ethiopia, where wild populations still grow at 1,500–2,200 metres elevation. Genetic evidence and historical records suggest it was first cultivated in Yemen, from where coffee cultivation spread to the rest of the world from the 15th century onward. Unlike Robusta (tetraploid), Arabica is an allotetraploid that arose from a natural hybridisation event between *C. canephora* and *C. eugenioides*, making it genetically unusual in the coffee genus.

The plant grows as a large shrub or small tree, bearing glossy dark-green leaves, fragrant white flowers, and cherry-like fruits. Under cultivation, Arabica is typically kept at 1.5–4 metres through pruning to facilitate harvesting.

## Growing Conditions

Arabica requires specific conditions that confine commercial production to the "Bean Belt" — the tropical band roughly between 25°N and 25°S:

| Factor | Optimal range |
| :--- | :--- |
| Altitude | 600–2,200 m (higher = slower maturation, more complexity) |
| Temperature | 15–24 °C annual mean |
| Rainfall | 1,200–2,000 mm annually |
| Soil | Well-drained, slightly acidic volcanic or loamy soils |

Higher-altitude growing environments produce slower cherry maturation, allowing more time for sugar and flavour compound development. Altitude is therefore closely associated with cup quality, and many producing regions classify their highest-altitude lots under designations such as "Strictly High Grown" (SHG) or "Strictly Hard Bean" (SHB).

## Cup Profile

Arabica's flavour profile varies widely by origin, variety, processing method, and roast, but the species is broadly characterised by:

- **Acidity:** Bright to moderate; a defining sensory attribute valued in specialty contexts
- **Aroma:** High complexity — fruit, floral, chocolate, caramel, and spice notes depending on terroir and variety
- **Body:** Light to full, depending on variety and processing
- **Caffeine:** Approximately 1.2–1.5% by dry weight (roughly half that of Robusta)
- **Sweetness:** Generally higher perceived sweetness than Robusta at equivalent roast levels

## Varieties

Arabica encompasses hundreds of named varieties and cultivars. Key groups include:

- **Typica:** One of the oldest and most widespread cultivar groups; basis for many regional varieties (Blue Mountain, Kona, Sumatra Mandheling lineages)
- **Bourbon:** A natural mutation of Typica, offering high cup quality and moderate yields; origin of many modern varieties
- **Gesha / Geisha:** A genetically distinct Ethiopian variety associated with exceptional floral and stone-fruit character; prominent in competition coffees
- **Caturra, Catuai, Mundo Novo:** High-yielding Bourbon derivatives developed for commercial production
- **SL28, SL34:** Kenyan selections known for high acidity and blackcurrant character
- **F1 hybrids:** Modern disease-resistant crosses under active development by World Coffee Research

See Coffee Varieties MOC for detailed coverage of individual varieties.

## Disease and Pest Susceptibility

Arabica's relative genetic homogeneity makes it vulnerable to several significant threats:

- **Coffee leaf rust** (*Hemileia vastatrix*): the most economically damaging fungal disease, responsible for devastating crop losses in Latin America; Arabica's susceptibility drives breeding of resistant varieties
- **Coffee berry borer** (*Hypothenemus hampei*): the most destructive coffee insect pest globally
- **CBD (Coffee Berry Disease):** A fungal disease significant in African production regions

Climate change — through rising temperatures and altered rainfall patterns — is projected to reduce suitable Arabica-growing land significantly by 2050, driving interest in more climate-resilient varieties and species.

## Arabica vs. Robusta

| Property | Arabica | Robusta |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Species | *Coffea arabica* | *Coffea canephora* |
| Caffeine | ~1.2–1.5% | ~2.2–2.7% |
| Acidity | Higher | Lower |
| Flavour complexity | Higher | Lower |
| Disease resistance | Lower | Higher |
| Altitude requirement | Higher | Lower |
| Typical use | Specialty and premium blends | Commodity blends, instant coffee, espresso blends |

## Key Facts

- *Coffea arabica* accounts for approximately 60–70% of global coffee production and dominates the specialty coffee sector
- Native to Ethiopian highland forests; first cultivated in Yemen from the 15th century
- Allotetraploid species (2n = 44), derived from natural hybridisation of *C. canephora* and *C. eugenioides*
- Requires altitude (600–2,200 m), moderate temperature (15–24 °C), and well-drained soil
- Lower caffeine (~1.2–1.5%) and higher aromatic complexity than Robusta
- Susceptible to coffee leaf rust; climate change threatens long-term suitable growing areas
