## Overview

Catuaí (also spelled Catuai without the accent) is an artificial hybrid of *Coffea arabica* varieties Caturra and Mundo Novo, developed by the Instituto Agronômico de Campinas (IAC) in Brazil and released commercially in 1972. The correct orthography in Portuguese is *Catuaí* (with the accent mark), though both forms are used in the international specialty coffee trade. Catuaí is one of the most widely planted Arabica varieties globally — dominant in Brazilian commodity production and common in Central American specialty farming.

> [!NOTE]
> This note uses the accented spelling *Catuaí*. The unaccented form *Catuai* redirects here. For full botanical and agronomic detail, see Catuai.

| Plant height | 1.5–2 m (dwarf; from Caturra) |
| Fruit colour | Red (*Catuaí Vermelho*) or yellow (*Catuaí Amarelo*) |
| Yield | Very high |
| Disease resistance | Susceptible to coffee leaf rust (*Hemileia vastatrix*) |

## Red and Yellow Catuaí

Catuaí exists in two distinct fruit-colour forms:

- **Catuaí Vermelho (Red Catuaí):** Red-fruited; the more common variety; standard appearance at harvest
- **Catuaí Amarelo (Yellow Catuaí):** Yellow-fruited; visually distinctive at harvest; slightly higher perceived sweetness in some comparisons; more visually striking as yellow cherries are unusual

Both forms share identical growth habit, yield characteristics, and disease susceptibility. Cup quality differences between red and yellow Catuaí, when grown and processed identically, are subtle and debated.

## Cultivation Context

Catuaí's compact dwarf growth habit allows dense planting (up to 5,000 trees per hectare vs. ~2,000 for full-size varieties) and mechanical strip harvesting, making it ideal for large-scale Brazilian flatland production. In Central American specialty farming (Costa Rica, Guatemala, Honduras), Catuaí grown at altitudes above 1,400 m produces more complex, specialty-grade cups.

## Cup Quality

- At low altitude (< 1,000 m): moderate quality; clean, mild, chocolatey, low acid; commodity grade
- At high altitude (> 1,400 m): more complex; bright citric acidity; caramel and mild fruit notes; competitive specialty scores
- Less distinctive than Bourbon, Typica, or SL28 at comparable altitude

## Key Facts

- *Catuaí* is the correct accented spelling in Portuguese; *Catuai* (no accent) is also widely used in English
- Caturra × Mundo Novo hybrid; released 1972; one of the most widely planted Arabica varieties globally
- Red (Vermelho) and yellow (Amarelo) fruited forms; both share the same agronomic characteristics
- High yield; compact growth; mechanical harvest-suitable; susceptible to coffee leaf rust
- Cup quality strongly altitude-dependent — specialty competitive above 1,400 m; commodity grade at low elevation
