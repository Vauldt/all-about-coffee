## Overview

JARC varieties refer to the improved *Coffea arabica* selections developed and released by the Jimma Agricultural Research Centre (JARC) in Ethiopia — the primary coffee research institution in the country of coffee's origin. JARC is a centre of the Ethiopian Institute of Agricultural Research (EIAR) located in Jimma, Oromia, and has been selecting improved coffee varieties from Ethiopia's vast indigenous coffee genetic diversity since the 1970s. JARC varieties combine the genetic diversity and cup quality potential of Ethiopian landrace and wild coffee germplasm with selected agronomic traits such as improved yield, disease resistance, and adaptability to smallholder farming conditions.

## About JARC

The Jimma Agricultural Research Centre was established in 1967 (originally as the Coffee Research Station, Jimma) and is the world's oldest and most significant coffee research institution in terms of access to Arabica genetic diversity. Ethiopia's wild and semi-wild coffee forests contain the greatest known genetic diversity of *C. arabica* in the world — wild populations in Kaffa, Bale, and Harenna forests, and the extensive traditional garden coffee systems of western Ethiopia, represent the primary global gene pool from which all cultivated Arabica ultimately derives.

JARC maintains the world's largest *C. arabica* germplasm collection: over 6,000 accessions collected from Ethiopian wild and cultivated populations. This collection underpins both JARC's own breeding programme and international gene bank activities (including material shared with World Coffee Research and CGIAR).

## JARC Breeding Methodology

JARC's breeding approach differs from many national coffee programmes:

- **Mass selection from indigenous material:** Rather than introducing exotic varieties (such as Bourbon, Typica, or Catimor) as parents, JARC primarily selects superior individuals from within Ethiopia's own indigenous diversity — wild forest collections, semi-forest, garden, and plantation ecotypes
- **Multi-location trials:** Candidate selections are tested across multiple Ethiopian growing environments (altitude, rainfall, soil) before release
- **Smallholder focus:** Released varieties are evaluated for performance under smallholder conditions — without expensive inputs, irrigation, or intensive management

## Key Released Varieties

JARC has released over 40 improved varieties for different Ethiopian regions. Representative examples include:

| Variety | Key traits | Region |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 741 (Jimma 741) | High yield, moderate leaf rust resistance | Western Ethiopia |
| 74110 | Good cup quality, disease resistance | Various |
| 74112 | High yield, suitable for high altitude | Various |
| 744 | Wild forest-derived; high cup quality potential | Sidama/Yirgacheffe region |
| 7454 | Wollega-origin; adapted to western conditions | Western Ethiopia |
| Ababuna | Multi-disease resistant, high yield | Various |

These designations reflect the station prefix (74 = Jimma 1974 selection cohort) and individual plant number. Named varieties such as Ababuna and Kurume represent more recent releases with formal variety names.

## Cup Quality

JARC varieties grown in appropriate high-altitude environments and properly processed are capable of specialty-level cup quality. Ethiopia's JARC-derived improved varieties are used in certified specialty lots sold at the Ethiopia Commodity Exchange (ECX) and directly by cooperatives. The genetic diversity within the JARC collection includes high-cup-quality accessions that, when selected and managed well, produce the floral, citrus, and stone-fruit characters that define premium Ethiopian specialty coffees.

## Conservation Significance

The JARC germplasm collection has global significance beyond Ethiopian commercial production. As climate change threatens the wild coffee forests of Ethiopia and as Arabica's narrow genetic base elsewhere becomes increasingly vulnerable to new diseases and climate stress, the JARC collection represents an irreplaceable resource for global coffee breeding. World Coffee Research and CABI have partnered with JARC on germplasm documentation, duplication, and distribution to international gene banks.

## Key Facts

- JARC (Jimma Agricultural Research Centre) is Ethiopia's primary coffee research institution, established 1967; maintains over 6,000 *C. arabica* accessions — the world's largest Arabica germplasm collection
- JARC releases improved varieties selected from Ethiopia's indigenous genetic diversity rather than from introduced exotic varieties; over 40 varieties released for different regions and conditions
- JARC varieties combine yield, disease resistance, and adaptability improvements while drawing on the genetic diversity responsible for Ethiopia's distinctive cup profiles
- The JARC germplasm collection has global significance for Arabica breeding and conservation as climate change and disease pressure threaten the world's narrow cultivated Arabica genetic base
- Variety names often encode selection provenance (e.g., 74 = 1974 Jimma selection cohort); newer releases carry common names (Ababuna, Kurume)
