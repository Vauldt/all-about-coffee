## Overview

The term brewer has two distinct meanings in coffee: it refers to a person who specialises in preparing coffee through brewing methods, and to the equipment used for that preparation. In the specialty coffee context, both senses carry specific professional and technical meaning.

## Brewer as a Professional Role

A coffee brewer is a café or roastery professional specialising in manual and batch filter coffee preparation, distinct from espresso-focused barista work. The role is associated with pour-over brew bars and filter-focused specialty cafés.

Core competencies typically include:

- Pour-over methods (V60, Kalita Wave, Chemex, and similar)
- Batch brewing operations and recipe management
- Immersion methods (French press, siphon, AeroPress)
- Sensory evaluation and cupping
- Recipe development for individual coffees
- Equipment calibration and maintenance

Brewer roles exist in specialty cafés with dedicated filter menus, roastery tasting rooms, and competition training environments. The World Brewers Cup is the primary international competition for brewers.

## Brewer as Equipment

Coffee brewing equipment is broadly categorised by brewing method.

### Manual Brewers

**Pour-over:** Conical and flat-bottom drippers designed for gravity percolation — V60, Kalita Wave, Chemex, Melitta, and similar. Each produces characteristic cup qualities determined by filter type, dripper geometry, and flow rate.

**Immersion:** French press, AeroPress, Clever Dripper, and siphon brewers. Coffee steeps in water for a set time before separation.

**Combination:** Some devices combine immersion and percolation phases, such as the Clever Dripper (immersion followed by gravity drainage).

### Automatic Brewers

**Home drip machines:** Range from basic models to premium SCA-certified brewers (such as the Technivorm Moccamaster and Bonavita), which maintain water temperature at 92–96°C and deliver controlled contact times meeting Gold Cup standards.

**Commercial batch brewers:** High-volume equipment (Fetco, Curtis, Bunn) for café and restaurant use, designed for consistent high-output extraction.

**Automated pour-over machines:** Devices such as the Fellow Aiden and Ratio Eight simulate pour-over technique with programmable water delivery patterns.

### Speciality Brewing Equipment

- **Cold brew systems:** Toddy and similar immersion cold brew devices; Kyoto-style slow-drip towers for drip cold brew
- **Siphon/vacuum brewers:** Hario and Yama glass siphon brewers; use vapour pressure and vacuum to cycle water through the coffee bed
- **Traditional devices:** Turkish cezve/ibrik, Vietnamese phin filter, South Indian filter, Moka pot

## SCA Certification

The Specialty Coffee Association certifies home and commercial brewers that meet defined brewing standards: water temperature of 92–96°C, brew time of four to eight minutes, and consistent extraction uniformity. SCA-certified brewers carry the Home Brewer Certified or Commercial Brewer Certified designation.

## Key Facts

- "Brewer" refers to both a coffee professional and brewing equipment
- The World Brewers Cup is the primary international competition for manual brewing
- SCA-certified home and commercial brewers must maintain 92–96°C brew temperature
- Manual pour-over brewing gives the brewer direct control over water delivery; automatic brewers automate this within fixed parameters
- Core brewing equipment categories: pour-over drippers, immersion brewers, automatic drip machines, cold brew systems
