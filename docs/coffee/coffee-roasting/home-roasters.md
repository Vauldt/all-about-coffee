## Overview

Home coffee roasting refers to the practice of roasting green coffee beans at home using small-scale, consumer-grade equipment or improvised methods. The practice appeals to coffee enthusiasts who want maximum freshness, access to a wider range of green coffees than the retail roasted market provides, or the satisfaction of controlling the roasting process end-to-end. Home roasting equipment ranges from improvised household appliances (popcorn poppers, stovetop pans) to dedicated small-batch roasters designed specifically for the domestic market. While home roasters cannot replicate the consistency or precision of commercial drum roasters, they are capable of producing excellent coffee when used with care and understanding of the roasting process.

## Home Roasting Equipment Types

### Popcorn Poppers (Air Poppers)

The most basic home roasting platform. Hot-air popcorn poppers (fluid-bed style) were popularised as home coffee roasters in the 1990s due to their low cost and availability:
- **Capacity:** 50–100 g per batch
- **Heat source:** Electric heating element; all-convection heat transfer
- **Control:** Minimal — power on/off only; no temperature or airflow adjustment
- **Smoke:** Significant during development; requires outdoor use or strong kitchen ventilation
- **Results:** Can produce good to excellent light-medium roasts; limited ability to target darker levels due to power constraints; very short roast times (4–7 minutes) can underdevelop if not careful
- **Cost:** Low (AUD 20–60 for a basic unit)

### Dedicated Home Drum Roasters

Purpose-built small drum roasters for domestic use. Common models include:

| Model | Capacity | Features | Price (approx.) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Behmor 1600+ | 200–450 g | Variable drum speed, manual profile control, smoke suppression | AUD 400–600 |
| Gene Café CBR-101 | 250 g | Rotating off-axis drum, temperature display, manual timer | AUD 700–900 |
| Hottop KN-8828 | 250 g | Full manual or programmable control, profile logging option | AUD 1,200–1,800 |

Dedicated home drum roasters provide more control and consistency than popcorn poppers, with adjustable roast time, some temperature feedback, and integrated cooling.

### Fluid Bed Home Roasters

Small-scale dedicated fluid bed (hot air) roasters designed for home use:

| Model | Capacity | Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nuvo Eco Ceramic | 50 g | Manual stovetop ceramic drum; no electronics |
| Fresh Roast SR800 | 120 g | Electric air roaster; digital temperature and fan speed control; USB data logging |
| Ikawa Pro (home edition) | 50–60 g | App-controlled; precise profile management; designed as a stepping stone to commercial Ikawa |

The Fresh Roast SR800 and Ikawa represent the high end of consumer fluid bed home roasters, offering meaningful profile control.

### Improvised Methods

- **Stovetop skillet or wok:** Possible but difficult; requires constant agitation; very uneven development; significant smoke; not recommended for quality-focused roasting
- **Oven roasting:** Even more problematic — no agitation, very long drying phase, smoke accumulates; results are typically flat and poorly developed
- **BBQ/grill:** Possible with a suitable rotating drum attachment; requires care; outdoor method reduces smoke issue

## Key Considerations for Home Roasting

### Smoke Management

Coffee roasting produces significant smoke, particularly from City+ through darker roast levels. Home roasting indoors without adequate ventilation causes smoke accumulation and potential smoke alarm triggering. Options:
- Roast outdoors or in a well-ventilated garage
- Use a dedicated inline fan and duct the roaster exhaust outside
- Popcorn poppers and small fluid bed roasters produce less smoke than drum roasters at equivalent roast levels

### Batch Size and Freshness

Home roasters work best roasting small, frequent batches (50–250 g) for freshness. Roasting more than needed means accepting staleness in later portions of a larger batch — the opposite of the primary motivation for home roasting.

### Green Coffee Sourcing

Green coffee for home roasters is available from:
- Specialty green coffee importers and brokers who offer retail quantities (250 g–5 kg)
- Online retailers specialising in home roasting supply
- Some specialty roasters who sell their green coffee to home roasters

Freshness of the green coffee is important; home roasters benefit from purchasing relatively current-crop green coffee.

### Profile Management

Home roasters with limited temperature control can still benefit from:
- Consistent charge weights
- Listening carefully to first crack timing and intensity
- Noting total roast time and adjusting for subsequent batches
- Simple paper or spreadsheet logging of each batch (weight in/out, first crack time, drop time, notes)

## Key Facts

- Home roasting equipment ranges from popcorn poppers (AUD 20–60) to dedicated drum roasters (AUD 400–1,800)
- Typical batch sizes: 50–450 g depending on equipment type
- Smoke is the primary practical challenge for indoor home roasting; outdoor or ventilated roasting is strongly preferred
- Simple logging (time, first crack, drop time, visual colour) significantly improves batch-to-batch consistency even with basic equipment
- Green coffee is available in retail quantities from specialty importers and online retailers
