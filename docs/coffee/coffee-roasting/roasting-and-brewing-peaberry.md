# Roasting and Brewing Peaberry

*Practical guidance for getting the best out of peaberry lots — from drum adjustments to brew method selection*

## Roasting Peaberry

### The Roasting Advantage

The most technically grounded argument for peaberry quality lies in roasting:

- **Even tumbling:** Round beans tumble more uniformly in the drum, reducing uneven heat exposure
- **Consistent surface contact:** No flat face creates more predictable contact with drum surface
- **Heat application:** More predictable roast curve development
- **Result:** Potentially fewer underdeveloped or overdeveloped spots compared to mixed flat-bean lots of uneven size

This is the primary reason many specialty roasters pay a premium for peaberry lots — the roasting consistency benefit is practical and repeatable, regardless of any cup quality debate.

### Roasting Adjustments

Peaberry lots require calibration before going into standard profiles:

- **Charge temperature:** May need slight reduction — denser beans absorb heat differently from flat beans
- **Development time:** Often benefits from slightly extended development phase
- **Drop temperature:** Adjust based on the specific lot and origin
- **Batch size:** Smaller batches recommended for precision on peaberry lots
- **Grind calibration post-roast:** After roasting, peaberry lots may behave differently at the grinder — recalibrate before dialling in

### Roast Level Recommendations

- **Light roast:** Best for showcasing origin character, brightness, and fruit complexity
- **Light-medium:** Develops sweetness while preserving brightness; a good all-round choice
- **Medium:** Caramel notes emerge; acidity softens; accessible entry point for a wider audience
- **Medium-dark:** Acceptable but begins masking origin character; defeats much of the sorting effort
- **Dark roast:** Not recommended for quality peaberry lots

## Brewing Peaberry

### Grind Adjustment

Peaberries are denser and harder than standard flat beans of equivalent origin. This affects how they behave at the grinder:

- **Grind coarser:** Start slightly coarser than your standard setting for the same origin
- **Why:** Denser beans resist grinding differently, resulting in a finer effective grind at the same burr setting
- **Signs of over-extraction:** Bitterness, astringency, or a long, hollow finish — back off the grind
- **Recalibrate per lot:** Density varies by origin, processing, and roast level

### Recommended Brew Methods

- **Pour over (V60, Kalita Wave):** Best method for showcasing concentrated origin character and bright acidity; highlights the clarity benefit of even roasting
- **Chemex:** Excellent for clean, clear cup expression; well-suited to light-roast peaberry lots
- **Aeropress:** Forgiving and versatile; good for experimenting with different extraction variables
- **French press:** Highlights body; softens acidity; accessible and approachable
- **Espresso:** Works well for origins with natural body and sweetness (Tanzania, Kenya peaberry in milk drinks); calibrate carefully
- **Cold brew:** Excellent; wine-like and fruit notes from East African peaberries translate beautifully into cold extraction

### Brewing Parameters

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Water temperature | 93–96°C (199–205°F) for light to light-medium roast |
| Brew ratio (filter) | 1:15–1:17 |
| Grind | Start slightly coarser than flat-bean baseline |
| Bloom | 30–45 seconds |
| Total pour-over time | 2:30–3:30 |
| Notes | Adjust to taste; peaberry is relatively forgiving once grind is calibrated correctly |

### Practical Tips

- Always taste before adjusting — peaberry lots can be forgiving once grind is right
- For new lots, start with a coarser grind and work inward; it is easier to extract more than to recover from over-extraction
- When comparing peaberry to flat-bean from the same origin, use identical brew parameters and adjust grind only
- Peaberry espresso benefits from longer pre-infusion due to density
