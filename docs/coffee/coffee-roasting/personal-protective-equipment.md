## Overview

Personal protective equipment (PPE) in a coffee roastery refers to the wearable protective items that reduce operator exposure to the physical hazards of the roasting environment — principally thermal burns, respiratory exposure to smoke and VOCs, eye injury from hot particles or steam, and hearing damage from equipment noise. PPE does not eliminate hazards; it reduces the consequence of exposure when other controls (engineering, procedural) have been applied and a residual risk remains. The appropriate selection of roastery PPE is informed by a hazard assessment of the specific roaster, facility, and task.

## Thermal and Burn Protection

**Heat-resistant gloves:** The most important item of PPE in most roastery environments:
- Long-cuffed leather or Kevlar gloves rated for contact with surfaces up to 250–300°C
- Required when operating the cooling tray immediately after drop, pulling samples, and cleaning hot roaster surfaces after a session
- Thin examination gloves or silicone oven mitts are inadequate for roaster use; full leather or Kevlar is the standard

**Footwear:** Closed-toe, leather or heat-resistant upper construction:
- Protects against hot bean spillage at drop and from the cooling tray
- Steel-toed boots are not required in most roastery contexts but improve protection in high-volume operations where heavy equipment handling is routine
- Open-toe shoes and synthetic-soled footwear are inappropriate

**Clothing:** Long-sleeved natural fibre garments:
- Cotton or wool preferred; synthetic fibres (polyester, nylon) melt against skin in burn contact
- Loose-fitting clothing that does not trail near the roaster, drum shaft, or rotating components
- Aprons in natural fibres may provide additional protection for operators working closely with the cooling tray

## Eye and Face Protection

**Safety glasses:** Minimum standard for all roastery personnel during active roasting:
- Protection against chaff particles, steam, and hot material expelled from the sample port during tryer use
- Standard clear safety glasses complying with relevant standards (AS/NZS 1337 in Australia; ANSI Z87.1 in the US)

**Face shield:** Recommended when pulling samples from the tryer or working near the drum discharge:
- Full-face protection against steam jets and hot particle projection
- Should be used in addition to, not instead of, safety glasses

## Respiratory Protection

Roastery airborne hazards include:
- VOCs from pyrolysis and Maillard reactions
- Fine particulate matter (smoke, chaff fragments)
- Carbon monoxide from gas combustion

**Primary control:** Adequate ventilation is the primary respiratory control; PPE is secondary. If ventilation is functioning correctly and regulatory exposure limits are being met, additional respiratory PPE may not be required for routine roasting tasks.

**When respiratory PPE is warranted:**
- During cleaning and maintenance tasks that disturb accumulated roaster deposits (chaff, oil residue)
- When ventilation is temporarily impaired
- In high-volume roasting environments where spot-measured VOC or particulate concentrations exceed limits

**Appropriate types:**
- **P1/P2 disposable particulate respirators (N95 equivalent):** For particulate matter during cleaning tasks
- **Half-face respirator with OV/P2 cartridges:** For combined organic vapour and particulate protection during high-exposure maintenance

## Hearing Protection

Commercial drum roasters, cooling tray fans, exhaust fans, and afterburner systems produce sustained noise levels that can exceed occupational exposure limits in enclosed roastery spaces:

- Assess noise levels at the operator position; if sustained exposure exceeds 85 dB(A), hearing protection is required
- Foam earplugs or earmuff-style hearing protection rated at adequate noise reduction
- Hearing protection should not prevent the operator from hearing first crack or other critical auditory roast cues; assess whether hearing protectors at appropriate attenuation levels allow adequate auditory monitoring

## Key Facts

- Heat-resistant long-cuffed leather or Kevlar gloves are the most critical PPE item for roaster operators
- Closed-toe leather footwear protects against hot bean spillage; synthetic soles and open-toe shoes are unsuitable
- Safety glasses minimum for all personnel; face shield recommended during tryer sampling
- Ventilation is the primary respiratory control; PPE supplements ventilation but does not replace it
- Assess roastery noise levels; hearing protection required if sustained exposure exceeds 85 dB(A)
