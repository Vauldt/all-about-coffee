# High-Volume Roasting

## Overview

High-volume roasting refers to coffee roasting operations producing large quantities of roasted coffee — typically from several hundred kilograms to multiple tonnes per week — on commercial-scale drum roasters or production lines. The operational requirements, quality management systems, and equipment choices of high-volume roasting differ substantially from small specialty roastery operations. While the fundamental roasting chemistry and profile principles remain the same, high-volume roasting demands greater emphasis on automation, batch consistency, energy efficiency, regulatory compliance, and industrial-grade equipment maintenance.

## Equipment for High-Volume Roasting

**Large drum roasters:** Commercial high-volume operations use drum roasters with batch capacities from 60 kg to 300 kg or more per batch:
- **60–120 kg:** Probat P60/P90, Diedrich IR-77, Loring Merlin — high-end specialty commercial range
- **120–240 kg:** Probat P120/P240, Burns International, Gothot — mid-to-large commercial
- **Above 240 kg:** Probat UG/PRG series, Neuhaus Neotec, Petroncini — large-scale commercial and industrial

**Continuous roasters:** Some very high-volume commercial operations use continuous (conveyor-type) roasters rather than batch drums, allowing uninterrupted production at a set throughput rate. These are less common in specialty contexts.

**Loring Smart Roaster:** A recirculating single-burner design that combusts exhaust internally, eliminating the need for a separate afterburner while delivering high-volume production (up to 35 kg batch / Falcon model to 70 kg batch / Merlin) with significantly reduced energy consumption compared to conventional drum systems.

## Quality Management at High Volume

High-volume roasting cannot rely on the per-batch artisan attention possible in a small roastery. Quality management systems are essential:

- **Automated profile control:** Motorised dampers, modulating gas valves, and integrated roast computers (Cropster, Probat Profile, proprietary systems) execute profiles with minimal manual intervention, reducing operator-to-operator variability
- **Statistical Process Control (SPC):** Batch parameters (Drop Temperature, DTR, yield, colour) are tracked statistically across all production batches; control charts flag out-of-limit batches for review before dispatch
- **Colour measurement:** Agtron spectrophotometers or equivalent colour measurement on every batch (or a statistical sample) provides an objective colour consistency check
- **Cupping sampling protocol:** A defined percentage of production batches are cupped; not every batch can be cupped in high-volume operations, making statistical sampling essential

## Energy Efficiency Considerations

High-volume roasting consumes substantial gas and electricity:
- Afterburner systems for VOC abatement add significant fuel consumption
- Heat recovery (recuperative afterburners, heat exchangers) is economically important at high volume
- Loring-style recirculating roasters reduce energy consumption by 80–90% vs. conventional drum + afterburner systems
- Larger batches have better energy efficiency per kilogram of roasted coffee than smaller batches on the same roaster (more efficient use of pre-heat energy)

## Consecutive Batch Considerations

High-volume sessions roasting many consecutive batches face amplified version of the normal consecutive consistency challenge:
- Drum heat accumulation is more pronounced over 20–40 consecutive batches than over 5–10
- Systematic charge temperature reduction across a long session must be carefully calibrated and consistently applied
- Operator changeover (shift changes mid-session) introduces consistency risk if handover procedures are not rigorous

## Regulatory Requirements

High-volume roasteries are subject to stricter environmental and safety regulation than small operations:
- Afterburner or equivalent VOC abatement typically mandatory above defined throughput thresholds
- Noise level assessments may be required for equipment modifications
- Fire suppression systems may be mandated
- Occupational health monitoring (air quality, noise) may be required under workplace safety legislation

## Key Facts

- High-volume roasting (> several hundred kg/week) requires large drum roasters (60–300 kg+ batch), automated profile control, and systematic quality management
- Automated profile systems reduce operator variability; SPC and colour measurement provide objective quality gates
- Energy efficiency: heat recovery from afterburners, larger batch sizes, and recirculating roaster designs (Loring) reduce per-kg fuel cost
- Consecutive consistency management is more critical and complex in long, high-volume sessions than in small specialty operations
- Regulatory requirements for VOC abatement, noise, and workplace safety are more stringent above defined production thresholds
