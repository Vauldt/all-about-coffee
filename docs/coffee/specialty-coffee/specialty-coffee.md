# Specialty Coffee

*"Specialty coffee describes beans of the best flavour which are produced in special micro-climates." — Erna Knutsen, 1974*


## Overview

Specialty coffee refers to the broader industry movement emphasising: 
- traceability from farm to cup,
- sustainable and ethical sourcing,
- origin expressiveness, 
- skilled roasting, and 
- precision brewing. 

The specialty coffee sector emerged as a distinct market in the 1960s and 1970s and has grown substantially since the 1990s.


## Defining Specialty Coffee

Technical Definition of Specialty Coffee 
Specialty coffee is a category of coffee defined by measurable quality standards: 
- Green coffee scoring 80 or more points on the SCA 100-point cupping scale, with
- No Category 1 defects (black beans, sour beans, fungus damage, foreign matter) in the green sample. 

**Technical definition (SCA):**
- Green coffee cupping score ≥ 80/100
- Zero Category 1 defects per 350 g green sample
- Fewer than five Category 2 defects per 350 g green sample
- Acceptable moisture content (10–12%) and water activity

The specialty coffee movement encompasses the full supply chain — 
- producers, 
- exporters, 
- green coffee buyers, 
- roasters, 
- baristas, and 
- consumers
At all stages priority must be given to coffee quality, traceability, and direct relationships over commodity pricing and mass production.

## SCA Score Scale

| Score | Classification |
| :--- | :--- |
| 90–100 | Outstanding |
| 85–89.99 | Excellent |
| 80–84.99 | Very Good (specialty threshold) |
| Below 80 | Not specialty grade |

## Key Characteristics

**Traceability:** Specialty coffee is typically sold with producer, farm, region, variety, processing method, and harvest year identified — enabling buyers, roasters, and consumers to understand origin.

**Direct trade:** Many specialty roasters buy directly from producers or through importers with transparent sourcing, often paying above commodity prices to incentivise quality at origin.

**Processing transparency:** Washed, natural, honey, and anaerobic processing are detailed as part of the product description; processing method is understood to significantly influence cup character.

**Roasting philosophy:** Specialty roasters generally aim to express origin character rather than roast character — lighter roasts preserving the flavour compounds created at origin.

**Brewing precision:** Specialty cafés and consumers use calibrated brewing equipment, weight-based measurements, and water quality control to achieve consistent, quality-focused extraction.

## Specialty vs. Commodity Coffee

| Property | Specialty | Commodity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Quality threshold | ≥ 80 SCA points | Below 80 points; blended for consistency |
| Traceability | Farm / lot level | Country or regional blend |
| Pricing | Above commodity market; direct or relationship-based | New York C market (ICE futures) |
| Defect tolerance | Zero Category 1; minimal Category 2 | Higher defect rates acceptable |
| Roast profile | Light to medium; origin-expressive | Medium to dark; roast-forward |
| Consumer focus | Sensory quality; origin education | Consistency; convenience; price |

## Key Facts

- Specialty coffee is technically defined as ≥ 80 SCA cupping points with zero Category 1 green defects
- The specialty sector emphasises traceability, sustainability, and farm-to-cup quality
- SCA (Specialty Coffee Association) is the primary industry body establishing standards for green grading, cupping, brewing, and barista education
- Emerged as a distinct market sector in the 1970s; rapid growth in the 2000s and 2010s associated with the "third wave" movement
- Specialty coffee accounts for a minority of global coffee volume but a disproportionately high share of value and industry innovation
