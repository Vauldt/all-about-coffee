# How do pricing and perceived value differ between specialty cafés and chains?

← Part of [Common Questions About Coffee Culture 1](../coffee-culture/common-questions-about-coffee-culture-1.md)

Specialty cafés and chains operate in different pricing frameworks despite selling nominally similar products. Chain pricing is anchored to volume, customisation, and convenience — Starbucks' commercial success is partly a function of its ability to charge a premium for a product that is extensively modified to individual taste (sweetness, milk type, flavour additions) at high throughput. The premium is for convenience, customisation, and brand consistency rather than intrinsic product quality.

Specialty cafés charge premiums based on a different value proposition: sourcing transparency, technical preparation quality, provenance of coffee and milk, and the competence of the barista. A $6.50 specialty flat white is priced against the cost of high-grade green coffee, quality roasting, precision espresso preparation, and the labour cost of a trained professional — not against the $5.00 chain equivalent.

Consumer perception of value diverges accordingly. Chain customers tend to evaluate value through a lens of consistency, speed, and portion size. Specialty customers evaluate through sensory quality, experience, and the alignment of the product with their values (ethical sourcing, craft, locality). Research on willingness to pay shows that specialty coffee consumers accept significantly higher price points when the value framing is explicit and credible — but this credibility must be earned through visible signals: knowledgeable staff, transparent sourcing information, and product quality that is actually distinguishable from the alternative.
