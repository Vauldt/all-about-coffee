## Overview

Coffee brewing is the process of extracting soluble compounds — including organic acids, sugars, lipids, and aromatic molecules — from roasted and ground coffee using water as a solvent. The quality and character of the final cup depend on managing a set of interacting variables: grind size, water temperature, brew ratio (coffee mass to water mass), contact time, agitation or flow pattern, and water mineral composition. Different brewing methods manipulate these variables in distinct ways, yielding different flavour profiles, body, and strength characteristics. The brewer's task is to maximise extraction of desirable compounds — acids, Maillard products, and aromatic volatiles — while limiting the extraction of harsh bitter and astringent compounds that are released at high extraction yields.

## Brewing Method Categories

All coffee brewing methods fall into one of four categories based on the physical mechanism of extraction:

| Category | Mechanism | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Immersion | Grounds steep in water; separated after a set time | French press, AeroPress, cold brew, cupping |
| Percolation | Water flows through a bed of grounds once | Pour-over (V60, Chemex, Kalita), drip filter, Moka pot |
| Pressure | Water forced through compacted grounds under pressure | Espresso, AeroPress (pressure variant) |
| Decoction / boiling | Coffee boiled or simmered directly in water | Turkish coffee (cezve/ibrik), cowboy coffee |

Hybrid methods (such as the Clever Dripper) combine immersion with a percolation release stage. Each category produces a characteristic cup — immersion tends to fuller body and more even extraction; percolation produces a cleaner, more delicate cup; pressure creates concentrated, emulsified espresso.

## Key Brewing Variables

**Grind size:** The most impactful variable for controlling extraction rate. Finer grinds present more surface area, extracting faster; coarser grinds extract more slowly. Each brew method requires a specific grind range for optimal extraction time.

**Water temperature:** Higher temperatures increase solubility and extraction speed. The SCA recommends 90–96 °C for most filter brewing. Lower temperatures (as in cold brew) require extended contact time to compensate for reduced extraction efficiency.

**Brew ratio:** The mass of coffee relative to the mass of water (e.g., 1:15 = 1 g coffee per 15 g water). Ratio determines the strength (TDS) of the brew independently from extraction yield. SCA filter target: 1:15–1:17.

**Contact time:** Duration of water–coffee contact. Too short = under-extraction (sour, thin); too long = over-extraction (bitter, harsh).

**Water quality:** Mineral content, hardness, and the complete absence of chlorine are prerequisites for quality extraction. See [Water in Coffee MOC](../maps-of-content/water-in-coffee-moc.md).

## Key Facts

- Coffee brewing extracts soluble compounds from ground coffee using water; the three core mechanisms are immersion, percolation, and pressure
- Five primary variables: grind size, water temperature, brew ratio, contact time, and water quality
- Under-extraction (too short, too coarse, too cool): sour, weak, thin; over-extraction (too long, too fine, too hot): bitter, harsh, astringent
- SCA brewing target for filter: extraction yield 18–22%, TDS 1.15–1.35%, water temperature 90–96 °C
- Paper filtration removes coffee lipids (diterpenes), producing a cleaner but lighter-bodied cup than metal-filtered or immersion methods
