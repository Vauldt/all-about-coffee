# SCA Barista Skills Training Guide

## A Cafe Manager's Syllabus for Staff Development & Progression Planning

> A structured training and certification roadmap built around the Specialty Coffee Association (SCA) Coffee Skills Program — Barista Skills module. Designed for cafe owners, head baristas, and operations leads who need to develop staff systematically from new hire to senior barista.

## 1. Program Overview

The **SCA Coffee Skills Program (CSP)** is the global standard for professional coffee education. The Barista Skills module is one of six disciplines (alongside Introduction to Coffee, Green Coffee, Sensory Skills, Roasting, and Brewing), and is the most directly relevant pathway for cafe staff development ([SCA Education](https://education.sca.coffee)).

Each Barista Skills certificate combines:

- **A written exam** delivered online through the SCA Digital Campus
- **A practical exam** delivered in person by an Authorized SCA Trainer (AST) at a Premier Training Campus or partner school

Successful candidates earn **points** that build toward the **SCA Coffee Skills Diploma** (100 points total, drawn from any combination of certificates across the six disciplines) ([SCA Skills Diploma](https://education.sca.coffee/sca-skills-diploma); [r/SpecialtyCoffee summary of point values](https://www.reddit.com/r/SpecialtyCoffee/comments/16r1jr2/sca_diplomas_and_certifications/)).

### Why use SCA as a staff development framework

- **Globally portable credential** — recognised in specialty cafes worldwide
- **Structured progression** — each level explicitly assumes the prior, so training compounds
- **Independent assessment** — removes manager bias and gives staff an external benchmark
- **Aligns with industry standards** — drink definitions, water quality, foam standard, and Gold Cup all reference SCA documents ([SCA Coffee Standards](https://sca.coffee/research/coffee-standards))

## 2. The Certification Pathway at a Glance

| Level | Points | Min. Course Hours | Written Exam | Practical Exam | Pass Mark (Written) | Recommended Experience |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Foundation** | 5 | 7 hours min. | 20 questions / 22 minutes | 40 minutes / 3 sections | 60% | None — entry level |
| **Intermediate** | 10 | 14 hours (12 + exams) | ~25–30 questions | Multi-section practical | 70% | 6+ months as a working barista |
| **Professional** | 25 | 21 hours min. (incl. exam) | 30 questions / 32 minutes | 95 minutes / 4 sections | 80% | 12+ months, ideally with some managerial responsibility |

Sources: [Espresso Academic SCA Barista Curriculum](https://www.espressoacademic.com/sca-barista-curriculum); [ROCC — Professional SCA Modules](https://sca.training/professional-sca-modules); [ROCC — How SCA Training Works](https://sca.training/how-sca-training-works).

### Reading this table as a manager

- **Foundation** is for *every* barista on the floor. Treat it as the baseline hire-to-bar standard.
- **Intermediate** is for baristas with at least one full season of bar experience. It introduces measurement, dialing-in, and workflow under pressure.
- **Professional** is for shift leads and senior baristas who train others. It rewards depth: brew theory, recipe development, menu construction, and team leadership.

## 3. Foundation Level (5 points)

> **Course aim**: Equip a beginner with the core practical skills required to set a grinder, pull espresso, texture milk, and produce SCA-standard espresso-based drinks while observing health, safety, and customer service practices ([Espresso Academic](https://www.espressoacademic.com/sca-barista-curriculum); [Boot Coffee](https://bootcoffee.com/sca-barista-module-coffee-skills-program/)).

### 3.1 Core competencies

| Domain | Competencies |
|---|---|
| **Coffee fundamentals** | Distinguish Arabica from Robusta; explain freshness and the influence of roast degree on flavour |
| **Workspace & workflow** | Identify components of grinder and espresso machine; demonstrate safe use; maintain a clean and organised station |
| **Espresso process** | Grind, dose, distribute and tamp consistently; calibrate grinder to a target recipe |
| **Extraction & brewing** | Calculate and apply brew ratio and the Espresso Brew Formula (EBF); understand strength vs extraction conceptually |
| **Sensory** | Use SCA Coffee Taster's Flavor Wheel terminology to describe espresso aroma, flavour, body, and texture |
| **Milk** | Foam to SCA Foam Standard; texture milk for cappuccino with correct heating temperature; perform basic free-pour latte art |
| **Espresso menu** | Produce SCA-standard espresso, macchiato, cappuccino, latte; describe drink composition |
| **Cleaning, health & safety** | Backflush espresso machine; daily grinder cleaning; stock rotation; cross-contamination prevention |
| **Water quality** | Apply SCA water guidelines (TDS 75–250 ppm target ~150 ppm; calcium hardness 50–175 ppm; alkalinity ~40–70 ppm; neutral pH; clean odour, clear colour) ([SCA water standards summary](https://thirdwavewater.eu/blogs/news/what-is-sca-water-standard-complete-guide-to-coffee-brewing-water-quality)) |
| **Customer service** | Describe the menu accurately; handle orders; basic complaint recovery |

### 3.2 Specific technical milestones

**Espresso**

- Pull a double espresso to a target recipe (e.g., 18 g in / 36 g out / ~25–30 s) within tolerance
- Adjust grind correctly when shot runs fast or slow
- Maintain consistent dose (±0.5 g) across consecutive shots
- Clean group head, backflush with detergent, replace filter baskets and shower screens

**Milk**

- Steam milk to **65°C target / 70°C maximum** (per SCA milk heating guidance) ([Espresso Academic](https://www.espressoacademic.com/sca-barista-curriculum))
- Produce microfoam free of large bubbles (SCA Foam Standard)
- Pour a recognisable basic latte art form (heart or tulip) on a cappuccino and a latte
- Demonstrate jug-sharing technique to split textured milk between two drinks

**Workflow & maintenance**

- Set up bar at open and break down at close
- Daily grinder hopper clean and burr brush; group seal inspection
- Backflush water-only between shots; backflush with detergent end-of-day
- Stock rotation (FIFO) for milk, beans, syrups

### 3.3 Assessment criteria

**Written exam** ([Espresso Academic](https://www.espressoacademic.com/sca-barista-curriculum))

- 20 multiple-choice questions / 22 minutes
- **Passing score: 60%**
- Topic distribution: coffee beans, workflow, grind/dose/tamp, extraction, sensory, milk, menu, cleaning/safety

**Practical exam**

- 40 minutes total / 3 sections
- Section pass marks (per Espresso Academic curriculum reference):
  - Section 1: 50% (3 of 6 points)
  - Section 2: 82.7% (48 points)
  - Section 3: 64% (9 points)
- **All three sections must be passed** to pass the exam

Practical assessment evaluates ([Tasse Coffee SCA Foundation guide](https://tasse-coffee.jp/blogs/sca-courses/sca-barista-skills-foundation-what-youll-learn-in-every-module)):

- Espresso shot consistency
- Milk steaming quality and pour
- Latte art execution
- Customer service and communication
- Equipment knowledge and safety

### 3.4 Required practical experience

- **Pre-course**: None mandated, but Introduction to Coffee is recommended
- **In-course**: Minimum 7 hours of structured practical training
- **Post-course on-the-job target before moving to Intermediate**: 6 months working bar shifts (~500–800 hours), producing the full SCA drinks menu daily

### 3.5 Manager's Foundation readiness checklist

- [ ] Sets up and breaks down the espresso bar unsupervised
- [ ] Pulls 10 consecutive double espressos within ±2 g and ±3 s of target recipe
- [ ] Textures milk for a cappuccino and a latte without re-steaming
- [ ] Pours at minimum a clean heart, tulip, or rosetta
- [ ] Completes daily, weekly, and monthly cleaning checklist accurately
- [ ] Describes every drink on the menu including milk-alternative options
- [ ] Handles a customer complaint using the cafe's recovery script

## 4. Intermediate Level (10 points)

> **Course aim**: Test core and advanced skills expected of a competent barista with around 6+ months experience. Successful candidates can dial in a brew recipe, produce the full drinks range with quality and speed, and meet basic customer service and maintenance demands ([SCA Barista Intermediate Curriculum, January 2018, V 2.0](https://www.scribd.com/document/721078063/1537955543-16273-FT196051-sca-barista-intermediate-curriculum-v2-0-2018)).

### 4.1 Core competencies

| Domain | Competencies (advancing on Foundation) |
|---|---|
| **Workflow management** | Lay out grinder → machine → fridge → service in efficient flow; demonstrate **double-baring** (working in pairs) |
| **Extraction & brewing** | Apply EBF and brew ratio with measurement; explain relationship between input variables and output flavour; taste differences between, e.g., 40% EBF vs 60% EBF recipe |
| **Recipe dialing-in** | Assess shots by taste, weight, and time; adjust grind, dose and ratio to bring extraction into target range (Gold Cup: TDS 1.15–1.35%, extraction 18–22%) ([Femobook on Gold Cup](https://www.femobook.com/blogs/coffee-knowledge/a-guide-to-the-golden-cup-standard)) |
| **Sensory** | Describe espresso using SCA Coffee Taster's Flavor Wheel; relate compound extraction order to taste development |
| **Milk** | Understand milk composition (proteins, fats, lactose) and processing (homogenisation, pasteurisation); foam quality and stability; effect of heat, acidity, proteolysis, lipolysis on foaming |
| **Drinks production at speed** | Prepare a complex 4-drink order to a high standard within **5 minutes**; steam milk and extract espresso simultaneously |
| **Customer service** | Adapt orders to special requests (alternative milks, decaf, foam levels, temperatures, sizing, strength); maintain communication during preparation |
| **Maintenance** | Perform deeper preventive maintenance: shower screen and gasket replacement intervals; grinder burr inspection |
| **Health & safety** | Allergen awareness; food safety regulations; injury prevention under pressure |

### 4.2 Specific technical milestones

**Extraction**

- Dial in an unfamiliar coffee from cold to within target recipe in under 6 shots
- Identify and correct under-extraction (sour, thin, fast) and over-extraction (bitter, dry, slow)
- Verify recipe with a refractometer (or describe the procedure if one is unavailable)
- Hold espresso temperature, pressure, and dose consistent while changing grind

**Milk**

- Steam multiple drinks to consistent texture across pitcher sizes (350 ml, 600 ml, 1 L)
- Recover from a milk error (split, scorched, large bubbles) without affecting service speed
- Produce three latte art patterns reliably: heart, tulip (3+ leaves), rosetta

**Workflow / 4-drink complex order benchmark** ([Intermediate curriculum 2.06.2](https://www.scribd.com/document/721078063/1537955543-16273-FT196051-sca-barista-intermediate-curriculum-v2-0-2018))

- 4 drinks completed in under 5 minutes
- Concurrent espresso extraction and milk steaming
- All drinks meet SCA Drinks Definitions standards
- Order flow accommodates special requests without rebuilding

### 4.3 Assessment criteria

**Written exam**

- Online via SCA Digital Campus; longer than Foundation (course providers report ~25–30 questions)
- **Passing score: 70%** (industry-standard mark for Intermediate)

**Practical exam** ([HGC Learning Lab](https://coffeelearninglab.com/products/sca-barista-intermediate))

- Tests grinder calibration, espresso extraction analysis, latte art skills, and efficient drink construction
- Multiple sections; all must be passed
- Tasting and verbal description of differences between two recipes is part of the practical

### 4.4 Required practical experience

- **Pre-course**: Introduction to Coffee and Barista Foundation strongly recommended (knowledge and skills are assumed and may be tested)
- **In-course**: Minimum 12 hours plus exams (14 hours total)
- **Post-course on-the-job target before moving to Professional**: 12 months total bar experience, ideally including some opening / closing shift responsibility

### 4.5 Manager's Intermediate readiness checklist

- [ ] Dials in a fresh bag of beans without supervision and documents the recipe
- [ ] Articulates the difference between strength (TDS) and extraction (yield) in plain language
- [ ] Produces 4 complex drinks in under 5 minutes during peak service
- [ ] Switches between two grinders (e.g., decaf and regular) without dropping recipe accuracy
- [ ] Pours rosetta, tulip, and inverted heart at speed, including in 6 oz cups
- [ ] Replaces shower screens, gaskets, and performs grinder burr inspection
- [ ] Trains a Foundation-level barista on a single skill (e.g., tamping)

## 5. Professional Level (25 points)

> **Course aim**: Test the advanced skills and detailed scientific understanding expected of a head barista or senior bar professional with 12+ months experience. Candidates demonstrate sophisticated tasting methodology, drink-ingredient mastery, recipe and menu development, team leadership over drink quality, and the highest standard of milk steaming and latte art ([Espresso Academic](https://www.espressoacademic.com/sca-barista-curriculum); [ROCC Professional Modules](https://sca.training/professional-sca-modules)).

### 5.1 Core competencies

| Domain | Competencies (advancing on Intermediate) |
|---|---|
| **Coffee science** | Detail of milk composition and processing; foam creation, quality, and stability mechanisms; proteolysis, lipolysis, coffee acidity and heat effects on milk |
| **Sensory leadership** | Sophisticated tasting methodology; structured sensory descriptions; calibration of staff palates |
| **Recipe development** | Build espresso brew recipes from scratch with reasoned variable choices; document and roll out a recipe for staff |
| **Menu construction** | Systematically structure beverage menus; balance drink profiles; cost vs quality decisions |
| **Drink ingredients** | Detailed understanding of every ingredient (multiple milks, alt milks, syrups, water profiles) and their effect on final drink |
| **Latte art at standard** | Apply highest consistent standard of free-pour latte art across drink sizes and milk types |
| **Workflow leadership** | Manage skills of others on bar; quality-control a team's output; train and coach |
| **Maintenance & equipment** | Diagnose machine issues; manage preventive maintenance schedule; advise on equipment selection |

### 5.2 Specific technical milestones

**Recipe & menu**

- Develop a documented brew recipe for a new coffee, justify each parameter (dose, ratio, time, temperature, basket choice), and present tasting notes
- Construct or restructure a multi-drink menu with rationale (target customer, flavour balance, throughput, profitability)
- Calibrate two baristas on the same recipe so their shots taste interchangeable

**Advanced sensory**

- Describe a coffee using a structured methodology (intensity, quality, descriptors aligned to the Coffee Taster's Flavor Wheel)
- Triangulate differences between three espresso samples and identify the odd one
- Coach another barista to taste under- vs over-extraction blind

**Latte art at the top standard**

- Pour at standard in standard cup sizes and in alternative milks (oat, soy, almond) without dropping definition
- Demonstrate at least one advanced free-pour pattern (e.g., wing tulip, swan, multi-element rosetta) consistently

**Team & workflow leadership**

- Run a pre-shift bar calibration: dial-in, milk check, station setup
- Identify a workflow bottleneck and propose a fix (layout, ingredient placement, order routing)
- Document a maintenance schedule (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly) for grinders, machine, water filtration, and fridge

### 5.3 Assessment criteria

**Written exam** ([Espresso Academic](https://www.espressoacademic.com/sca-barista-curriculum))

- 30 questions / 32 minutes online
- **Passing score: 80%**

**Practical exam** ([ROCC Professional Modules](https://sca.training/professional-sca-modules))

- 95 minutes / 4 sections
- Each section is calibrated to a unique skill competency
- **All sections must be passed**; a 100% score is not expected, but competence must be shown in every section
- Section-specific pass marks are stated on the exam paper itself

The practical typically covers: advanced extraction analysis, recipe development under time pressure, simultaneous high-volume drink production, advanced milk and latte art, and a verbal/applied component on team and quality management.

### 5.4 Required practical experience

- **Pre-requisite**: Barista Skills **Intermediate** is required
- **Recommended pre-requisites**: Introduction to Coffee, Barista Skills Foundation, **Brewing Intermediate**
- **Ideal candidate**: 12+ months as a working barista, ideally with some managerial responsibility
- **Course length**: Minimum 21 hours including practical exam

### 5.5 Manager's Professional readiness checklist

- [ ] Owns the espresso recipe document for the cafe and updates it for new coffees
- [ ] Has trained at least one barista from Foundation skill level to Intermediate readiness
- [ ] Leads the open or close at least 3 shifts per week
- [ ] Diagnoses and resolves at least one common machine fault (e.g., temperature drift, group leak, low pressure)
- [ ] Produces SCA-standard drinks across at least 3 milk types without recipe drift
- [ ] Calibrates the team during pre-shift and signs off on bar opening
- [ ] Communicates extraction theory to non-baristas (front-of-house, ownership) clearly

## 6. Manager's Progression Planning Toolkit

### 6.1 Staff development matrix

Use this matrix to map every barista on your team. Update quarterly.

| Barista | Hire date | Current SCA level | Internal level (1–5) | Next milestone | Target cert date | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| _e.g., A. Nguyen_ | 2025-09 | Foundation (cert) | 2 | Dial-in unsupervised | Q3 2026 | Latte art strong, slow on dial-in |
| _e.g., R. Patel_ | 2024-01 | Intermediate (cert) | 4 | Train 1 junior | Q4 2026 | Ready for Pro within 6 months |

### 6.2 Suggested training timeline (per barista)

| Tenure | Internal target | SCA target |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 weeks | Onboarding, machine safety, opening/closing checklist, drink menu memorisation | — |
| 1–3 months | Drink production to cafe standard (assistant role) | — |
| 3–6 months | Bar-lead competence, basic dial-in supervised, latte art baseline | **Foundation** course + cert |
| 6–12 months | Solo bar shifts at peak, dial-in unsupervised, training a new hire on one skill | Cross-training: Sensory Foundation or Brewing Foundation |
| 12–18 months | Quality control, complex 4-drink benchmark, opening calibration | **Intermediate** course + cert |
| 18–24 months | Recipe development, menu input, team training, equipment maintenance | **Brewing Intermediate** (recommended bridge) |
| 24+ months | Bar leadership, hiring panel, full equipment ownership | **Professional** course + cert |

### 6.3 Pre-course preparation: a 4-week plan

For each level, run this four-week prep before sending staff to an external SCA course:

- **Week 1 — Knowledge**: assigned reading from the SCA Coffee Standards and the Coffee Taster's Flavor Wheel; daily 15-minute quiz.
- **Week 2 — Drills**: targeted bar drills (e.g., for Intermediate: blind dial-in from a fresh bag; for Professional: recipe build for an unfamiliar coffee).
- **Week 3 — Mock practical**: simulate the practical exam under time pressure, with another senior barista assessing.
- **Week 4 — Mock written**: 20–30 question quiz mirroring the SCA exam format and pass mark; revise weak areas.

### 6.4 Cost / ROI considerations for managers

- Foundation, Intermediate, and Professional certs together total **40 of the 100 points** required for the SCA Coffee Skills Diploma — strong career incentive for staff retention.
- Co-investment models (cafe pays course fee, barista commits to 12 months post-cert) align incentives without exposing the business to one-off training risk.
- Bundle external SCA training with internal recipes, SOPs, and machine quirks — SCA gives the framework, your cafe gives the context.

## 7. Internal Milestones Between SCA Levels

External SCA certificates are punctuation marks; the daily progression of a barista happens between them. Use these internal levels (1–5) as in-house signals.

### Internal Level 1 — Trainee

- Can describe every drink on the menu
- Steams milk to texture for cappuccinos under supervision
- Pulls espresso to recipe under supervision
- Completes opening or closing checklist with a senior barista present

### Internal Level 2 — Bar Assistant (Foundation-equivalent)

- Solo bar capable in non-peak periods
- Pours heart and tulip reliably
- Daily cleaning unsupervised
- Holds **SCA Barista Foundation** or is course-ready

### Internal Level 3 — Solo Barista

- Solo bar at peak service
- Dials in unsupervised; documents recipe in shift log
- Pours rosetta consistently; can pour into 6 oz cups
- Trains Level 1 trainees on a single defined skill
- Holds **SCA Barista Intermediate** or is course-ready

### Internal Level 4 — Senior Barista

- Owns at least one operational area (e.g., espresso quality, milk programme, water/maintenance)
- Leads pre-shift calibration
- Develops or refines a recipe with manager sign-off
- Can substitute as shift lead

### Internal Level 5 — Head Barista / Bar Lead

- Owns the cafe's espresso recipe document
- Trains the team end-to-end; runs internal certifications
- Manages preventive maintenance schedule
- Holds **SCA Barista Professional** or is course-ready
- Mentors candidates toward their next SCA cert

## 8. Appendices

### Appendix A — Glossary

- **AST** — Authorised SCA Trainer; the only individual permitted to deliver and assess SCA practical exams.
- **CSP** — Coffee Skills Program, the SCA's modular professional education programme.
- **EBF** — Espresso Brew Formula, the SCA's expression of brew ratio for espresso.
- **Gold Cup standard** — Brewed coffee target of TDS 1.15–1.35% with extraction 18–22% ([SCA via Moccamaster](https://support.moccamaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500009409121-What-is-the-Golden-Cup-standard)).
- **Premier Training Campus** — SCA-accredited education provider authorised to deliver CSP courses and exams.
- **SCA Foam Standard** — SCA-defined target for textured milk used in cappuccino and latte art preparation.
- **SCA Drinks Definitions** — SCA reference document defining espresso-based drinks (cappuccino, latte, macchiato, etc.) used as the assessment benchmark.
- **TDS** — Total Dissolved Solids, measured with a refractometer to determine brew strength.

### Appendix B — Equipment & resource checklist for in-house training

**Equipment**

- Commercial 2-group espresso machine (kept in calibration)
- Two grinders (e.g., one regular, one decaf or single-origin)
- Refractometer (e.g., VST or Atago) — essential for Intermediate / Professional drills
- Set of milk pitchers (350 ml, 600 ml, 1 L)
- Knock box, tampers (matched to basket diameter), distribution tool
- Cleaning chemicals: espresso machine detergent, grinder cleaner, milk-line cleaner
- TDS / hardness / alkalinity test strips for water programme
- Scales accurate to 0.1 g (one in / one out per grinder)

**Reference materials**

- SCA Coffee Standards documents ([sca.coffee/research/coffee-standards](https://sca.coffee/research/coffee-standards))
- SCA Drinks Definitions
- SCA Coffee Taster's Flavor Wheel (printed, on bar)
- Cafe's documented espresso recipe, milk SOP, maintenance schedule

**Training infrastructure**

- A weekly sensory / cupping session (30 min)
- A monthly internal calibration shift
- A shared digital log for recipes, dial-ins, equipment issues (Obsidian, Notion, or shared sheet)

### Appendix C — Sample shift drills

- **Foundation drill: tamp consistency** — pull 10 shots, weigh in/out, time each, record on a checklist. Reject any shot outside ±2 g and ±3 s.
- **Intermediate drill: blind dial-in** — senior barista loads an unmarked single-origin into the second grinder; barista has 6 shots to bring it to a specified ratio and time, judged by taste.
- **Intermediate drill: 4-drink benchmark** — produce 1 espresso, 1 cappuccino, 1 latte, 1 piccolo in under 5 minutes, all to SCA Drinks Definitions.
- **Professional drill: recipe development** — given a new coffee, document a recipe (dose, ratio, time, basket, temperature) with reasoning, and present to the team.
- **Professional drill: train-the-trainer** — senior barista coaches a Foundation-level barista on rosetta pouring, then has them reproduce it twice in front of the team.
