# 100-Point Scale

**Tags:** #coffee/tasting #coffee/tasting/evaluation

## Overview

The 100-Point Scale is the industry-standard scoring system for evaluating coffee quality, particularly in specialty coffee. Developed and standardised by the [Specialty Coffee Association (SCA)](../specialty-coffee/specialty-coffee-association-sca.md), this method provides an objective framework for assessing coffee across multiple sensory dimensions. A score of 80 or above designates a coffee as specialty grade.

## Score Categories

**Specialty Coffee**: 80–100 points

- These coffees exhibit distinctive characteristics, clarity, and no defects
- Must score 80 or above to receive specialty grade designation

**Premium Coffee**: 75–79.99 points

- Good quality coffees with few minor defects
- Suitable for commercial specialty use

**Exchange Grade**: 60–74.99 points

- Standard commercial coffee quality
- May have notable defects

**Below Standard**: below 60 points

- Significant defects present
- Not suitable for specialty market

### Point Range Detail

- **95–100**: Exceptional — virtually flawless, extraordinary complexity
- **90–94**: Outstanding — excellent quality with remarkable characteristics
- **85–89**: Very Good — high quality with notable attributes
- **80–84**: Good — specialty grade, clean cup with positive attributes
- **75–79**: Fair — commercial grade, acceptable quality
- **70–74**: Below Average — noticeable defects or deficiencies
- **Below 70**: Poor — significant defects

## Scoring Components

The 100-Point Scale evaluates coffee across these primary attributes:

### Core Attributes (10 points each)

- **[Aroma](aroma.md)**: Dry and wet aromatics
- **Flavour**: Primary taste impressions and characteristics
- **[Aftertaste](aftertaste.md)**: Length and quality of flavours after swallowing
- **[Acidity](acidity.md)**: Quality, intensity, and type of perceived acidity
- **[Body](body.md)**: Tactile mouthfeel and texture
- **[Balance](balance.md)**: How well components work together
- **[Sweetness](../coffee-tasting/sweetness.md)**: Natural sweetness (5 samples × 2 points)
- **Clean Cup**: Lack of defects (5 samples × 2 points)
- **Uniformity**: Consistency across cups (5 samples × 2 points)

### Additional Component

- **Overall**: Cupper's holistic assessment (10 points)

### Defect Deductions

Coffee Defects are subtracted from the base score of 36 points (for Sweetness, Clean Cup, and Uniformity when perfect). Defects are classified as "taint" or "fault" with varying severity.

## Final Score Calculation

**Base Score**: 36 points (for perfect Sweetness, Clean Cup, Uniformity)
**+ Attribute Scores**: 7 attributes × 10 points = 70 points maximum
**− Defects**: Subtracted based on severity
**= Total Score**: Out of 100 points

## Scoring Intensity

Each attribute is scored using specific intensity markers:

- **6.00**: Good (minimum specialty quality)
- **7.00**: Very Good
- **8.00**: Excellent
- **9.00**: Outstanding
- **10.00**: Extraordinary

Most specialty coffees score between 6.00–8.00 per attribute, with scores above 8.50 being exceptional.

## Scoring Methodology

### Holistic Evaluation

1. Assess overall quality impression
2. Consider complexity and balance
3. Evaluate cleanliness and intensity
4. Account for distinctiveness
5. Factor in defects (if any)

### Factors Influencing Score

- Cleanliness of cup
- Complexity of flavour
- Balance of attributes
- Intensity and clarity
- Uniqueness or typicity
- Presence or absence of defects

## Practical Application

### Who Uses This System?

- [Q Graders](../coffee-tasting/q-grader-certification.md) (certified coffee quality graders)
- Coffee importers and exporters
- Roasters for quality control and purchasing decisions
- Competition judges for [Cup of Excellence](../coffee-business/cup-of-excellence.md) and similar events
- Coffee producers for quality feedback

### Why It Matters

- Provides objective quality benchmarks
- Establishes pricing tiers in the green coffee market
- Enables clear communication about coffee quality across the supply chain
- Identifies coffees worthy of premium pricing
- Helps producers understand quality improvement areas

### When to Use

**Appropriate contexts:**

- Formal cupping sessions for green coffee evaluation
- Quality control and purchasing decisions
- Producer feedback and development
- Competition judging ([Cup of Excellence](../coffee-business/cup-of-excellence.md) and similar events)
- Consumer-facing scoring where a single number aids communication

**Less appropriate contexts:**

- Attribute-specific feedback where descriptor analysis is more useful
- Producer payment tied to specific defect counts (use [SCA Cupping Form](../coffee-tasting/sca-cupping-form.md) for granular data)
- Research requiring highly granular attribute data

## Advantages

- **Comprehensive**: Evaluates multiple sensory dimensions rather than a single impression
- **Standardised**: Internationally recognised, enabling consistent communication across the supply chain
- **Actionable**: Attribute-level breakdown tells producers exactly what to improve
- **Scalable**: Usable for single-origin coffees, blends, and competition evaluation alike
- **Intuitive**: The 80+ specialty threshold is widely understood in the industry

## Limitations

While widely adopted, the 100-Point Scale has some criticisms:

- **Subjectivity**: Despite structure, personal taste preferences influence scores
- **Narrow Range**: Most specialty coffees cluster between 83–88 points
- **Cultural Bias**: System reflects Western specialty coffee preferences
- **Limited Distinction**: Small point differences may not reflect meaningful quality gaps
- **Score Inflation**: Grade inflation concerns with many coffees scoring in similar ranges
- **Training Challenges**: Achieving inter-judge calibration requires significant practice and resources

## Calibration Challenges

Consistent scoring across judges is one of the system's practical difficulties.

**Common issues:**

- Different judges use different parts of the scale
- Personal bias affects scoring more than intended
- Difficult to agree on exact point increments
- Fewer reference standards available than for wine scoring

**Calibration methods:**

- Regular comparison sessions with coffees of known quality
- Blind triangle tests with coffees at different quality levels
- Discussion of score rationale between judges
- Anchoring scores to reference coffees at each intensity level

## Conversion from SCA Scores

Some practitioners use rough conversions between the holistic 100-point style and SCA aggregate scores:

| SCA Score | Approx. 100-Point Equivalent |
|-----------|------------------------------|
| 90+       | 95+                          |
| 87–89     | 92–94                        |
| 84–86     | 88–91                        |
| 80–83     | 85–87                        |

These conversions are approximate and not officially standardised.

## Best Practices

1. Establish personal anchors — create reference points at different score levels using coffees of known quality
2. Use increments consistently — decide whether scoring in whole, half, or tenth points and maintain that throughout
3. Document rationale — note why a coffee received its score, especially for scores above 87 or below 83
4. Blind taste — remove identifying information to reduce expectation bias
5. Compare with peers — regular calibration sessions with other trained cuppers
6. Consider context — adjust expectations based on coffee type, origin, and processing method

## Critical Perspectives

**Supporters argue:**

- Attribute-level scoring provides actionable producer feedback
- International standardisation enables objective trade communication
- Trained cuppers can achieve high inter-judge reliability
- The system has driven significant quality improvement in producing countries

**Critics argue:**

- Cultural bias in what constitutes "quality" (e.g., preference for brightness over earthiness)
- Difficult to achieve meaningful inter-judge reliability without costly calibration programmes
- Score compression makes meaningful differentiation between coffees challenging

## Historical Context

The 100-Point Scale was developed to create consistency in coffee quality assessment across the global specialty coffee industry. Prior to its standardisation, coffee evaluation varied significantly by region and organisation, making quality communication difficult across the supply chain. The system borrowed structural elements from wine evaluation scoring and was adapted to reflect coffee's unique sensory attributes and the [SCA](../specialty-coffee/specialty-coffee-association-sca.md)'s cupping methodology.

## Key Facts

- Scores of 80+ designate specialty grade coffee
- Seven core sensory attributes each scored out of 10
- Sweetness, Clean Cup, and Uniformity each contribute up to 10 points across 5 samples
- Most specialty coffees cluster between 83–88 points
- Scores above 90 are considered outstanding; above 95 are exceptional
- Developed and maintained by the [Specialty Coffee Association (SCA)](../specialty-coffee/specialty-coffee-association-sca.md)
