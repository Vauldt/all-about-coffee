## Overview

Coffee chemistry encompasses the complex system of chemical compounds present in roasted coffee — their origins in the green bean, their transformation during roasting, and their role in the flavour, aroma, body, and physiological effects of the brewed beverage. Green coffee contains over 1,000 identified chemical compounds; roasting transforms many and creates hundreds of new ones through thermal reactions (principally the Maillard reaction and caramelisation), with the roasted bean containing over 800 volatile aroma-active compounds alone. Understanding coffee chemistry is foundational for roasters developing profiles, brewers optimising extraction, and researchers studying coffee's health effects.

## Green Coffee Composition

Before roasting, coffee beans contain the following primary compound classes:

| Compound class | Approximate % dry weight | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Carbohydrates (sucrose, polysaccharides) | 50–60% | Roasting substrate; caramelisation; body |
| Lipids (oils, waxes, diterpenes) | 12–18% | Mouthfeel; oil-carried aromatics; body |
| Proteins and free amino acids | 10–13% | Maillard reaction substrate; nitrogen source |
| Chlorogenic acids (CGAs) | 6–10% (Arabica); 10–14% (Robusta) | Antioxidant; bitterness precursor; astringency |
| Caffeine | 1.2–1.5% (Arabica); 2.7–4% (Robusta) | Stimulant; contributes bitterness |
| Trigonelline | 0.5–1.5% | Roasting substrate; niacin source; contributes bitterness |
| Organic acids | 1–2% | Acidity; flavour |
| Minerals | ~4% | Water interaction; flavour |
| Water | ~8–12% (green) | Moisture content |

## Key Compound Classes

### Chlorogenic Acids (CGAs)

Chlorogenic acids are a family of phenolic compounds that are Arabica's most abundant non-carbohydrate component. They play multiple roles:
- **Antioxidant properties** in green coffee (health significance)
- **Bitterness precursors** in roasting — CGAs degrade into bitter lactone compounds and phenolic acids during Maillard reactions
- **Astringency** in under-extracted or over-extracted brewed coffee
- Robusta has approximately double the CGA content of Arabica — contributing to Robusta's more bitter, harsher cup

### Caffeine

Caffeine is a xanthine alkaloid that:
- Is heat-stable — it does not degrade during roasting at conventional temperatures
- Contributes approximately 10–15% of the perceived bitterness in brewed coffee
- Inhibits adenosine receptors, producing the stimulant effect
- Is more concentrated in Robusta (~3.5%) than Arabica (~1.3%)
- Light roasted coffee does not contain more caffeine than dark — caffeine content per bean is essentially unchanged by roast degree

### Sucrose and Caramelisation

Sucrose comprises approximately 8% of green Arabica and is completely destroyed during roasting — none remains in the final roasted bean. Sucrose undergoes:
- **Caramelisation** (direct thermal degradation) producing caramel, butterscotch, brown sugar compounds
- **Maillard reaction** (with amino acids) producing pyrazines, furans, and other roast-characteristic compounds

### The Maillard Reaction

The Maillard reaction (between amino acids and reducing sugars under heat) is the primary driver of roast flavour and colour development, producing:
- **Pyrazines:** Nutty, roasted, earthy aroma compounds; increase with roast degree
- **Furans:** Caramel, sweet, nutty, fruit-like
- **Thiols:** Extremely potent at very low concentrations; contribute roasted, sulphurous, "coffee-like" character
- **Melanoidins:** Large brown polymers responsible for coffee's dark colour and some body

### Organic Acids

Coffee contains several organic acids that contribute to perceived acidity and complexity:

| Acid | Character | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Citric acid | Citrus, bright | Present in green bean; partially degrades in dark roast |
| Malic acid | Apple, smooth | Contributes pleasant roundness |
| Phosphoric acid | Clean, mineral, "sparkly" | Prominent in Kenyan coffees |
| Acetic acid | Vinegary | Low levels: complexity; high levels: defect |
| Formic acid | Sharp | Contributes to overall acidity |
| Quinic acid | Harsh, bitter | Increases with roast degree; contributes to dark-roast bitterness |
| Chlorogenic acid lactones | Bitter | Formed during roasting from CGAs |

### Lipids

Coffee lipids (cafestol, kahweol, triglycerides, fatty acids) contribute to:
- **Body and mouthfeel** — lipids form the viscous oily phase of the cup
- **Aroma carrier** — volatile aromatics dissolve in coffee oils
- **Health effects** — cafestol and kahweol raise LDL cholesterol; removed by paper filtration

### Volatile Aromatics

Over 800 volatile aroma compounds have been identified in roasted coffee, though only a fraction are present in quantities above the olfactory detection threshold. Key compound classes include:
- **Furans:** Caramel, sweet (e.g. furfural, 2-furfuryl)
- **Pyrazines:** Nutty, earthy, roasty
- **Thiols and sulfur compounds:** Potent "coffee" character at very low concentrations
- **Esters:** Fruity (more prominent in light roast and origin-forward coffees)
- **Aldehydes and ketones:** Caramel, butter, green

## Key Facts

- Green coffee contains 50–60% carbohydrates, 12–18% lipids, 6–10% chlorogenic acids (Arabica), and 1.2–1.5% caffeine
- Roasting destroys sucrose completely; Maillard reaction creates pyrazines, furans, and thiols; caramelisation produces caramel-type compounds
- Over 800 volatile aroma compounds identified in roasted coffee; only a subset present above detection threshold
- Caffeine content per bean is essentially unchanged by roast degree — roast level does not significantly affect caffeine concentration
- Chlorogenic acids are bitterness precursors — their degradation products (lactones, quinic acid) increase with roast degree
- Paper filtration removes diterpenes (cafestol, kahweol) — reducing LDL cholesterol impact of filtered vs. unfiltered coffee
