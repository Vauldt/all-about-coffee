## Overview

A bar spoon is a long-handled stirring and layering implement used in both coffee service and cocktail preparation. In the coffee context, bar spoons are used to stir espresso-based drinks, layer cold coffee cocktails, add sweeteners or flavouring syrups, and perform the controlled pours needed to create layered drinks such as an Irish coffee or a latte with a visible crema layer. The long twisted handle — a defining feature — enables the controlled flow of liquid for layering techniques.

## Design Features

A standard bar spoon has several distinguishing design characteristics:

- **Long handle:** Typically 25–35 cm in length, allowing it to reach the bottom of tall glasses and mixing jugs
- **Twisted shaft:** The spiral twist along the handle serves two purposes — it facilitates the controlled-pour layering technique (liquid flows along the spiral) and improves grip when spinning
- **Small bowl:** The spoon end is smaller than a teaspoon (approximately 5 ml); used for small volume transfers and tasting
- **Flat disc or teardrop end:** The opposite end to the bowl; used as a muddler or for layering (pouring liquid over the flat disc to slow and spread the flow)

Common materials: stainless steel (standard), gold-plated stainless steel (premium), copper (decorative / specialty bar use).

## Use in Coffee Service

### Stirring

Bar spoons are used to stir:
- Espresso macchiato and cortado — integrating small volumes of milk or foam
- Long espresso drinks (Americano, long black) — ensuring even temperature distribution
- Flavoured coffee drinks — incorporating syrups fully into the liquid base

### Layering

Layering is the technique of creating visually distinct strata in a drink by adding liquids of different densities without mixing them. In coffee:
- **Irish coffee** — cream is poured over the back of the bar spoon onto hot coffee, floating a cream layer
- **Cold coffee cocktails** — layers of cold brew, milk, and syrup are separated using the spoon to slow the pour
- **Iced latte layering** — cold milk added beneath espresso for a distinct visual layer

### Measuring and Transferring

Bar spoons are used in some coffee preparation contexts to transfer small amounts of:
- Ground coffee (informal measurement; not recommended over scales)
- Sugar or raw sweeteners
- Flavouring powders (e.g., matcha, cocoa) before integration

## Use in Coffee Cocktails

The bar spoon is a standard implement in coffee cocktail preparation, used in espresso martini service, Irish coffee construction, and any layered or stirred coffee-spirit drink. See [Coffee Cocktails](../../coffee-drinks/coffee-cocktails.md).

## Key Facts

- A bar spoon is a long-handled (25–35 cm) stirring implement with a twisted shaft and small bowl
- The twisted handle facilitates layering technique — liquid flows along the spiral to slow and spread the pour
- The flat disc end is used to pour liquid over to create floating layers (as in Irish coffee cream)
- Standard bar spoons have an approximate bowl capacity of 5 ml — not a reliable measure for specialty coffee dosing
- Stainless steel is the most common material; stainless steel is corrosion-resistant and dishwasher-safe
