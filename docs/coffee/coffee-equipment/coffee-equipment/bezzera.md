## Overview

Bezzera is an Italian espresso machine manufacturer with a claim to one of the most significant events in coffee history: Luigi Bezzera filed the first patent for an espresso machine in 1901, describing a device that used steam pressure to force hot water rapidly through ground coffee. The patent was purchased by Desiderio Pavoni, who commercialised the technology under the La Pavoni brand; however, the Bezzera company continued and today manufactures a range of semi-commercial and prosumer espresso machines. Bezzera occupies a respected position in the specialty coffee equipment market, known for traditional Italian craftsmanship and reliable commercial performance.

## Historical Significance

Luigi Bezzera, a Milanese inventor and businessman, patented the first espresso machine mechanism in 1901 — describing a steam-pressure system for brewing individual cups of coffee rapidly. The key innovation was the use of pressure to speed the brewing process, reducing brew time from minutes to seconds and enabling individual-cup service.

Key historical events:
- **1901:** Luigi Bezzera patents the first espresso machine in Italy
- **1903:** Desiderio Pavoni purchases the patent and begins commercial production under the La Pavoni brand
- **1906:** The espresso machine is publicly demonstrated at the Milan Fair
- **Post-Pavoni:** The Bezzera company continues manufacturing machines under the family name

The 1901 Bezzera machine used steam pressure (up to approximately 1.5 bar), producing a drink different from modern 9-bar pump espresso — more similar to a strong moka-style coffee. The modern 9-bar extraction standard was developed in the 1940s–50s.

## Modern Machine Range

The contemporary Bezzera company produces semi-commercial and prosumer espresso machines:

| Product Line | Segment | Key Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Bezzera Magica | Prosumer | Dual boiler; E61 group; PID temperature control |
| Bezzera Duo | Prosumer / commercial | Dual boiler; volumetric dosing; multi-user |
| Bezzera Matrix | Commercial | Multiple groups; commercial café use |
| Bezzera Aria | Prosumer | Heat exchanger; compact; E61 group |
| Bezzera Unica | Entry commercial | Single boiler; traditional design |

Common technologies across the modern Bezzera range:
- **E61 group head** — the classic thermally stable group head design (introduced 1961 by Faema; now industry-standard)
- **Dual boiler** — separate boilers for brewing and steaming; precise temperature stability
- **PID temperature control** — electronic precision temperature management on upper-tier models
- **Volumetric dosing** — programmable shot volumes for commercial consistency

## Market Position

Bezzera is positioned in the prosumer and lower-commercial segment, competing with [ECM (Espresso Coffee Machines)](../ecm-espresso-coffee-machines.md), [Rocket Espresso](../rocket-espresso.md), [Profitec](../profitec.md), and [Astoria](astoria.md). The brand trades on historical heritage and Italian craftsmanship. Machines are distributed through specialty coffee equipment dealers and used in smaller specialty cafés and by serious home baristas.

## Key Facts

- Luigi Bezzera filed the first espresso machine patent in 1901 — one of the foundational events in espresso history
- The 1901 machine used steam pressure; modern 9-bar pump espresso was standardised in the 1940s–50s
- Desiderio Pavoni purchased Bezzera's 1901 patent and commercialised it under the La Pavoni brand in 1903
- Modern Bezzera machines are positioned in the prosumer and light commercial segment; known for dual boiler and E61 group designs
- Competing brands include ECM, Rocket Espresso, Profitec, and Astoria
