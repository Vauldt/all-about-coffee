## Overview

Briggo was an American automated coffee company founded in Austin, Texas, in 2008, which developed and operated a fully automated robotic coffee kiosk system branded the *Coffee Haus*. The system was designed to prepare barista-quality espresso drinks via a closed robotic environment without any human involvement in the preparation process, positioned explicitly as a quality-first automated offering rather than a vending machine upgrade. Briggo is notable in the robotic barista sector for its early founding date, its emphasis on specialty-grade output, and its high-profile deployment at Austin-Bergstrom International Airport. The company was acquired by CURO in 2021, and its technology and operations were subsequently integrated into CURO's broader automated food and beverage platform.

## Company History

Briggo was founded in 2008 by Charles Studor and Kevin Nater in Austin, Texas — notably earlier than most robotic barista competitors. The company spent several years in research and development before deploying its first commercial Coffee Haus unit.

The Austin-Bergstrom International Airport installation, launched in 2017, became Briggo's flagship deployment and the reference case for its commercial proposition. The airport context was strategically chosen: high foot traffic, extended trading hours, and a captive consumer base with tolerance for novel service formats.

Briggo raised multiple rounds of venture capital through the late 2010s and expanded beyond Austin to additional airport and corporate campus deployments. The company attracted attention for its premium positioning — drinks were priced comparably to specialty café beverages, not at vending machine price points — and its claims of barista-quality output.

In 2021, Briggo was acquired by CURO Group, a technology-focused food and beverage automation company. The acquisition integrated Briggo's robotics technology, software platform, and operational expertise into CURO's portfolio. Post-acquisition branding and product direction evolved under the CURO umbrella.

## The Coffee Haus System

### Enclosure and Format

The Briggo Coffee Haus is a fully enclosed, sealed robotic unit approximately the size of a large vending machine or compact kiosk. Unlike the transparent enclosures used by some competitors, the internal preparation environment is fully enclosed, creating a hygienic sealed brewing space with no direct exposure to the ambient environment. This was marketed as a food-safety and consistency advantage.

The exterior presents a clean consumer interface — touchscreen ordering panel, illuminated branding, and a dispensing slot — with no visible mechanical components during normal operation.

### Brewing Technology

Inside the Coffee Haus, a robotic system manages the complete preparation sequence:

- **Grinding:** Beans are ground to order using an integrated grinder. Briggo emphasised fresh grinding per drink as a quality differentiator from systems using pre-ground coffee.
- **Espresso extraction:** A commercial-grade espresso module extracts shots to programmed parameters. Temperature stability and pre-infusion are controlled electronically.
- **Milk texturing:** Milk is steamed and textured to appropriate temperature and consistency for each drink type using an automated steaming module.
- **Flavour customisation:** An array of flavour pumps dispenses syrups and additions per customer specification.
- **Assembly and delivery:** The completed beverage is dispensed to the customer via a secure pickup slot.

The system is remotely monitored via cloud-connected software, allowing operators to track machine performance, inventory levels, and quality parameters without on-site staffing.

### Ordering Platform

Briggo placed significant emphasis on its digital ordering platform. Customers order via a dedicated mobile application or the kiosk touchscreen. The app retains customer preferences, enabling one-tap reordering of customised drinks. Briggo positioned this as a personalisation capability that matched or exceeded the human barista experience for regular customers.

## Quality Positioning

Briggo was among the most explicit of robotic barista companies in claiming specialty-grade coffee quality. The company's marketing positioned the Coffee Haus as a precision instrument rather than a convenience appliance, with messaging around consistency, fresh grinding, and calibrated extraction.

Independent assessments were generally positive about the quality floor — Coffee Haus output was consistently above vending machine standards and competitive with well-run chain café espresso. The system could not match the ceiling of skilled specialty barista work — real-time sensory calibration, latte art, and the human dimension of the service encounter — but this was not the target proposition.

## Airport and Corporate Deployment Model

Briggo's primary deployment contexts were airports and large corporate campuses, chosen for their combination of:

- **Sustained high traffic** across extended hours
- **Labour constraint** — airport operating hours and campus overnight coverage are difficult and expensive to staff
- **Captive consumers** with limited competing options
- **Tolerance for format novelty** among frequent travellers and tech-industry workers

The Austin-Bergstrom Airport deployment demonstrated sustained commercial viability over multiple years, validating the airport format as a core market for the technology.

## Acquisition by CURO

CURO Group's 2021 acquisition of Briggo reflected consolidation in the robotic food and beverage space. CURO's platform approach — integrating multiple automated food and beverage formats under unified software infrastructure — was complemented by Briggo's coffee-specific hardware and operational expertise. Post-acquisition, the Coffee Haus technology was rebranded and developed further within the CURO portfolio.

## Key Facts

- Founded 2008 by Charles Studor and Kevin Nater in Austin, Texas — among the earliest robotic barista companies
- Flagship product: the *Coffee Haus*, a fully enclosed automated coffee kiosk
- Flagship deployment: Austin-Bergstrom International Airport, launched 2017
- Differentiated by fresh grinding per order, sealed hygienic enclosure, and premium price positioning
- Mobile app with drink personalisation and one-tap reorder
- Acquired by CURO Group in 2021
- Primary deployment contexts: airports and large corporate campuses
