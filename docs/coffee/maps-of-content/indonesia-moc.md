# Indonesia MOC

Indonesia is the world's fourth-largest coffee producer and the origin of one of coffee's most distinctive and influential processing innovations — *Giling Basah* (wet-hulling), the method responsible for Indonesian Arabica's defining profile of heavy body, low acidity, and complex earth and herbs. The country's coffee spans an archipelago of over 17,000 islands, with Arabica produced in the highland zones of northern Sumatra (Aceh/Gayo, Lintong/Mandheling), Sulawesi (Toraja, Enrekang), Java, Flores, Bali, and Papua, while Robusta (approximately 90% of production) dominates the lowlands. Dutch colonial introduction in the 1690s made Java the world's first commercial coffee source outside Yemen; the word *java* as a synonym for coffee derives from this history. This MOC maps Indonesia's coffee knowledge from the national overview through five primary island growing regions and the distinctive processing methods that define Indonesian origin character.

## Varieties

**Heritage:**
Typica (Sumatran selection — adapted 300+ years) | Bourbon (Java estates) | Abyssinia (Sulawesi)

**Disease-resistant modern:**
Catimor / Ateng Super (Aceh; rust-resistant) | S795 (Java, Sulawesi; Kent selection)

**Robusta:** Diverse unimproved seedling populations; some provincial selections in Lampung/South Sumatra

See [Indonesia](../coffee-geography/indonesia.md) for full variety and genetic diversity discussion.

## Processing and Preparation

**Processing:** Wet-Hulling (Giling Basah) (dominant; Indonesian invention; produces heavy body, low acid, earth) | [Washed Process](../coffee-processing/washed-process.md) (specialty Arabica — Aceh, Toraja, Kintamani) | Natural Processing (limited — Flores, Sulawesi specialty)

**Cup profile:** Wet-hulled Sumatra/Sulawesi: heavy body, very low acidity, dark chocolate, tobacco, earth, herbs, forest floor | Washed Gayo: cleaner, complex, stone fruit | Kintamani: citrus, clean, light body
