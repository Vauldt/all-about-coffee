# Weather Damage

## Overview

Weather damage in coffee refers to the physical injury or quality loss sustained by *Coffea* plants or coffee cherries as a result of extreme or unseasonal weather events — principally frost, hail, drought, flooding, and excessive rainfall. Coffee plants, particularly *Coffea arabica*, have narrow climate tolerance ranges and are highly vulnerable to exceptional weather events that fall outside normal seasonal parameters. Weather damage can range from temporary setbacks (flower drop, partial crop loss) to catastrophic and permanent plantation loss (frost-killed trees), with consequential effects on global supply, green coffee prices, and origin availability.

## Types of Weather Damage

### Frost

Frost is the most catastrophic weather event for *Coffea arabica*, which is not frost-tolerant. Even a single frost event at -2°C to -3°C can kill or severely damage Arabica trees:
- **Mild frost (-1 to -2°C):** Leaf damage, flower and cherry abortion; partial crop loss; trees may recover over 1–2 seasons
- **Severe frost (-3°C and below):** Complete defoliation; bark damage; death of branches; tree death if prolonged
- **Recovery time:** Surviving trees may require 2–3 years to return to full productive capacity; replanted trees take 3–5 years to first harvest

**Brazil frost events** are the most globally significant — Brazil's coffee regions (Minas Gerais, São Paulo, Paraná) sit at the southern edge of the Coffee Belt (15–25°S) and are occasionally exposed to polar air masses from Antarctica. Major frost events in Brazil in 1975, 1994, and 2021 caused immediate global coffee price spikes. The 2021 frost significantly damaged the Cerrado Mineiro and Sul de Minas regions.

### Hail

Hail damages coffee cherries on the tree and can strip leaves and break branches:
- Direct physical damage to cherries causes bruising, splitting, and entry points for fungal and bacterial pathogens
- Damaged cherries develop fermentation defects and must be sorted from the harvest
- Severe hail can strip an entire flush of cherries; leaves torn from branches reduce photosynthetic capacity for the following season
- Trees recover from hail more rapidly than frost — usually within one season

### Drought

Extended dry spells beyond the normal dry season cause water stress in coffee plants:
- Mild drought stress: reduced cherry size, thinner beans, potential quality loss
- Severe drought: cherry abortion, leaf drop, permanent damage to root systems
- Coffee plants are relatively drought-tolerant during the dry season (which they naturally experience), but drought during cherry development or flowering is damaging
- Irrigation in regions like Brazil's Cerrado mitigates drought risk

### Excessive Rainfall / Flooding

Too much rain at the wrong time creates different but equally damaging problems:
- **During flowering:** Heavy rain at flowering can wash pollen away, reducing fruit set and causing irregular cherry development
- **During harvest:** Rain during the harvest period prevents sun-drying, forces use of mechanical dryers (increasing cost and energy use), and increases the risk of mould and fermentation defects
- **Flooding:** Saturated soils kill coffee roots (anaerobic root rot); flooding of processing stations can destroy stored green coffee

### Wind

Strong wind (including tropical cyclones in vulnerable regions) can:
- Physically uproot trees or break major branches
- Strip cherries from the tree prematurely before ripening
- Affect regions like Central America (hurricane exposure) and coastal Southeast Asia

## Price Impact of Weather Events

Because Brazil accounts for 35–40% of global coffee supply, weather events in Brazil directly influence global green coffee commodity prices:
- The 2021 Brazil frost and subsequent drought caused ICE Arabica futures to double within months — from ~$1.40/lb to nearly $2.70/lb
- Major frost events (1975, 1994) caused sustained multi-year price rallies
- Buyers, roasters, and traders monitor Brazilian weather forecasts closely during the southern hemisphere winter (July–August frost risk season)

## Key Facts

- Frost is the most catastrophic weather event for *Coffea arabica* — even a single night at -2°C or below can kill trees or cause total crop loss
- Brazil's southern coffee regions (Minas Gerais, São Paulo) are periodically exposed to Antarctic cold fronts; major frost events cause global price spikes
- Hail causes physical cherry damage and fungal entry points; recovery is faster than frost (within one season)
- Drought during cherry development or flowering directly reduces quality and yield; irrigation mitigates risk in flat-terrain regions
- Excessive rainfall during harvest prevents sun-drying, increases mould and fermentation defect risk
- The 2021 Brazil frost and drought caused ICE Arabica futures to nearly double in six months
