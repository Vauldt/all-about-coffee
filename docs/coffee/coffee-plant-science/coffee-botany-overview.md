## Overview

Coffee botany is the branch of plant science concerned with the biology, taxonomy, anatomy, and physiology of *Coffea* — the genus of flowering plants that produces coffee. The genus belongs to the family Rubiaceae and comprises approximately 130 species, of which *Coffea arabica* and *Coffea canephora* (Robusta) account for virtually all commercial production. Coffee plants are woody evergreen shrubs or small trees that bear fruit (the coffee cherry) containing one or two seeds — the coffee beans. Botanical understanding of the coffee plant underpins agronomy, variety development, disease management, and the science of how growing conditions shape flavour potential in the green bean.

## Key Facts

- *Coffea* belongs to the family Rubiaceae; approximately 130 known species, two commercially dominant
- *Coffea arabica* is unique as a naturally occurring tetraploid (allopolyploid hybrid of *C. canephora* × *C. eugenioides*)
- Coffee plants are woody perennial shrubs flowering in synchrony with rain; cherries take approximately nine months to develop from flower to ripe fruit
- The coffee cherry contains two seeds (occasionally one peaberry); the seed = the green coffee bean
- All wild *Coffea* genetic diversity is native to tropical Africa and Madagascar
