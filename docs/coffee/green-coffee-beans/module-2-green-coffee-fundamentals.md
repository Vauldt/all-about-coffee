## Overview

Module 2 covers the fundamentals of green coffee — the unroasted coffee seed from harvest through processing and grading. Understanding green coffee is essential for roasters, buyers, and anyone working upstream in the specialty supply chain. This module covers the biology of the coffee plant, fruit structure, processing methods, quality grading, and the key factors that differentiate specialty from commodity green coffee.

## Module Content

### 2.1 — The Coffee Plant and Cherry

Coffee originates from the *Coffea* genus of plants. Understanding the plant's biology, growing conditions, and fruit structure is the foundation of green coffee knowledge.

- **The coffee plant:** [Coffea Genus](../coffee-plant-science/coffea-genus.md) — species, morphology, growing conditions
- **The coffee cherry:** [Coffee Cherry](coffee-cherry.md) — fruit layers, seed development, peaberry
- **Cultivation:** [Beans, plants & agriculture](beans-plants-and-agriculture.md) — agriculture, shade growing, harvesting methods
- **Species:** [Arabica](../coffee-varieties/arabica.md) and [Canephora](../coffee-varieties/canephora.md) — the two dominant commercial species

### 2.2 — Terroir and Origin Factors

The flavour potential of green coffee is shaped by environmental factors before any post-harvest intervention:

- **Altitude:** [Terroir Factors Altitude](../terroir/terroir-factors-altitude-2.md) — higher altitude; slower development; denser bean; more complex flavour
- **Climate:** [Terroir Factors Climate and Latitude](../terroir/terroir-factors-climate-and-latitude-2.md) — rainfall, temperature, seasons
- **Soil:** [Terroir Factors Soil](../terroir/terroir-factors-soil-2.md) — volcanic soils, drainage, mineral content
- **Origin profiles:** [Brazilian-Terroir-Profile](../terroir/brazilian-terroir-profile-2.md) | [Saudi Arabia](../coffee-geography/saudi-arabia.md) | [Coffee Origin MOC](../coffee-origin-moc.md)

### 2.3 — Processing Methods

Post-harvest processing determines how the seed is separated from the fruit and dried — and profoundly influences cup character:

- **Washed (wet) process:** [Wet Process](../coffee-processing/wet-process.md) — removes all fruit before drying; clean, origin-transparent cup
- **Natural (dry) process:** [Natural Process](../coffee-processing/natural-process.md) — whole cherry dried; fruity, complex cup
- **Honey process:** [Processing](../coffee-processing/processing.md) — partial mucilage retained; intermediate character
- **Terroir and processing:** [Terroir and Processing](../terroir/terroir-and-processing.md) — how processing interacts with origin factors

### 2.4 — Green Coffee Quality and Grading

Quality assessment of green coffee involves physical inspection and cupping evaluation:

- **Defects:** [Defects & Faults](defects-and-faults.md) — physical defects that affect cup quality; SCA grading categories
- **Cupping for green evaluation:** [Cupping](../tasting-coffee/cupping.md) — standardised evaluation protocol
- **Specialty vs. commodity:** [Specialty Coffee](../specialty-coffee/specialty-coffee.md) — SCA score ≥ 80; traceability; no category 1 defects

### 2.5 — Varietals and Cultivars

The coffee variety planted is a key terroir and flavour factor:

- **Cultivar overview:** [Cultivar](../coffee-varieties/cultivar.md) — what cultivars are; selection and development
- **Catuaí:** Catuaí — widely planted compact hybrid; common in Central and South America
- **Coffee varieties:** [Coffee Origin MOC](../coffee-origin-moc.md) for links to individual variety articles

## Key Facts

- Green coffee quality is determined before roasting — altitude, climate, soil, harvesting precision, and processing all set the flavour ceiling
- Washed processing produces clean, origin-transparent cups; natural processing produces fruity, complex cups; honey processing sits between
- SCA specialty grade requires a cupping score ≥ 80 and no Category 1 defects in the green sample
- Arabica is the dominant specialty species (60–70% of global production); grown at 600–2,200 m altitude
