# How to Make a Flat White

A flat white is made by combining espresso with a smaller amount of steamed milk than a latte, giving it a stronger coffee flavour and a thin, velvety layer of microfoam. A common guide is about 1 part espresso to 2 parts milk, often served in a smaller 5 to 8 ounce cup.

## Basic ingredients

- 2 shots of espresso, or a strong concentrated coffee
- About 4 to 5 ounces of milk
- Optional sweetener, though many people drink it unsweetened

## Method with an espresso machine

1. **Brew the espresso.** Pull a double espresso into a small cup. See [BBC Good Food's flat white recipe](https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/flat-white), [Lavazza's guide](https://www.lavazza.com.au/en/coffee-secrets/how-to-make-flat-white-coffee), and [Starbucks At Home](https://athome.starbucks.com/recipe/flat-white).
2. **Steam the milk for microfoam.** Steam the milk so it becomes silky and glossy, with very small bubbles rather than thick foam. See [Five Senses Coffee](https://fivesenses.com.au/blogs/news/the-truth-about-the-flat-white), [Barista Courses Australia](https://baristacoursesaustralia.com.au/blog/how-to-make-a-flat-white/), and this [basic coffee skills video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SbrLUjBe8tI).
3. **Keep the foam thin.** A flat white should have only a thin layer of foam, not the thicker cap you would expect on a cappuccino. See [basic coffee skills video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SbrLUjBe8tI) and [Five Senses Coffee](https://fivesenses.com.au/blogs/news/the-truth-about-the-flat-white).
4. **Pour the milk into the espresso.** Pour steadily so the milk integrates smoothly with the espresso and leaves a glossy surface. See [BBC Good Food's flat white recipe](https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/flat-white) and [Starbucks At Home](https://athome.starbucks.com/recipe/flat-white).

## Method without an espresso machine

1. **Make strong coffee.** Use a moka pot, AeroPress, or another concentrated brew method as an espresso substitute. See [Methodical Coffee](https://methodicalcoffee.com/blogs/coffee-culture/what-is-a-flat-white-and-how-do-you-make-one) and [Kev's Guide](https://coffeeblog.co.uk/what-is-a-flat-white-how-to-make-at-home/).
2. **Heat the milk.** Warm the milk until hot but not boiling. See [Methodical Coffee](https://methodicalcoffee.com/blogs/coffee-culture/what-is-a-flat-white-and-how-do-you-make-one).
3. **Create a silky texture.** Froth lightly with a handheld frother, whisk, or French press so the milk becomes smooth rather than very foamy. See [Methodical Coffee](https://methodicalcoffee.com/blogs/coffee-culture/what-is-a-flat-white-and-how-do-you-make-one).
4. **Combine and serve.** Pour the milk into the coffee and aim for a smooth, flat surface with only a little foam on top. See [The Cookie Rookie](https://www.thecookierookie.com/flat-white/) and [Kev's Guide](https://coffeeblog.co.uk/what-is-a-flat-white-how-to-make-at-home/).

## Quick tips

- Whole milk usually gives the best microfoam and the creamiest texture. See [Methodical Coffee](https://methodicalcoffee.com/blogs/coffee-culture/what-is-a-flat-white-and-how-do-you-make-one) and [Barista Courses Australia](https://baristacoursesaustralia.com.au/blog/flat-white-recipe/).
- Keep the milk temperature moderate, around 60 to 65 degrees Celsius, to avoid scalding and preserve sweetness. See [Five Senses Coffee](https://fivesenses.com.au/blogs/news/the-truth-about-the-flat-white) and this [barista training video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QCTmjLFhu-U).
- If you want a more traditional flat white style, use less milk than a latte and focus on texture rather than lots of foam. See [Lavazza's guide](https://www.lavazza.com.au/en/coffee-secrets/how-to-make-flat-white-coffee), [Kev's Guide](https://coffeeblog.co.uk/what-is-a-flat-white-how-to-make-at-home/), and this [basic coffee skills video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SbrLUjBe8tI).
