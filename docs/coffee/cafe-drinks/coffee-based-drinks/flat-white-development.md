# Flat White Development

## Overview

The flat white is a milk-based espresso beverage consisting of a double ristretto or double espresso base topped with velvety microfoamed milk, typically served in a 150–180 ml ceramic cup. Its higher coffee-to-milk ratio relative to a latte and its characteristically thin layer of integrated microfoam distinguish it from both the latte and the cappuccino. The drink originated in Australia or New Zealand — the precise origin is disputed by both countries — and has since spread globally to become a standard offering in specialty and mainstream café markets worldwide.

## Definition and Characteristics

A flat white is typically defined by the following parameters:

- **Volume:** 150–180 ml, served in a ceramic cup
- **Coffee base:** double ristretto or double espresso; the ristretto (a shorter extraction than a standard espresso shot) yields a sweeter, less bitter flavour profile
- **Milk:** steamed to produce velvety microfoam — fine-textured, silky milk with minimal surface foam; the microfoam integrates with the espresso rather than sitting on top
- **Temperature:** typically served at 65–68°C
- **Ratio:** higher coffee-to-milk ratio than a latte; lower foam volume than a cappuccino

The defining characteristic of the flat white is the quality of its microfoam. The milk must be steamed to a smooth, paint-like texture without large bubbles or a thick layer of stiff foam. This requirement drove significant development of milk-steaming technique in Australia and New Zealand, where baristas regard milk texturing as a core professional skill.

## The Origin Dispute

The flat white's origin is one of the most debated questions in Australasian café culture. Both Australia and New Zealand lay claim to having invented the drink, and neither claim has been definitively settled.

### The Australian Claim

Australian accounts most commonly attribute the flat white's creation to Sydney cafés in the early 1980s. One frequently cited account credits café owner Alan Preston of Moors Espresso Bar in Sydney, where the drink reportedly appeared on the menu in 1985. Other Australian sources place the flat white's emergence in Melbourne during the same era.

### The New Zealand Claim

New Zealand sources trace the flat white to Wellington, with accounts pointing to Fraser McInnes — a barista working at a Wellington café — as having named the drink in 1989 after an unsuccessful attempt to produce a cappuccino with low-fat milk produced an insufficiently foamy result. Other New Zealand accounts name different venues and individuals.

Neither origin story has been supported by conclusive documentary evidence, and the debate continues in a largely good-natured spirit between the two countries.

## Technical Significance

The flat white's requirements placed unusual demands on baristas at a time when espresso preparation in most markets was far less precise. To produce a consistent flat white, a barista must be able to:

- Extract a ristretto or double shot with precision
- Steam milk to a specific microfoam texture without over-aerating
- Pour the milk in a way that integrates it with the espresso

These demands accelerated the development of milk-steaming pedagogy and latte art technique in Australian and New Zealand café training. The skills developed in this context contributed to the international reputation of Australasian baristas and were exported globally as the [Australian Coffee Culture](../../around-the-world/australia/australian-coffee-culture.md) spread during the 2000s and 2010s.

## Global Spread

The flat white remained primarily an Australia and New Zealand phenomenon until the late 2000s. It began appearing on menus in the United Kingdom from approximately 2005, carried by Australian and New Zealand baristas who opened cafés in London and other British cities. Starbucks added the flat white to its UK menu in 2010 and to its United States menu in 2015 — the latter representing a watershed moment for mainstream recognition. Following Starbucks' US introduction, the flat white became a standard menu item across specialty and commercial café chains in North America.

The drink continues to evolve in specialty cafés, with variations including single-origin espresso bases, plant-based milk alternatives, and adjustments to serve temperature and volume.

## Key Facts

- Typically served in a 150–180 ml ceramic cup
- Based on a double ristretto or double espresso
- Microfoam must be silky and well-integrated — not stiff or thick
- Typically served at 65–68°C
- Origin disputed between Australia (circa early 1980s) and New Zealand (circa 1989)
- Entered the UK specialty market from approximately 2005
- Starbucks added flat white to US menu in 2015
- Higher coffee-to-milk ratio than a latte; less foam than a cappuccino
