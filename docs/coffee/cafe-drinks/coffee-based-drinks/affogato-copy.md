## Overview

An affogato (Italian: *affogato al caffè*, "drowned in coffee") is an Italian dessert consisting of a shot of hot espresso poured over a scoop of gelato or ice cream. The name refers to the gelato being submerged — drowned — by the coffee. In specialty coffee contexts, affogato occupies the intersection of espresso service and dessert, valued for the sensory contrast between hot and cold, bitter and sweet.

## Composition

A classic affogato uses two components:

| Component | Standard Specification |
| :--- | :--- |
| Gelato or ice cream | 80–100 g, traditionally vanilla (*fior di latte*) or a neutral dairy base |
| Espresso | One freshly pulled single or double shot, poured immediately |

The espresso is poured over the gelato at the point of service — not in advance — so that the contrast between the hot shot and the cold gelato is experienced at the table. The gelato partially melts on contact, producing a mixture that transitions from distinct layers into a unified, creamy coffee dessert.

## Sensory Characteristics

The affogato's appeal relies on layered contrasts:

- **Temperature contrast:** Hot espresso against frozen gelato creates an immediate sensory shift
- **Texture contrast:** The liquid espresso melts the solid gelato surface, producing a range of textures as the dessert is consumed
- **Flavour contrast:** Espresso bitterness and acidity are softened by the fat and sweetness of the gelato
- **Crema:** The espresso crema floats briefly on the gelato surface before integrating, contributing to aroma

## Variations

The classic vanilla affogato is the most common form, but variations include:

- **Gelato flavours:** Pistachio, chocolate, hazelnut, and caramel are frequently used in Italian cafés; lighter floral or fruit-based gelato works well with fruity espresso profiles
- **Liqueur additions:** Amaretto, coffee liqueur (*Kahlúa*, *Tia Maria*), hazelnut liqueur, or grappa are common additions in restaurant settings
- **Toppings:** Crushed biscotti, toasted nuts, or chocolate shavings are sometimes added

## Espresso Selection

The espresso used in an affogato should be calibrated for the pairing context. Chocolate-forward, medium-bodied espressos with moderate acidity integrate well with vanilla dairy bases. Very high-acidity or extremely light espressos can clash with full-fat gelato, producing a thin or sour flavour contrast rather than a complementary one.

Specialty cafés sometimes use single-origin espresso to create intentional flavour pairings — for example, a fruity natural Ethiopian espresso paired with a neutral dairy gelato to produce a fruit-forward, dessert-style cup.

## Service Notes

- Gelato should be scooped to order and served in a chilled glass to slow initial melting before the espresso is poured
- The espresso shot must be pulled immediately before pouring; stale shots introduce flat, bitter notes
- Affogato is consumed with a spoon, transitioning to sipping as the gelato melts fully

## Key Facts

- Affogato means "drowned" in Italian, referring to the gelato being submerged in espresso
- The classic preparation uses vanilla gelato and a single or double shot of espresso
- The dessert relies on contrasts: hot versus cold, bitter versus sweet, liquid versus semi-solid
- Espresso must be poured immediately after pulling; delayed pouring produces inferior results
- Higher-fat gelato bases (vanilla, neutral dairy) complement most espresso profiles better than fruit-based alternatives
