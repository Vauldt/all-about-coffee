## Overview

An Americano (also *caffè Americano*) is an espresso-based drink made by adding hot water to one or more espresso shots, producing a beverage with a strength similar to filter coffee while retaining the aromatic compounds and crema characteristic of espresso extraction. The name derives from American soldiers stationed in Italy during World War II who reportedly diluted local espresso to approximate the lighter drip coffee they were accustomed to.

## Preparation

The standard method is to pull an espresso shot into the serving vessel and then add hot water on top. This preserves the crema layer, which collects on the surface of the finished drink.

**Typical preparation:**
- Pull a single or double espresso shot (approximately 30–60 ml)
- Add hot water (approximately 90–180 ml) to achieve the desired strength
- Total volume: typically 150–240 ml depending on size

**Common ratios (espresso to water):**

| Ratio | Character |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1:2 | Strong, espresso character clearly evident |
| 1:3 | Balanced; standard café size |
| 1:4 | Lighter; approaches filter coffee strength |

## Americano vs. Long Black

The Americano and [Long Black](../../coffee-brewing-espresso/long-black.md) are closely related but differ in assembly order:

| Method | Order | Crema | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Americano | Espresso poured into water, OR water added to espresso | Crema partially dissipates | Water added to espresso is most common in the US and UK |
| Long Black | Hot water poured first, espresso added on top | Crema intact on surface | Standard in Australia and New Zealand |

The long black method (water first) preserves the crema more completely, as the espresso is gently floated on top of the water rather than being diluted by water poured directly over it.

## Distinguishing Characteristics

- **Flavour:** Retains espresso's roast-driven aromatics and body; differs from drip coffee in extraction chemistry and mouthfeel
- **Crema:** Some crema visible on the surface (more pronounced in long black variant)
- **Strength:** Variable by recipe; typically equivalent to or slightly stronger than filter coffee by volume

## Key Facts

- An Americano is espresso diluted with hot water to produce a filter-coffee-strength drink
- The name derives from World War II-era American soldiers diluting Italian espresso
- Standard ratios range from 1:2 to 1:4 espresso to water
- The long black (water first, espresso second) is the Australian/New Zealand variant; it preserves crema more effectively
- An Americano differs from drip coffee in extraction chemistry — it retains espresso's aromatic compounds despite similar strength
