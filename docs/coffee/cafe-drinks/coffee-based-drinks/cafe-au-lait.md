# How to Make a Café au Lait

A café au lait is a French-style coffee made with equal parts strong brewed coffee and hot milk. Unlike a latte, it is traditionally made with brewed coffee rather than espresso, and it usually has little to no foam.

## Basic ingredients

- Strong brewed coffee, often filter coffee, French press, or moka pot coffee
- Hot milk
- Optional sugar

## Basic recipe

- About 1 part strong coffee
- About 1 part hot milk
- Little to no foam

## Method

1. **Brew strong coffee.** Make a full-flavoured brewed coffee using a drip brewer, French press, or moka pot. See [CoffeeGeek](https://coffeegeek.com/guides/howtos/cafe-au-lait-how-to/), [BBC Good Food](https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/cafe-au-lait), and [Lifeboost Coffee](https://lifeboostcoffee.com/blogs/lifeboost/what-is-cafe-au-lait-simple-diy-homemade-recipe).
2. **Heat the milk.** Warm the milk until hot but not boiling. Traditional methods often use scalded or gently heated milk rather than heavily foamed milk. See [The Spruce Eats](https://www.thespruceeats.com/cafe-au-lait-recipe-1374920), [One Great Coffee](https://onegreatcoffee.com/blogs/latest-news/what-is-cafe-au-lait-how-do-you-make-it), and this [cafe au lait video guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pr7xA80xpYY).
3. **Keep foam minimal.** A classic café au lait is smooth and milky, but not topped with a thick foam cap. See [Lifeboost Coffee](https://lifeboostcoffee.com/blogs/lifeboost/what-is-cafe-au-lait-simple-diy-homemade-recipe), [Coffee Annan](https://coffeeannan.com/en/blogs/blog/cafe-au-lait), and [CoffeeGeek history note](https://coffeegeek.com/blog/history/the-history-of-the-cafe-au-lait-as-i-see-it/).
4. **Combine equal parts coffee and milk.** Pour the coffee and hot milk together in roughly equal amounts into a mug or bowl. See [BBC Good Food](https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/cafe-au-lait), [The Spruce Eats](https://www.thespruceeats.com/cafe-au-lait-recipe-1374920), [Coffee Annan](https://coffeeannan.com/en/blogs/blog/cafe-au-lait), and [Starbucks At Home](https://www.starbucksathome.com/au/recipes/cafe-au-lait).
5. **Serve immediately.** Café au lait is often served hot in a mug or a traditional bowl. See [CoffeeGeek](https://coffeegeek.com/guides/howtos/cafe-au-lait-how-to/), [Lifeboost Coffee](https://lifeboostcoffee.com/blogs/lifeboost/what-is-cafe-au-lait-simple-diy-homemade-recipe), and [CoffeeGeek history note](https://coffeegeek.com/blog/history/the-history-of-the-cafe-au-lait-as-i-see-it/).

## Method without special equipment

1. **Use any strong brewed coffee method.** A French press, stovetop moka pot, or drip brewer all work well. See [Lifeboost Coffee](https://lifeboostcoffee.com/blogs/lifeboost/what-is-cafe-au-lait-simple-diy-homemade-recipe), [Coffee Annan](https://coffeeannan.com/en/blogs/blog/cafe-au-lait), and [Cartograph Coffee](https://cartographcoffee.com/blogs/news/how-to-make-cafe-au-lait).
2. **Warm the milk on the stove or in the microwave.** Heat it until very hot, but stop before it boils. See [One Great Coffee](https://onegreatcoffee.com/blogs/latest-news/what-is-cafe-au-lait-how-do-you-make-it), [Lifeboost Coffee](https://lifeboostcoffee.com/blogs/lifeboost/what-is-cafe-au-lait-simple-diy-homemade-recipe), and [Cartograph Coffee](https://cartographcoffee.com/blogs/news/how-to-make-cafe-au-lait).
3. **Optionally froth very lightly.** If you want a smoother texture, a handheld frother can add a little body, but avoid making thick foam. See [Cartograph Coffee](https://cartographcoffee.com/blogs/news/how-to-make-cafe-au-lait) and [Lifeboost Coffee](https://lifeboostcoffee.com/blogs/lifeboost/what-is-cafe-au-lait-simple-diy-homemade-recipe).
4. **Pour and enjoy.** Combine the coffee and milk in equal parts and drink while hot. See [BBC Good Food](https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/cafe-au-lait), [The Spruce Eats](https://www.thespruceeats.com/cafe-au-lait-recipe-1374920), and [Starbucks At Home](https://www.starbucksathome.com/au/recipes/cafe-au-lait).

## Quick tips

- Café au lait is milder than black coffee, but usually stronger-tasting than a latte because it uses brewed coffee rather than espresso with lots of milk. See [The Spruce Eats](https://www.thespruceeats.com/cafe-au-lait-recipe-1374920), [Lifeboost Coffee](https://lifeboostcoffee.com/blogs/lifeboost/what-is-cafe-au-lait-simple-diy-homemade-recipe), and [CoffeeGeek history note](https://coffeegeek.com/blog/history/the-history-of-the-cafe-au-lait-as-i-see-it/).
- Whole milk is the most traditional choice, though oat and other milk alternatives can also work. See [Coffee Annan](https://coffeeannan.com/en/blogs/blog/cafe-au-lait), [One Great Coffee](https://onegreatcoffee.com/blogs/latest-news/what-is-cafe-au-lait-how-do-you-make-it), and [Lifeboost Coffee](https://lifeboostcoffee.com/blogs/lifeboost/what-is-cafe-au-lait-simple-diy-homemade-recipe).
- If you want a more traditional French feel, serve it in a bowl rather than a cup. See [CoffeeGeek](https://coffeegeek.com/guides/howtos/cafe-au-lait-how-to/), [Lifeboost Coffee](https://lifeboostcoffee.com/blogs/lifeboost/what-is-cafe-au-lait-simple-diy-homemade-recipe), and [One Great Coffee](https://onegreatcoffee.com/blogs/latest-news/what-is-cafe-au-lait-how-do-you-make-it).
