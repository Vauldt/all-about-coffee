## Overview

Filter coffee, as a café drink, is brewed coffee produced by passing hot water through ground coffee held in a paper, metal, or cloth filter medium. It is served black or with milk and is the dominant coffee beverage format in North America and Northern Europe. As a café offering, filter coffee includes both manually brewed pour-over preparations and automated batch-brewed black coffee. Full treatment of the brewing science and technique is covered in Filter Coffee.

## Basic Parameters

A typical filter coffee recipe uses a brew ratio of approximately 1:15 to 1:17 (coffee to water by weight), with water temperature of 90–96 °C, producing a beverage TDS of 1.2–1.5% at an extraction yield of 18–22%. Grind size is medium to medium-coarse depending on the specific brewer and recipe.

## Key Facts

- Filter coffee is brewed by passing hot water through a filter medium that separates the grounds from the beverage
- Common café formats: manual pour-over (V60, Chemex, Kalita) and batch brew
- Standard brew ratio approximately 1:15–1:17; water temperature 90–96 °C
- Paper filtration produces higher clarity and lighter body than metal or unfiltered methods
