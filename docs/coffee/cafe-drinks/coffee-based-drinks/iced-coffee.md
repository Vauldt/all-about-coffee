# Iced Coffee

## Overview

Iced coffee is a broad category of cold coffee drinks made by combining brewed coffee or espresso with ice, and optionally milk or sweetener. It is one of the most widely consumed coffee formats globally, offered in cafés and prepared at home across a wide range of styles and base brew methods. The defining characteristic is chilled serving temperature rather than any single preparation technique.

## Preparation Methods

### Espresso-Based Iced Coffee

The most common café preparation uses one to two shots of espresso poured over a glass filled with ice. Because ice dilutes the drink as it melts, the espresso is typically pulled at standard strength or slightly concentrated to maintain flavour balance. Cold milk, cream, or plant-based milk may be added. Sweetening with simple syrup or flavoured syrup before pouring integrates more evenly than adding sugar directly to a cold drink.

For improved clarity and reduced dilution, some preparations chill the brewed coffee before pouring it over ice rather than adding hot coffee directly to the glass.

### Filter Coffee–Based Iced Coffee

Strong-brewed filter coffee — drip, French press, or AeroPress — serves as a lower-intensity base. The coffee is brewed at a higher ratio than usual to compensate for ice dilution, then cooled before serving. This method is accessible without espresso equipment and produces a lighter-bodied result.

### Instant Coffee Method

Instant coffee dissolved in a small volume of warm water, then poured over ice with cold milk, is a common home preparation. The instant coffee should be dissolved fully in hot liquid before chilling, as it does not dissolve readily in cold water. Sweetener added at the dissolving stage integrates more effectively.

### Flash-Chilled Coffee

Flash chilling — brewing hot coffee directly onto ice — is a rapid preparation used in both home and professional contexts. It produces a bright, clean cup and is the basis of [Japanese Iced Coffee](../../coffee-brewing/japanese-iced-coffee.md). The ice volume is factored into the total water weight for the brew recipe to avoid over-dilution.

## Serving Variations

Coffee ice cubes, made by freezing brewed coffee, prevent flavour dilution as the drink melts and are used in both home and café settings. Milk foam, whipped cream, or flavoured syrups are common additions in café versions. Sweetened condensed milk is traditional in Southeast Asian iced coffee styles.

## Key Facts

- Standard café preparation: one to two espresso shots over ice, with or without milk
- Espresso concentration: typically standard extraction; no modification required if ice ratio is accounted for
- Common ratios for filter-based iced coffee: brew at approximately 1.5× the usual coffee-to-water ratio to offset ice dilution
- Serving temperature: below 4 °C is standard for quality retention
- Distinct from Cold Brew Coffee, which is steeped in cold water over 12–24 hours without heat
