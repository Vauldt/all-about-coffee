## Overview

A cappuccino is an espresso-based milk drink traditionally composed of equal parts espresso, steamed milk, and milk foam, typically served in a 150–200 ml cup. It is characterised by a prominent foam cap and a drier, more aerated texture than a caffè latte, with the higher milk foam ratio producing a lighter mouthfeel and more pronounced espresso flavour. The cappuccino is one of the foundational drinks of Italian espresso culture and remains among the most widely served espresso drinks globally.

## Key Facts

- Traditional composition: equal parts espresso (~30 ml), steamed milk, and milk foam in a 150–200 ml cup
- The distinct foam layer is the defining characteristic distinguishing the cappuccino from a caffè latte
- Drier mouthfeel than a latte due to higher foam proportion; more intense espresso character
- Traditionally served in the morning in Italian café culture; not typically consumed after midday
- Modern specialty interpretations often use a thinner, more integrated microfoam while retaining the ratio
