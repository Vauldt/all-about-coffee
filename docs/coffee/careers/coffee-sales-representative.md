# Coffee Sales Representative / Business Development Manager

A **Coffee Sales Representative** or **Coffee Business Development Manager (BDM)** drives revenue growth by acquiring new wholesale, retail, or hospitality accounts and nurturing existing client relationships. In the coffee industry, these roles are typically associated with roasters and equipment suppliers who sell to cafés, restaurants, hotels, and offices.

## Role Overview

Sales and business development roles in coffee are relationship-intensive, requiring both product expertise and commercial skill. A BDM at a specialty roaster might manage a portfolio of café accounts — ensuring those businesses receive consistent supply, technical support, and training — while simultaneously identifying and onboarding new wholesale partners. At equipment companies, the role often involves specifying, demonstrating, and supporting installation alongside the sales process.

## Key Responsibilities

- Prospecting, qualifying, and acquiring new wholesale and commercial accounts
- Managing an existing client portfolio — maintaining relationships, resolving issues, and driving retention
- Presenting coffee products, brewing programmes, and equipment solutions to prospective clients
- Negotiating supply agreements, pricing structures, and contract terms
- Coordinating with operations, roasting, and training teams to deliver onboarding for new accounts
- Conducting or arranging equipment installations, barista training, and technical callouts
- Monitoring market trends and competitor activity to identify growth opportunities
- Reporting on pipeline, conversion rates, and account performance using CRM tools

## Skills and Knowledge

- Solid foundational coffee knowledge — espresso, brewing methods, [Coffee Roasting](../coffee-roasting/coffee-roasting.md), and product differentiation
- Understanding of café operations, labour costs, and the economics of a coffee programme
- Strong interpersonal and negotiation skills
- CRM proficiency (Salesforce, HubSpot, or industry-specific tools)
- Ability to conduct compelling product demonstrations and tastings
- Familiarity with major coffee equipment brands such as [La Marzocco](https://www.lamarzocco.com), [Victoria Arduino](https://www.victorioarduino.com), and [Mahlkönig](https://www.mahlkoenig.com)

## Qualifications and Pathways

- Background in hospitality, barista work, or café management provides strong product credibility
- Sales or business development experience from food and beverage or FMCG sectors
- Degree in Business, Marketing, or Hospitality Management (advantageous)
- [SCA Barista Skills](https://sca.coffee/education) or [Brewing](https://sca.coffee/education) certification supports client-facing product conversations
- Q Grader or sensory training is beneficial for specialty-focused roles

## Account Types

Coffee sales representatives typically target a range of account types:

| Account Type | Key Selling Points |
|---|---|
| Independent Cafés | Quality differentiation, barista support, training |
| Restaurant Groups | Menu integration, consistency, volume pricing |
| Hotels & Hospitality | White-label options, equipment packages, staff training |
| Office / Workplace | Convenience formats, bean-to-cup solutions, subscription supply |
| Specialty Retailers | Retail packaging, branded display, online wholesale |

## Industry Context

The wholesale coffee market is competitive, particularly in mature specialty markets like Australia, the UK, and Scandinavia. Roasters increasingly differentiate through service offerings — equipment loans, regular calibration visits, and ongoing training — creating a bundled value proposition beyond the coffee itself. This model requires BDMs who can articulate not just coffee quality but the full support ecosystem. The growth of [Direct Trade](../specialty-coffee/direct-trade.md) sourcing has also given sales staff compelling origin narratives to use with clients.
