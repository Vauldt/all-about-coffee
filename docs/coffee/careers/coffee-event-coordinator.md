# Coffee Competition Organiser / Event Coordinator

A **Coffee Competition Organiser** or **Coffee Event Coordinator** plans, manages, and executes coffee competitions and industry events — from national barista championships to trade expos, cupping competitions, and festival-format consumer experiences. This role requires a blend of project management, coffee industry knowledge, and logistics expertise.

## Role Overview

Coffee competitions are a central pillar of the specialty coffee industry's quality culture. Organised at national and international levels under bodies such as [World Coffee Events (WCE)](https://worldcoffeeevents.org) and national host associations such as the [Specialty Coffee Association of Australia (SCAA)](https://www.scaa.org.au), these events require meticulous coordination across judges, competitors, sponsors, venues, and media. Organisers may work directly for a national coffee association, as independent event producers, or within trade expo companies.

## Key Responsibilities

- Managing the full event lifecycle from concept and budgeting through to execution and post-event debrief
- Coordinating competitor registration, briefing documents, and scheduling
- Recruiting, briefing, and managing judging panels (sensory and head judges)
- Liaising with equipment sponsors for competition floor setup and technical specifications
- Sourcing venues and coordinating with AV, catering, and logistics suppliers
- Ensuring adherence to [WCE competition rules and formats](https://worldcoffeeevents.org/competition-rules)
- Managing volunteer and staff rosters across multi-day events
- Coordinating live streaming, media access, and social media coverage
- Compiling post-event reports, scoresheets, and competitor feedback

## Competition Formats

The [World Coffee Events](https://worldcoffeeevents.org) umbrella includes multiple competition disciplines, each with distinct logistical requirements:

| Competition | Format Summary |
|---|---|
| [World Barista Championship (WBC)](https://worldbaristachampionship.org) | 15-minute espresso, milk, and signature beverage routine |
| [World Brewers Cup (WBrC)](https://worldbrewerscup.org) | Filter brewing — compulsory and open service rounds |
| [World Cup Tasters Championship](https://worldcoffeeevents.org/competitions/world-cup-tasters-championship/) | Speed triangle-test sensory identification |
| [World Latte Art Championship](https://worldcoffeeevents.org/competitions/world-latte-art-championship/) | Free-pour and designer latte art patterns |
| [World Coffee in Good Spirits Championship](https://worldcoffeeevents.org/competitions/world-coffee-in-good-spirits-championship/) | Coffee and alcohol beverage pairings |
| [World Cezve/Ibrik Championship](https://worldcoffeeevents.org/competitions/world-cezve-ibrik-championship/) | Traditional Turkish-style coffee preparation |

## Skills and Knowledge

- Event and project management — timeline planning, budget control, risk management
- Strong understanding of coffee competition rules and judging criteria
- Relationship management with sponsors, national associations, and WCE
- Venue and supplier procurement and contract negotiation
- Volunteer coordination and team leadership under pressure
- Familiarity with competition equipment from brands such as [La Marzocco](https://www.lamarzocco.com), [Mahlkönig](https://www.mahlkoenig.com), and [Nuova Simonelli](https://www.nuovasimonelli.it)

## Qualifications and Pathways

There is no single qualification specific to this role. Practitioners typically combine:

- Event management experience from hospitality, conferences, or festivals
- Active involvement in the specialty coffee community as a competitor, judge, or volunteer
- [SCA membership](https://sca.coffee/membership) and familiarity with WCE governance structures
- Certification as a [WCE Certified Judge](https://worldcoffeeevents.org/judging/) is highly advantageous
- Certificate or degree in Event Management, Hospitality, or Business

## Industry Context

Australia has a strong national competition infrastructure under the [Specialty Coffee Association of Australia (SCAA)](https://www.scaa.org.au), which hosts national qualifying rounds for WBC, WBrC, and other WCE disciplines. Major trade events such as the [Melbourne International Coffee Expo (MICE)](https://internationalcoffeeexpo.com.au) and [World of Coffee](https://worldofcoffee.org) — held annually in a rotating European city — represent the largest-scale events in the calendar. These flagship expos combine competition stages with trade exhibitions, education programmes, and consumer activations.
