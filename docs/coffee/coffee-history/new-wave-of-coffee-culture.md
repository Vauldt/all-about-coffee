# Are we seeing a new "wave" of coffee culture, and what defines it?

← Part of [Common Questions About Coffee Culture 1](../coffee-culture/common-questions-about-coffee-culture-1.md)

The "waves of coffee" framework — first wave (mass commodity, instant, post-war), second wave (Starbucks, espresso mainstream, flavoured drinks), third wave (specialty, origin, craft) — has been the dominant periodisation framework since Trish Rothgeb coined the term in 2002. Whether a fourth wave is underway is contested within the industry, but several developments suggest a meaningful shift.

The fourth-wave candidate characteristics include: the integration of data and precision science into café operations (refractometers, dialling logs, pressure profiling, water recipe software); a reconfiguration of the barista role toward hospitality and communication as the primary differentiators from automation; a post-exclusivity ethos that prioritises accessibility and community engagement over connoisseurship gatekeeping; and sustainability as an operational imperative rather than a marketing claim.

There is also a geographic and demographic dimension. The specialty coffee conversation has been dominated by a small number of Western cities — Melbourne, London, Copenhagen, Portland, Tokyo. A new wave might be characterised by the maturation of specialty scenes in producing countries (Colombia, Ethiopia, Brazil) where local specialty consumption is growing and origin-based roasters are emerging; and by the diversification of the specialty workforce and customer base away from the demographic homogeneity that has characterised it.

Whether this constitutes a new wave or a maturing of the third wave is partly a semantic question. The substantive developments are real: coffee culture is changing in ways that the third-wave framework does not fully capture.
