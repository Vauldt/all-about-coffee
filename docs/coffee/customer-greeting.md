# Customer Greeting

Customer greeting is the opening moment of every service interaction — the first impression a barista makes when a guest approaches the counter. Executed well, it sets a welcoming tone, establishes trust, and signals to the customer that they are seen and valued.

## Why It Matters

The greeting is a customer's first point of contact with the café's hospitality. A warm, attentive greeting reduces perceived wait time, puts nervous or unfamiliar customers at ease, and establishes the barista as approachable. Research in service industries consistently shows that the first and last moments of an interaction have the strongest impact on how the overall experience is remembered.

## Core Principles

- **Acknowledge promptly** — make eye contact and acknowledge the customer within a few seconds of their arrival, even if you are mid-task
- **Use a natural opener** — a simple "Hi, how are you?" or "Good morning!" is more genuine than scripted phrases
- **Match your energy to theirs** — read the customer's body language; mirror a relaxed tone for regulars and a brighter, more attentive tone for new or hesitant guests
- **Use their name** — when taking a name for an order, use it again when handing the drink over; this creates a personal connection
- **Avoid hollow phrases** — phrases like "no worries" or "no problem" in response to a thank-you can inadvertently suggest the service *could* have been a problem

## Non-Verbal Elements

Body language is as important as words. Facing the customer squarely, making eye contact, and smiling genuinely all reinforce the verbal greeting. Avoid turning your back during the greeting or letting your attention drift to the espresso machine mid-sentence.

## At Level 1

A Foundation Barista is developing the habit of consistent acknowledgement. The goal at this stage is reliability — every customer gets a greeting, every time — rather than refinement. Natural warmth develops with practice; the discipline of consistency is what is built first.
