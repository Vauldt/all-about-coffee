# Dietary Trends and Coffee Culture

## Overview

Dietary trends — principally the adoption of plant-based milks and reduced-sugar preferences — have significantly reshaped café menus, barista training, and the specialty coffee industry's commercial positioning over the past decade. Plant milk adoption has introduced new technical challenges at the espresso bar while sugar-free and clean-label consumer preferences have largely aligned with specialty coffee's values-based proposition around natural flavour.

## Plant Milk Adoption

Plant milk adoption has been the single most significant menu shift in café culture over the past decade. Oat milk, in particular, went from a specialty-market curiosity to a mainstream café staple between approximately 2017 and 2022, driven by lactose intolerance prevalence, dairy-free dietary preferences, and environmental positioning. Oat milk's commercial success was substantially enabled by Oatly's barista formulation, which textures and steams in a way that earlier plant milks — almond and soy — did not, addressing a genuine technical barrier to specialty café adoption.

The plant milk shift has created both opportunities and technical challenges. Baristas trained on whole cow's milk must retrain milk-texturing technique for each plant milk, which behaves differently due to varying protein and fat content. Customer expectations of latte art — visible in dairy drinks — are not consistently met with plant milks, which creates service complexity. Pricing is also complicated: plant milks cost significantly more wholesale than dairy, but charging surcharges for non-dairy choices has been criticised as a penalty on dietary preference or ethical choice.

## Sugar-Free and Clean-Label Trends

Sugar-free and reduced-calorie trends have had a more moderate impact on specialty coffee culture, largely because specialty's quality proposition is built around natural flavour rather than added sweetness. The shift toward clean-label beverages — coffee drinks with a short, recognisable ingredient list — aligns naturally with specialty's values.

The challenge lies at the commercial middle: chain consumers driving sugar-free demand have been habituated to sweetened coffee and often find the transition to unsweetened specialty coffee difficult. Specialty cafés have responded with naturally sweetened alternatives such as house-made syrups, honey, and date-sweetened preparations that preserve clean-label positioning while accommodating customer preferences.

## Key Facts

- Oat milk became a mainstream café staple in most markets between approximately 2017 and 2022
- Plant milks behave differently from dairy under steaming due to varying protein and fat content, requiring adapted texturing technique
- Sugar-free trends align with specialty coffee's natural-flavour proposition more readily than with commercial chain coffee culture
- Clean-label positioning — beverages with minimal, recognisable ingredients — is a shared value between dietary trends and specialty coffee
