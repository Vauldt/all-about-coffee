# Cafés and Local Economies

## Overview

Cafés play a significant role in local economies beyond their direct revenue — as anchor tenants for pedestrian retail strips, employers of entry-level workers, and contributors to local spend multipliers. Their economic significance has grown as online retail has displaced category retail, making experiential and social venues disproportionately important to main-street commercial viability.

## Anchor Tenant Effect

A café that generates regular foot traffic multiple times per day creates passing exposure for neighbouring retailers — bookshops, florists, and fashion boutiques — that operate on lower visit frequency. This multiplier effect is well-documented in retail geography; the presence of a quality café is often a leading indicator of strip retail viability. Cafés are among the few business types that draw customers two or three times daily, making them effective traffic generators for surrounding retail.

## Employment and Local Spending

The café sector is a significant employer of young workers, migrants, and people in transitional employment. It is one of the few industries where entry-level employment does not require formal credentials but provides transferable skills in hospitality, customer service, and food handling.

Independent cafés also tend to exhibit high local spend multipliers compared with chain operators. They typically source from local roasters, bakeries, and dairy suppliers, circulating money within the local economy rather than extracting it to a head office or remote supply chain. This makes the independent café sector a net contributor to local economic circulation in a way that national chains are not.

## Main-Street Activation

The café's contribution to commercial strip vitality has become particularly significant in the context of online retail disruption. As category retail — books, music, electronics — has migrated online, experiential and social venues have become disproportionately important to main-street activation. A strip that can sustain a cluster of quality cafés retains foot traffic and therefore broader retail viability. This shift has elevated the café from a convenience business to a strategic component of urban commercial planning.

## Key Facts

- Cafés function as anchor tenants: their high daily visit frequency generates foot traffic that benefits lower-frequency neighbouring retailers
- Independent cafés tend to have higher local spend multipliers than chains, sourcing from local roasters, bakeries, and dairy suppliers
- The café sector is a major employer of young workers and migrants in entry-level roles with transferable skills
- As online retail has displaced category retail, cafés and other experiential venues have become strategically important to main-street commercial viability
