# Why do some people work better in a busy café than in a quiet office?

← Part of [Common Questions About Coffee Culture 1](common-questions-about-coffee-culture-1.md)

Research on ambient noise and cognitive performance shows that moderate levels of ambient noise — around 65–70 decibels, consistent with a busy café — enhance creative cognitive performance for most people relative to complete silence. The effect is attributed to mild distraction: low-level background noise prevents the brain from attending too closely to its own internal monologue, which can inhibit associative thinking. This is distinct from high-noise environments (above ~85 dB), which impair focus tasks.

A 2012 study published in the *Journal of Consumer Research* (Mehta, Zhu, and Cheema) specifically tested this, finding that moderate ambient noise enhanced abstract processing and creative output. The café-as-productivity venue is therefore not entirely a self-deception or productivity theatre — there is a cognitive basis for the preference.

Beyond noise level, the café offers a socially enforced attention structure. Working in public creates mild accountability — the visibility of being seen to work, even by strangers, appears to activate task engagement in some people. It also provides a defined temporal frame: the length of a coffee, or until the battery dies, creates natural work blocks. The absence of home distractions (chores, other household members) and the mild social stimulation of the environment both reduce procrastination triggers that quiet home offices can exacerbate.
