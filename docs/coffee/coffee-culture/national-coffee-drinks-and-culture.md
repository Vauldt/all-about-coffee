# What are some iconic national coffee drinks, and what do they say about local culture?

← Part of [Common Questions About Coffee Culture 1](common-questions-about-coffee-culture-1.md)

Iconic national drinks encode cultural values in their construction. Italy's espresso — short, concentrated, consumed rapidly — reflects a culture that treats coffee as punctuation rather than experience: a precise, efficient pleasure slotted between other activities. The ritual is fixed; the drink is not customised.

Australia's flat white (a double ristretto with textured whole milk in a smaller format than a latte) emerged from a culture that prizes technical quality and coffee seriousness over volume and sweetness. The flat white is not a scaled-down latte; it is a different drink with a different ratio philosophy. Its global spread through specialty café culture reflects Australian influence on third-wave coffee internationally.

Turkish coffee (*Türk kahvesi*) is brewed unfiltered, served with the grounds, and consumed slowly — the grounds are subsequently read for fortune-telling. The drink is inseparable from a broader culture of hospitality and conversation. Refusing it is a social act.

Vietnam's *cà phê trứng* (egg coffee) — robusta espresso blended with whipped egg yolk and condensed milk — arose from wartime milk scarcity and has become a national signature. Ethiopia's *buna* connects to origin mythology and social ceremony in ways no other country's drink does. Each of these examples shows that the drink is always secondary to the context it was built for.
