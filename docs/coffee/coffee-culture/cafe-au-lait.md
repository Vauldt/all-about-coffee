# Café au Lait

## Overview

A café au lait (French: "coffee with milk") is a brewed coffee drink prepared with hot brewed coffee and hot milk, typically in approximately equal proportions. Unlike a caffè latte, which is built on espresso, a café au lait uses filter or press-brewed coffee as its base, resulting in a less concentrated but broader-flavoured drink with a thinner, less creamy texture. It is strongly associated with French café culture and traditional morning coffee service.

## Key Characteristics

- **Base:** Brewed coffee (French press, drip, or pour-over), not espresso
- **Milk:** Hot milk, not steamed or textured microfoam
- **Ratio:** Typically 1:1 coffee to milk, though this varies by context and tradition
- **Texture:** Thinner and less creamy than a caffè latte; the milk is not emulsified or foamed
- **Service:** Traditionally served in a wide bowl or large cup in French café culture; this form is called a *bol*

## Comparison with Similar Drinks

A café au lait is frequently confused with a caffè latte. The distinction is fundamental: a caffè latte uses espresso as the coffee base and steamed microfoam milk; a café au lait uses brewed coffee and hot (unsteamed) milk. The flavour profile is consequently different — the café au lait is less intense, less creamy, and has a broader, more dilute character.

In the United States, café au lait in New Orleans style is made with strong drip coffee blended with chicory, combined with scalded whole milk in equal parts — a regional variant with a distinct bitter, roasty, and mildly herbal character.

## Key Facts

- Café au lait uses brewed coffee (not espresso) and hot milk (not steamed microfoam) in approximately equal proportions
- Associated with French café culture; traditionally served in a wide bowl at breakfast
- Fundamentally different from a caffè latte despite superficial similarity — the base, milk treatment, and texture are all distinct
- New Orleans café au lait is a well-known regional variant using chicory-blended coffee and scalded milk
