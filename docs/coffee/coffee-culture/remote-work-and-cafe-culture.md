# How has remote work changed how people use cafés for socialising and working?

← Part of [Common Questions About Coffee Culture 1](common-questions-about-coffee-culture-1.md)

Remote and hybrid work patterns, accelerated significantly after 2020, redistributed foot traffic away from city centres and toward residential neighbourhoods. Local cafés in suburban and inner-suburban areas experienced demand increases that destination cafés in CBD office districts did not. The café replaced the office as the neutral, semi-public workspace for a significant portion of the knowledge workforce.

This shift has created observable tensions. Cafés not designed for laptop work — small tables, poor wifi, no power points — face pressure to accommodate a new use case that their layout and revenue model were not built for. Conversely, cafés that embrace remote workers face the economic problem of slow table turns: a person nursing a single flat white for three hours generates significantly less revenue per square metre than a table turning four times in the same period.

Socially, the remote worker café culture has shifted some of the social dynamics of the space. Solitary laptop use displaces the conversational, communal use that defined third-place theory. Some operators have responded by restricting laptop use during peak hours — a revealing negotiation between the café's commercial needs and its social function. The café as workspace is a structurally new use that existing models are still adapting to.
