# Espresso vs Filter

## Overview

Espresso and filter coffee extract soluble compounds from ground coffee through fundamentally different mechanisms: espresso uses high pressure (9 bar) and a very short contact time to produce a concentrated beverage; filter coffee uses gravity or immersion at near-atmospheric pressure over a longer contact time to produce a more dilute, clarity-focused cup. The result is two distinct flavour profiles from the same bean. A detailed treatment of the flavour differences is covered in [Espresso-specific vs filter-specific flavour mapping](../coffee-brewing/espresso-specific-vs-filter-specific-flavour-mapping.md).

## Key Differences

| Parameter | Espresso | Filter (pour-over / drip) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Brew ratio | 1:2 (approx. 18 g / 36 g) | 1:15–1:17 |
| TDS | 8–12% | 1.15–1.45% |
| Pressure | 9 bar | ~0 bar (gravity) |
| Contact time | 25–35 seconds | 3–5 minutes |
| Grind size | Very fine | Medium to medium-coarse |
| Filtration | No paper; oils pass through | Paper removes oils and fines |
| Body | Full, thick, coating | Clean, light |

## Flavour Profiles

**Espresso** amplifies body, sweetness, and caramel/chocolate notes through concentration and oil-inclusive extraction. Fine-grained acidity is present but typically muted relative to filter. All flavour compounds — positive and negative — are magnified by the 8–12% TDS concentration.

**Filter coffee** preserves acidity, floral aromatics, and fruit clarity. Paper filtration removes emulsified oils and micro-fines, producing a clean, transparent cup that allows individual origin compounds to be perceived distinctly. Light roast, high-acidity coffees (Ethiopian, Kenyan) are most expressive in filter.

## Roast-Level Interaction

Roast level significantly shapes how method and flavour interact. Light roasts are generally most expressive as filter coffee, where their acidity, floral character, and fruit notes are transparent; at espresso concentration, these same qualities can read as sharp or overwhelming. Dark roasts are more suited to espresso, where concentration and the absence of paper filtration support their body and bittersweet character.

## Key Facts

- Espresso: 9 bar, 1:2 ratio, 25–35 seconds, 8–12% TDS; amplifies body, sweetness, caramel, and intensity
- Filter: ~0 bar, 1:15–1:17 ratio, 3–5 minutes, 1.15–1.45% TDS; amplifies clarity, acidity, and floral/fruit notes
- Paper filtration (filter) removes emulsified oils — a key difference in mouthfeel and flavour transparency
- Light-roast, high-acidity coffees are most expressive in filter; medium-dark, low-acidity coffees suit espresso
