## Overview

Coffee origin and processing are two of the three fundamental pillars of coffee identity alongside variety. Origin — encompassing country, region, farm, altitude, and variety — establishes the flavour potential of the raw material, while processing — the method used to remove the coffee cherry's fruit and prepare the green bean — determines how much of that potential is preserved, altered, or overlaid with additional character. The interaction between origin terroir and processing method is one of the primary sources of flavour variation across specialty coffees.

## The Interaction

Origin and processing do not operate independently. The same variety grown at the same farm will produce substantially different cup profiles depending on whether it is washed, natural, or honey-processed:

| Processing method | Effect on origin expression |
| :--- | :--- |
| Washed | Removes fruit influence; terroir, altitude, and variety express most clearly |
| Natural | Adds dried-fruit, berry, and wine-like fermentation notes; can amplify or mask origin character |
| Honey/pulped natural | Intermediate; adds sweetness and some fruit influence while retaining more terroir clarity than natural |
| Experimental (anaerobic, etc.) | Can substantially transform cup character; may override origin terroir with process-derived flavours |

## Key Facts

- Origin establishes flavour potential; processing transforms and shapes what is ultimately expressed in the cup
- Washed processing allows the clearest expression of origin terroir; natural processing adds substantial process-derived character
- Processing often explains large flavour differences between coffees from the same region — sometimes more than altitude or variety
- Experimental processing methods can introduce flavour profiles that override traditional regional characteristics entirely
