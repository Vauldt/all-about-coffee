## Overview

The extraction process is the sequence of events by which brewing water dissolves soluble compounds from ground coffee into solution. Extraction proceeds through three broadly sequential stages — early, middle, and late — with different compound classes dominating each stage. The target for most brewing methods is to capture the early and middle stages while minimising the late stage, producing a cup with balanced acidity, sweetness, and mild bitterness.

## Extraction Stages

| Stage | Compounds extracted | Flavour contribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Early | Bright acids, volatile aromatics, light sugars | Brightness, fruit, citrus |
| Middle | Complex sugars, caramelisation products, caffeine | Sweetness, body, balance |
| Late | Bitter melanoidins, quinic acid, tannins | Bitterness, astringency, dryness |

## Target Extraction Yield

The SCA target extraction yield for most brewing methods is 18–22% of the coffee's dry mass dissolved into the brew. This range approximates the sweet spot where the middle stage dominates and the late stage is minimal:

- **Below 18%:** Under-extracted — sour, sharp, thin, hollow
- **18–22%:** Target zone — balanced, sweet, complex
- **Above 22%:** Over-extracted — bitter, dry, astringent, harsh

## Key Facts

- Coffee extraction proceeds in stages: acids and aromatics first, sweetness compounds mid-brew, bitter compounds last
- SCA target: 18–22% extraction yield; under-extracted tastes sour; over-extracted tastes bitter
